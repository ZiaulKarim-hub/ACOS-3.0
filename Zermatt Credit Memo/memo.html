01 — Recommendation

Advance to formal underwriting and issue an indicative proposal — subject to conditions precedent.

The request is well-collateralized and sits behind meaningful first-loss equity, and the sponsor’s stabilized case implies a low loan-to-value and a high debt yield. The case for the loan rests almost entirely on execution: a large renovation, a brand conversion, and a near-doubling of occupancy off a depressed base. The projections are sponsor-supplied and not yet independently verified. OKOA should progress the file to underwriting and issue an indicative term sheet conditioned on independent appraisal and feasibility, a completed Property Condition Assessment, reconciled financials, a verified stabilized proforma, an executed franchise agreement, and a completion / interest-reserve structure. Final approval, pricing, and structure are reserved to the Credit Committee.

03
Property
& Market

A destination-market resort, under-managed and under-invested

The Zermatt is a full-service resort and amenity property in Midway, in Utah’s Heber Valley — minutes from Deer Valley and Park City, in a seasonal mountain-tourism market. The collateral base is 140 owned, pledgeable hotel units (“keys”), against 51,100 available room-nights a year. The wider condominium-hotel comprises roughly 226 guestrooms: 158 individually owned condo units in the main building plus 68 in the annex. The franchisee owns 130 of the 158 main-building units and must retain at least 130 (82%) under the brand agreement.

The thesis is straightforward: a resort with a real demand base that has seen no capital reinvestment, run below its potential. A renovation plus a national brand should lift rate, occupancy, and food-and-beverage capture. The counter-point is that recent operating levels are weak, and the stabilized targets require a substantial recovery — see Sections 06 and 07.

Market note

Location and amenity depth are genuine strengths. But this is a single-asset, seasonal hospitality bet in a condo-hotel structure — the operating and legal complexity is above that of a typical stabilized commercial asset.

Structural watch-point

The plan is a long, no-cash-flow window: roughly 14 months from close to reopening, and ~20 months to stabilization, during which the property is closed or ramping and interest / fees accrue to the loan balance. The exit hinges on both stabilization and a receptive 2028 refinance market, with only ~4 months of runway between the stabilize date and the current bridge maturity.

Two flags to resolve before close

1. Negative equity. The operating entity is balance-sheet insolvent — equity of −$2,120,415.29 at year-end 2025 (liabilities exceed assets). This is a core reason a recapitalization is needed, and it raises the stakes on clean-up at close.

2. A net-income mismatch. The sponsor summary reports 2024 net income of −$71,059, while the balance sheet reports 2024 net income of $290,847.04. The 2025 figure agrees ($118,991). The difference must be reconciled before underwriting relies on either number.

Open / unpriced scope

About $4.82MM of the $12.0MM allowance is not yet priced, and a Property Condition Assessment (PCA — a professional building inspection) is ordered but pending. Known open items include a heavily leaking water feature behind a wall, lobby-door condition, pool chemical / pump-room scope, an ~8,000 SF exterior stamped-concrete match risk, and mechanical-room leaks. A property-issue report already lists 34 identified items. Renovation cost and schedule are a live risk until the PCA lands and the budget is fully bought out.

Net credit posture

Well-collateralized (140 keys plus land), with $14,519,679 (43.7%) of first-loss equity ahead of the loan and a low implied stabilized LTV. The risk is concentrated in execution and verification, not in stabilized coverage — which is exactly what conditions precedent are for.

The shape of the loan

$14.5MM of first-loss equity sits ahead of a $15.0MM senior bridge — a 45.1% loan-to-cost into a hard asset.

Recommendation
& Conditions

Recommendation & conditions precedent

Recommendation: advance the file to formal underwriting and issue an indicative term sheet, conditioned on the items below. The collateral, equity cushion, and stabilized metrics support the loan; the open questions are all verifiable in diligence. Final approval, pricing, and structure are reserved to the OKOA Credit Committee.

Conditions precedent

  • Independent appraisal and a hospitality feasibility / market study.
  • Completed Property Condition Assessment (PCA) and a fully bought-out, contingency-backed renovation budget.
  • Reconciled historical financials — resolve the 2024 net-income discrepancy; confirm the “Kraw” rental-split scope on transfer.
  • Independently reviewed stabilized proforma (occupancy, ADR, F&B ramp, opex ratio).
  • Executed Marriott franchise agreement and PIP scope; confirmation of unit control (≥130 keys).
  • Interest-reserve / accrual sizing tested against the fully-accreted exit balance and exit LTV.
  • Intercreditor and structure terms for the $6.5MM retained senior and the C-PACE facility.
  • Clean-up of current liabilities and confirmation of clear collateral (Plats A, B, F) at closing.
What would change the call

A weak PCA, an unbuyable renovation budget, or an independent proforma that does not support the occupancy / ADR recovery would move this from “advance, conditional” toward “decline or re-size.” The stabilized cushion is comfortable only if the stabilized case holds.
