# BrandSync — Logo Brief

Interviewed 2026-07-27. Source: chat interview, ACOS Logo Forge step 1.

## Name (verbatim, for the wordmark)

**BrandSync** — capital B, capital S, one word, no space, no hyphen.

## What it does

BrandSync sells small businesses an affordable logo + website bundle, then keeps
the site alive for a monthly fee. It also sells SEO, brand assets, and branding
training.

Service list and pricing as stated by the user:

| Service | Price |
|---|---|
| Website + logo (one-off bundle) | $500 |
| Website maintenance / uptime | $30 per month |
| SEO | $2000 per month |
| Brand asset creation | not priced yet |
| Branding training | not priced yet |

## Audience

Small businesses. Owner-operators and solo shops buying their first real
identity — price-sensitive, not design-literate, need to be reassured rather
than impressed.

## Personality (the three steering words)

1. **Approachable & warm** — friendly, human, unintimidating.
2. **Trustworthy & steady** — reliable, established, calm; supports recurring revenue.
3. **Modern & sharp** — clean, current, technically competent.

Explicitly NOT chosen: *bold & energetic*. Keep candidates confident but never
loud. No shouting weights, no high-contrast colour clashes.

## The positioning tension (drives the whole round)

The price list pulls in two directions at once:

- **$500** bundle → must read affordable and approachable; a luxury-looking mark
  makes $500 feel suspiciously cheap.
- **$2000/month** SEO retainer → must read credible and serious; a budget-looking
  mark makes $2000 hard to ask for.

Target: **credible but warm.** Professional enough to justify the retainer,
friendly enough that a solo shop owner is not scared off. Rule of thumb from
service-brand positioning, not a law — user may override.

## Colour

- **Direction chosen: blue / teal family.** User picked this over the
  recommended warm-neutral + accent option.
- **Known tradeoff, accepted:** blue is the default trust colour for agencies and
  software, so it is the hardest palette to look distinctive in. Mitigation is
  the designer's problem, not the user's: push toward teal-leaning blues,
  unusual blue values, and blue paired with a warm neutral, rather than generic
  corporate navy. Spread the ten candidates across that range.
- **Off-limits: none stated.**

## Form

**Symbol + wordmark — separable system.** User's words: "Symbol and Wordmark both
but separately." Confirmed reading (user replied "surprise me", standard reading
taken):

1. a **standalone symbol** that works alone — tab icon, social avatar, stamp;
2. a **standalone wordmark** that works alone — site header, letterhead, invoices;
3. a **combined lockup** of the two, as the primary logo.

Hard requirement: **each piece must hold up on its own**, not only when paired.
Consequence for file structure — symbol lives in its own `<g id="mark">`, wordmark
in its own `<g id="wordmark">`, so the editor can show/hide/move/export each
independently.

## Symbols

- **Nothing required, nothing banned by the user.**
- **Designer note:** "Sync" pulls hard toward the two-circular-arrows refresh
  icon. It is the first idea everyone has and is therefore everywhere. Explore it
  in 1–2 candidates so the user can judge it honestly; spend the rest of the
  round elsewhere.
- Other territory worth mining: two forms merging/aligning, a link or knot, a
  tuning-fork / in-phase wave, stacked layers snapping into register, the B and S
  interlocking, a spark or seed for the "small business grows" story.

## References

**None.** User: "I don't know what I am looking for, surprise me." Treat this as
licence for genuine diversity in round 1 — the round's job is to show the user
what they react to, not to converge early.

## Small-size floor

The standalone symbol must survive the preview page's size waterfall down to
24–32px (house minimum-size floor). Over-detailed marks fail here — that is a
design signal, not a rendering bug.
