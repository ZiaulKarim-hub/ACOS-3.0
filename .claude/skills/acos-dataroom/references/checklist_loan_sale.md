# Base Checklist — Loan Sale

> **Scope:** Outbound diligence for selling a whole loan to a third-party investor.
> **Role:** Prior — applied via Phase 7a tailoring to produce per-deal scope.
> **Default action:** Every item is `in_scope` until tailoring marks it otherwise.
> **Orientation:** OKOA is the SELLER. All items describe what OKOA must produce
> for the buyer. The enforceability and perfection lens applies to an EXISTING loan,
> not a new origination.

---

## Category 01 — Loan Documents & Enforceability Chain

### 01.01 Original Promissory Note
- **What:** The executed, wet-ink or e-signed original promissory note evidencing the debt obligation.
- **Why it matters:** The note is the debt instrument. Without the original (or a certified lost-note affidavit), the buyer cannot enforce repayment in court.
- **Typical form:** Original signed document; PDF scan with custodian certification if original is held by a custodian.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / closing binder / note custodian.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.02 Allonge(s) to the Note
- **What:** Every allonge endorsing the note from the originating lender through each subsequent holder to OKOA, and a blank or buyer-specific endorsement transferring to the new buyer.
- **Why it matters:** The endorsement chain proves OKOA's standing to sell and enables the buyer to become a holder in due course.
- **Typical form:** Original allonge(s) attached to or accompanying the note; blank-endorsed allonge executed by OKOA at closing.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file; if originated and sold before OKOA, prior servicer records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.03 Loan Agreement / Credit Agreement
- **What:** The master loan agreement or credit agreement governing terms, covenants, events of default, and remedies.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer needs to understand all contractual obligations and rights they are assuming.
- **Typical form:** Executed PDF; may be a standalone agreement or incorporated into the note.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.04 Construction Loan Agreement (if applicable)
- **What:** If the loan funded construction or renovation, the specific construction loan agreement with draw schedule, budget, and completion requirements.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer inherits any unfunded draw obligations and must understand completion risk.
- **Typical form:** Executed construction loan agreement with exhibits (budget, draw schedule, completion guaranty).
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder / construction administration file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.05 All Amendments & Modifications to the Note and Loan Agreement
- **What:** Every amendment, modification agreement, side letter, or consent that altered the original loan terms.
- **Why it matters:** Unidentified amendments can undermine enforceability or reveal undisclosed borrower concessions.
- **Typical form:** Executed amendment agreements in chronological order.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file; servicer records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.06 Assignment of Mortgage / Deed of Trust (Full Chain)
- **What:** Recorded assignments of the deed of trust or mortgage from originator through every intermediate assignee to OKOA, plus the assignment to buyer executed at closing.
- **Why it matters:** Without a clean recorded chain, the buyer's lien is unperfected or contested. Recording gaps are a title insurer's nightmare.
- **Typical form:** Recorded assignment instruments (county recorder certified copies); unrecorded assignment in escrow for closing.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder's office; OKOA loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.07 Deed of Trust / Mortgage (Executed and Recorded)
- **What:** The executed and county-recorded deed of trust (or mortgage, depending on jurisdiction) securing the loan against the real property collateral.
- **Why it matters:** This is the lien instrument. Buyer needs the recorded version to verify lien position and legal description.
- **Typical form:** County recorder certified copy with recording stamp and instrument number.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder's office; title company; closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.08 Assignments of Leases and Rents
- **What:** Recorded assignment of leases and rents (ALR) instrument granting the lender rights to collect rents upon default.
- **Why it matters:** Without a perfected ALR, the buyer loses a primary enforcement tool in a cash-flowing property default scenario.
- **Typical form:** Recorded ALR; county recorder certified copy.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.09 Environmental Indemnity Agreement
- **What:** Executed agreement by which borrower (and often guarantors) indemnify the lender for environmental liabilities.
- **Why it matters:** Environmental exposure can exceed collateral value. Buyer inherits the indemnity or is left without it.
- **Typical form:** Executed environmental indemnity agreement (standalone or embedded in loan docs).
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.10 Hazardous Substances Certificate / Borrower Environmental Representation
- **What:** Borrower's representations regarding known hazardous materials at or near the property.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes the baseline on which the environmental indemnity is predicated.
- **Typical form:** Executed certificate or representations within the loan agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.11 Completion Guaranty (construction loans)
- **What:** Executed guaranty by the sponsor/developer to complete construction and deliver the project per approved plans.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer inherits completion risk on any unfunded draw. The guaranty is the backstop.
- **Typical form:** Executed completion guaranty, often running in favor of lender and its successors.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.12 Carve-Out Guaranty (Bad-Boy Guaranty)
- **What:** Personal guaranty by sponsor(s) covering non-recourse carve-outs: fraud, misappropriation of rents, voluntary bankruptcy, environmental events, waste, and similar acts.
- **Why it matters:** This is the buyer's primary recourse against the sponsor beyond the collateral. Its enforceability and the guarantor's net worth are critical.
- **Typical form:** Executed guaranty agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.13 Payment Guaranty (if full recourse or partial recourse)
- **What:** Personal or corporate guaranty covering full or partial repayment of the loan, not limited to carve-outs.
- **Why it matters:** Changes the risk profile materially — buyer needs to know whether recourse exists and who stands behind it.
- **Typical form:** Executed payment guaranty agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.14 Pledge Agreement / Pledge of Membership Interests
- **What:** Agreement pledging the borrower entity's equity interests (e.g., LLC membership interests) as additional collateral.
- **Why it matters:** Provides the lender (and buyer) a path to take ownership through a UCC foreclosure, which is faster than judicial foreclosure in many states.
- **Typical form:** Executed pledge agreement; stock/membership certificate with transfer power if certificated.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.15 Intercreditor Agreement / Subordination Agreement (if applicable)
- **What:** Agreement governing priority and rights between senior and subordinate lenders (mezzanine, preferred equity, subordinate mortgage).
- **Why it matters:** Buyer of the senior loan must know what rights the mez lender has to cure, purchase the senior loan, or interfere with foreclosure.
- **Typical form:** Executed intercreditor or subordination agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; mez lender's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.16 Subordination, Non-Disturbance, and Attornment Agreements (SNDAs)
- **What:** SNDAs executed between the lender and any major tenants, subordinating tenant leases to the mortgage while protecting tenants from disturbance.
- **Why it matters:** Without SNDAs, major tenants may walk upon foreclosure. Buyer needs to know lease continuity is protected.
- **Typical form:** Executed SNDAs for anchor or material tenants.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; property management records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.17 Estoppel Certificates from Major Tenants
- **What:** Tenant-executed certificates confirming lease terms, rent amounts, the absence of landlord defaults, and any side agreements.
- **Why it matters:** Prevents tenants from later claiming concessions or defaults not reflected in the lease file.
- **Typical form:** Executed estoppel certificates (dated within 90 days of sale closing, if possible).
- **Where to find it:** Origination file; property management.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.18 Lockbox / Cash Management Agreement
- **What:** Agreement directing property cash flows through a lender-controlled lockbox or cash management account.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer needs to know whether cash controls are in place and whether they are enforceable; controls affect operational continuity post-transfer.
- **Typical form:** Executed lockbox and cash management agreement; depository account control agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; servicer records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.19 Depository Account Control Agreement (DACA)
- **What:** Agreement establishing the lender's control over the borrower's deposit accounts, perfecting the lender's security interest.
- **Why it matters:** Without a DACA, control over operating accounts is unperfected under UCC Article 9.
- **Typical form:** Tri-party executed DACA (lender, borrower, depository bank).
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; servicer.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.20 Interest Rate Cap Agreement / Swap Agreement (if floating rate)
- **What:** Documentation of any interest rate cap or swap purchased by the borrower as required by the loan docs, including the cap agreement, confirmation, and assignment of cap to lender.
- **Why it matters:** Rate caps are collateral; buyer must receive an assignment. If the cap has lapsed, buyer faces unhedged rate exposure.
- **Typical form:** Executed ISDA master agreement; cap confirmation; lender assignment of cap rights.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; swap counterparty.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 02 — Collateral Perfection (Real Property)

### 02.01 Recorded Deed of Trust / Mortgage — Certified Copy
- **What:** County recorder certified copy of the deed of trust or mortgage with recording stamp, instrument number, and recording date.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the lien is of public record and establishes the priority date.
- **Typical form:** Certified copy from county recorder or title company.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.02 Lien Priority Confirmation
- **What:** Evidence that OKOA's lien is in the stated priority position (1st, 2nd, etc.) — typically confirmed by the title commitment or ALTA policy.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer's underwriting is based on the assumed lien position. A hidden senior encumbrance changes the entire risk profile.
- **Typical form:** Title commitment Schedule B or endorsement; lien search results.
- **Where to find it:** Title company; origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.03 Recorded Assignment Chain — All Intermediate Assignments
- **What:** Recorded copies of every assignment of the deed of trust or mortgage from originator to OKOA, with no gaps.
- **Why it matters:** A break in the chain leaves OKOA without standing to enforce and clouds the buyer's lien.
- **Typical form:** Recorder-certified copies with instrument numbers and recording dates for each assignment.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; title company; prior servicer records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.04 Legal Description of Collateral Property
- **What:** The full, legally sufficient description of the real property collateral (metes and bounds, lot and block, or government survey) as it appears in the recorded deed of trust.
- **Why it matters:** A defective or incomplete legal description renders the deed of trust unenforceable against the parcel.
- **Typical form:** Exhibit A to the deed of trust; confirmed by survey.
- **Where to find it:** Recorded deed of trust; survey.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.05 Parcel Identification Numbers (APN / Tax ID)
- **What:** County assessor parcel numbers for every parcel included in the collateral.
- **Why it matters:** Needed to pull tax records, verify tax payment status, and confirm collateral completeness.
- **Typical form:** List of APNs in loan file; confirmed in title commitment.
- **Where to find it:** County assessor; title commitment.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.06 Property Address and Collateral Description
- **What:** Full street address(es), property type, and a brief description of improvements.
- **Why it matters:** Basic identification for diligence coordination and underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Summary sheet in loan file.
- **Where to find it:** Loan summary; origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.07 Fixture Filing (UCC-1 Financing Statement — Real Property Fixtures)
- **What:** UCC-1 financing statement filed as a fixture filing in the real property records, covering fixtures attached to the property.
- **Why it matters:** Perfects the lender's security interest in fixtures; prevents a subsequent buyer or lienholder from arguing fixtures are not encumbered.
- **Typical form:** Filed UCC-1 with real property records notation; copy from county recorder.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder (fixture filing jurisdiction); origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 02.08 Subordination of HOA or REA Liens (if applicable)
- **What:** Any agreements subordinating homeowners association liens or reciprocal easement agreements to the deed of trust.
- **Why it matters:** In some states HOA liens are super-priority and can wipe out a lender's first position.
- **Typical form:** Executed subordination agreement recorded against the property.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 03 — Collateral Perfection (Personal Property / UCC)

### 03.01 UCC-1 Financing Statement — Original Filing
- **What:** The UCC-1 financing statement filed with the appropriate Secretary of State (typically the state of borrower organization) perfecting the lender's security interest in personal property collateral.
- **Why it matters:** Without a filed and effective UCC-1, the security interest in personal property is unperfected and subordinate to a trustee in bankruptcy.
- **Typical form:** Filed UCC-1 copy with file number and filing date; Secretary of State search confirmation.
- **Where to find it:** State Secretary of State filing records; origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.02 UCC Search Results (Current)
- **What:** Current UCC lien searches in the borrower's state of organization and any other relevant jurisdictions, confirming OKOA's filing and identifying any competing liens.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer needs to know if any third party has a superior or competing security interest in the personal property collateral.
- **Typical form:** Secretary of State search certificate (dated within 30 days of sale, ideally).
- **Where to find it:** Commercial search firm; Secretary of State.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.03 UCC-3 Continuation Statements (if UCC-1 is over 4 years old)
- **What:** UCC-3 continuation filings that keep the UCC-1 effective beyond its initial 5-year term.
- **Why it matters:** An un-continued UCC-1 lapses at 5 years and the security interest becomes unperfected — a fatal defect if the loan has been outstanding for more than 5 years.
- **Typical form:** Filed UCC-3 continuation copies.
- **Where to find it:** Secretary of State records; origination/servicing file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.04 UCC-3 Amendment Statements (name changes, collateral changes)
- **What:** Any UCC-3 amendments reflecting changes in borrower name, collateral description, or lender name since the original UCC-1 was filed.
- **Why it matters:** If a borrower changed its name and no amendment was filed, the financing statement may be seriously misleading and therefore ineffective.
- **Typical form:** Filed UCC-3 amendment copies.
- **Where to find it:** Secretary of State; origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.05 Collateral Description Schedule (Personal Property)
- **What:** Complete description of personal property collateral covered by the UCC-1 — may include furniture, fixtures, equipment, accounts, general intangibles, and equity interests.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer needs to know exactly what personal property is pledged and whether the description in the UCC-1 is sufficient.
- **Typical form:** Exhibit to the security agreement; UCC-1 collateral description.
- **Where to find it:** Security agreement; UCC-1 filing.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.06 Security Agreement (Personal Property)
- **What:** Executed security agreement creating the security interest in personal property collateral that the UCC-1 perfects.
- **Why it matters:** Without the underlying security agreement, there is no security interest to perfect.
- **Typical form:** Executed security agreement, often incorporated in the loan agreement or deed of trust.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.07 Pledge of Membership Interests — Stock / Certificate Transfer
- **What:** If membership interests were certificated and pledged, the actual certificates with transfer powers (or confirmation that interests are uncertificated and controlled via control agreement).
- **Why it matters:** Physical delivery or control is required for perfection of pledged equity interests.
- **Typical form:** Original membership certificates; executed transfer powers (or control agreement for uncertificated interests).
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; borrower's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 04 — Title & Survey

### 04.01 ALTA Lender's Title Insurance Policy (Original)
- **What:** The original ALTA lender's title insurance policy issued at closing in favor of the original lender, insuring the priority, enforceability, and validity of the deed of trust.
- **Why it matters:** This policy is assignable to the buyer (with endorsement). Without it, the buyer has no insurance coverage for title defects.
- **Typical form:** Original policy with all endorsements; Schedule A (insured amount, legal description) and Schedule B (exceptions).
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 04.02 Title Policy Assignment Endorsement (or New Date-Down)
- **What:** Endorsement from the title insurer assigning the existing policy to the new loan buyer, or a new date-down endorsement extending coverage to the date of sale.
- **Why it matters:** The original policy alone does not protect the buyer against matters arising between origination and loan sale.
- **Typical form:** Endorsement executed by title insurer; date-down endorsement.
- **Where to find it:** Title company (ordered at time of sale).
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 04.03 Current Title Commitment / Title Bring-Down Search
- **What:** A current title search (24–36 months) or title commitment showing the status of title as of the loan sale date, including any new liens, judgments, or encumbrances recorded after origination.
- **Why it matters:** Material encumbrances recorded post-origination — tax liens, mechanic's liens, judgment liens — can subordinate or impair the buyer's security.
- **Typical form:** Title commitment with Schedule A and B; preliminary title report.
- **Where to find it:** Title company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.04 Survey (ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey)
- **What:** A current or as-of-closing ALTA/NSPS land title survey of the collateral property.
- **Why it matters:** Survey confirms boundaries, easements, encroachments, access, and that the legal description matches the physical property.
- **Typical form:** Signed and sealed survey plat and certificate meeting ALTA/NSPS standards.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; borrower's surveyor.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.05 Survey Affidavit / No-Change Affidavit (if survey is dated)
- **What:** Borrower affidavit certifying that no changes to the property have occurred since the survey that would affect the survey's accuracy.
- **Why it matters:** Allows the title insurer to extend survey coverage without a new survey if the affidavit is acceptable.
- **Typical form:** Executed affidavit meeting title company requirements.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 04.06 Recorded Easements, CC&Rs, and REAs
- **What:** Copies of all recorded easements, covenants, conditions and restrictions (CC&Rs), and reciprocal easement agreements affecting the property.
- **Why it matters:** These can restrict use, impose maintenance obligations, or grant third-party rights that affect collateral value.
- **Typical form:** Recorded instruments (county recorder certified copies).
- **Where to find it:** Title commitment Schedule B; county recorder.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 04.07 Mechanics' and Materialmen's Lien Waivers and Releases
- **What:** Final lien waivers from all contractors and subcontractors who performed work on the property (particularly critical for construction/renovation loans).
- **Why it matters:** Unpaid mechanic's liens can be super-priority in some states and defeat a lender's first lien.
- **Typical form:** Executed unconditional lien waivers (final); lien releases.
- **Where to find it:** Construction administration file; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.08 Tax Certificate / Tax Status Report
- **What:** Evidence that all real property taxes and special assessments are current, typically a county tax certificate or assessor's record.
- **Why it matters:** Unpaid property taxes prime a first mortgage in virtually every state.
- **Typical form:** County tax certificate; assessor's online record; tax search.
- **Where to find it:** County assessor/tax collector; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 04.09 HOA Status Letter / HOA Lien Search (if applicable)
- **What:** Confirmation from any homeowners or condominium association that assessments are current and no super-priority lien exists.
- **Why it matters:** In about half of U.S. states, HOA super-priority liens extinguish even a first mortgage on foreclosure.
- **Typical form:** HOA status letter; lien search from commercial search firm.
- **Where to find it:** HOA management; commercial search firm.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.10 Zoning Letter / Zoning Compliance Certificate
- **What:** Confirmation from the local municipality that the property's current use is a permitted use under applicable zoning, or is a legally non-conforming use.
- **Why it matters:** A non-conforming use that cannot be rebuilt after casualty materially impairs collateral value.
- **Typical form:** Municipal zoning letter; zoning compliance certificate; zoning report from third-party firm.
- **Where to find it:** Local planning/zoning department; zoning report in origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 05 — Borrower & Sponsor Documentation

### 05.01 Borrower Entity Formation Documents
- **What:** Articles of organization (LLC), certificate of formation, or certificate of incorporation for the borrower entity, as filed with the state of organization.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the entity's legal existence and the state of organization (which governs UCC filing jurisdiction and entity-level law).
- **Typical form:** State-certified articles/certificate of organization.
- **Where to find it:** State Secretary of State; closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.02 Borrower Operating Agreement / LLC Agreement
- **What:** The fully executed operating agreement (or partnership agreement) governing the borrower entity, including all amendments.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms authority to borrow, identifies managers/members, and evidences SPE covenants (single-purpose entity restrictions).
- **Typical form:** Executed operating agreement with all amendments and exhibits.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; borrower's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.03 Borrower Good Standing Certificate (Current)
- **What:** Certificate from the borrower's state of organization confirming the entity is in good standing (i.e., not dissolved, revoked, or suspended).
- **Why it matters:** A borrower that has lost good standing may lack authority to execute agreements or transfer assets.
- **Typical form:** State-issued certificate of good standing (ideally dated within 90 days of sale).
- **Where to find it:** State Secretary of State (ordered fresh for the sale).
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.04 Borrower Foreign Qualification Certificates (if applicable)
- **What:** Evidence that the borrower is qualified to do business in the state where the property is located, if different from the state of organization.
- **Why it matters:** An entity not qualified in the property state may be unable to enforce contracts there.
- **Typical form:** Certificate of authority / foreign qualification certificate from property-state Secretary of State.
- **Where to find it:** Secretary of State of the property state; closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 05.05 Borrower Authorization Resolutions / Consent
- **What:** Manager or member resolution (LLC) or board resolution (corporation) authorizing execution of the loan documents.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms authority to borrow. Without proper authorization, the loan documents may be voidable.
- **Typical form:** Executed resolution or unanimous written consent.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.06 Borrower Incumbency Certificate
- **What:** Certificate identifying authorized signatories and their titles, confirming the persons who signed the loan documents had authority to do so.
- **Why it matters:** Provides evidence that the specific individuals who signed were authorized.
- **Typical form:** Executed incumbency certificate from borrower's secretary or manager.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.07 Borrower Organizational Chart (Ownership Structure)
- **What:** Diagram showing the full ownership chain from the individual sponsors to the borrower entity, including all intermediate holding companies and percentage ownership.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer needs to understand who ultimately controls the borrower and who stands behind any guaranties.
- **Typical form:** Org chart diagram (PDF); may be embedded in credit memo or origination package.
- **Where to find it:** Credit memo; origination file; borrower's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.08 OFAC / AML Borrower Screening Records
- **What:** Evidence that OKOA ran OFAC, AML, and KYC checks on the borrower entity and all principal owners at origination, and any re-screen since.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer inherits regulatory exposure if the borrower is a sanctioned party. Buyer's own compliance program will require re-screening; OKOA's documentation shows due diligence was done.
- **Typical form:** Screening printout / report with pass/clear result; dated at origination and any re-screen date.
- **Where to find it:** Compliance file; origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.09 Beneficial Ownership Certification (FinCEN CDD Rule)
- **What:** Certification identifying all individuals who own 25% or more of the borrower entity, as required by FinCEN's Customer Due Diligence rule.
- **Why it matters:** Required for OKOA's own BSA/AML compliance; buyer's compliance team will want to see it.
- **Typical form:** Executed beneficial ownership certification form.
- **Where to find it:** Compliance file; origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 05.10 Borrower Credit Report (at origination)
- **What:** Personal or business credit report for the borrower principal(s) pulled at loan origination.
- **Why it matters:** Historical credit record gives buyer context on sponsor creditworthiness at time of underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Tri-merge credit report or business credit report (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion).
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** low

## Category 06 — Guarantor Documentation

### 06.01 Guarantor Identity and Biographical Information
- **What:** Full legal name, address, date of birth (individuals), and entity information for every guarantor.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer must be able to identify and locate guarantors if enforcement becomes necessary.
- **Typical form:** Guarantor information sheet; copy of government-issued ID for individuals.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file; guaranty documents.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.02 Guarantor Personal Financial Statement (at origination)
- **What:** Signed personal financial statement (assets, liabilities, net worth) for each individual guarantor as of origination.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes whether the guaranty had meaningful economic backing at underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Executed personal financial statement (USCIS Form 1003 or equivalent); may be on lender's standard form.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.03 Guarantor Personal Financial Statement (Current)
- **What:** Most recent signed personal financial statement for each guarantor (within 12 months of sale, if available).
- **Why it matters:** Tells the buyer whether the guaranty has current economic substance — a guarantor whose net worth has evaporated since origination is a red flag.
- **Typical form:** Executed personal financial statement dated within the past 12 months.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer records; annual reporting requirements per loan docs.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 06.04 Guarantor Entity Formation Documents (corporate guarantors)
- **What:** For any corporate or entity guarantors, the same formation documents required for the borrower (articles, operating agreement, good standing).
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the guarantor entity's legal existence and authority to issue the guaranty.
- **Typical form:** State-certified articles; operating agreement; good standing certificate.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; Secretary of State.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.05 Guarantor Authorization Resolutions (corporate guarantors)
- **What:** Corporate or LLC resolution authorizing the entity to execute the guaranty.
- **Why it matters:** Without proper authorization, the guaranty may be voidable.
- **Typical form:** Executed board or manager resolution.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.06 Guarantor OFAC / AML Screening
- **What:** Evidence that OFAC and AML/KYC checks were run on each guarantor at origination and any re-screen since.
- **Why it matters:** Same regulatory exposure as for the borrower; guarantors are principals.
- **Typical form:** Screening report with clear result.
- **Where to find it:** Compliance file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.07 Guarantor Tax Returns (prior 2–3 years, if recourse or carve-out guaranty)
- **What:** Personal (and business, if applicable) federal tax returns for each guarantor for the two to three years prior to origination.
- **Why it matters:** Tax returns confirm income, assets, and the economic substance of the guaranty — more reliable than a self-prepared personal financial statement.
- **Typical form:** Signed federal tax returns (1040 and schedules).
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 07 — Property / Asset Documentation

### 07.01 Property Summary Sheet / Collateral Summary
- **What:** A one-to-two page summary of the collateral property: address, asset type, year built, square footage, unit count (if multifamily), occupancy, and current status.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer's internal approval process requires a concise property description.
- **Typical form:** Loan summary or deal summary prepared by OKOA at origination or most recently updated.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file; OKOA deal file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.02 Rent Roll (current, as of a specific date)
- **What:** Tenant-by-tenant rent roll showing unit or suite, tenant name, lease start/end, contracted rent, actual rent collected, security deposit, and any concessions or arrears.
- **Why it matters:** Primary cash flow evidence for stabilized properties; directly supports the buyer's underwritten debt service coverage.
- **Typical form:** Property management rent roll (Excel or PDF); date-stamped.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; servicer.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.03 Lease Abstracts or Full Lease Copies
- **What:** For commercial properties: full executed copies (or abstracts) of all leases and lease amendments for each tenant.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer must verify rent roll accuracy and identify any adverse lease provisions (purchase options, co-tenancy clauses, termination rights).
- **Typical form:** Executed lease agreements; if voluminous, third-party lease abstracts.
- **Where to find it:** Property management; closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.04 Operating Statements — Trailing 12 Months (T-12)
- **What:** Income and expense statements for the property for the trailing 12 months, showing gross revenue, vacancy, operating expenses, and net operating income.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers underwrite cash-flowing properties on actual NOI. T-12 is the primary income verification tool.
- **Typical form:** Property manager monthly operating statements; year-to-date plus prior-year statements.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; borrower's books.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.05 Operating Statements — Prior 2 Years Annual
- **What:** Annual operating statements for the two calendar years preceding the most recent T-12.
- **Why it matters:** Trend analysis across three years of financial performance reveals seasonality, vacancy cycles, and expense creep.
- **Typical form:** Annual profit and loss statements; tax return Schedule E or Form 8825.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower; property manager; tax returns.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.06 Property Management Agreement
- **What:** The executed property management agreement governing the third-party manager (if any), including management fee, term, and termination provisions.
- **Why it matters:** A long-term non-terminable management agreement with an affiliated manager can impair a buyer's ability to replace management post-foreclosure.
- **Typical form:** Executed property management agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; property management.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 07.07 Certificate of Occupancy and Permits
- **What:** The certificate of occupancy for the improvements and any material building permits (especially for recent renovations or construction).
- **Why it matters:** Without a CO, the improvements may not be legally habitable, which impairs value and marketability.
- **Typical form:** Municipal CO; permit records from local building department.
- **Where to find it:** Local building department; closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.08 Construction Budget and Draw Schedule (construction / renovation loans)
- **What:** The approved construction budget, draw schedule, and a report of all draws funded to date versus remaining unfunded balance.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer inherits any obligation to fund future draws. A clear picture of funded vs. remaining is essential to underwrite completion risk.
- **Typical form:** Approved budget with line-item detail; draw ledger with dates and amounts.
- **Where to find it:** Construction administration file; servicer.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.09 Construction Inspector / Owner's Representative Reports
- **What:** Third-party construction inspector reports for each draw funded, confirming work-in-place matches the draw request.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer needs assurance that draws were funded for completed work, not in advance of completion.
- **Typical form:** Inspector draw reports with percentage-complete by line item.
- **Where to find it:** Construction administration file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.10 Photos of Property (current)
- **What:** Current exterior and interior photographs of the collateral property.
- **Why it matters:** Visual evidence of property condition; detects deferred maintenance or casualty that might not appear in financial statements.
- **Typical form:** Date-stamped photo set (PDF or zip); ideally taken within 6 months of sale.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA site visit records; property inspector; Google Street View as supplement only.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 08 — Third-Party Reports & Inspections

### 08.01 Appraisal Report (original at origination)
- **What:** The full FIRREA-compliant appraisal prepared by a state-certified appraiser at or near origination, establishing the "as-is" and (if applicable) "as-stabilized" or "as-complete" value.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes the loan-to-value used at origination. Buyer uses this as baseline and will order a desk review or new appraisal.
- **Typical form:** Full narrative appraisal report (URAR or narrative format); PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file; appraisal management company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.02 Appraisal Update / Desk Review (if origination appraisal is over 12 months old)
- **What:** An appraisal update or desk review bringing the value opinion current to within 12 months of the sale.
- **Why it matters:** Stale appraisals do not reflect current market conditions. A material decline in value since origination changes the buyer's LTV and risk profile.
- **Typical form:** FIRREA-compliant appraisal update form or desk review report.
- **Where to find it:** Ordered by OKOA or servicer for the loan sale.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.03 Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA)
- **What:** ASTM E1527-21 Phase I ESA prepared by a qualified environmental professional at or near origination.
- **Why it matters:** Environmental contamination can make the collateral worthless and expose the lender (and its successors) to CERCLA liability if innocent landowner protections are not in place. The Phase I establishes the innocent landowner baseline.
- **Typical form:** Phase I ESA report (full narrative with conclusions and recommendations).
- **Where to find it:** Origination file; environmental consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 08.04 Phase II Environmental Site Assessment (if ordered)
- **What:** Subsurface investigation report (soil borings, groundwater sampling) ordered when the Phase I identified recognized environmental conditions (RECs).
- **Why it matters:** Phase II quantifies contamination risk that the Phase I only flagged.
- **Typical form:** Phase II ESA report with laboratory results and risk characterization.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file; environmental consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if Phase I identified RECs)

### 08.05 Environmental Regulatory Database Search Results
- **What:** EDR (Environmental Data Resources) or equivalent regulatory database search underlying the Phase I, identifying nearby sites on federal/state environmental databases.
- **Why it matters:** Shows the environmental context surrounding the property; regulatory-listed sites nearby can affect value.
- **Typical form:** EDR Radius Map Report or equivalent; embedded in Phase I or as standalone exhibit.
- **Where to find it:** Phase I ESA appendix; environmental consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 08.06 Property Condition Report (PCR) / Property Condition Assessment (PCA)
- **What:** ASTM E2018-15 Property Condition Assessment by a licensed engineer or inspector covering structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and roofing systems.
- **Why it matters:** Identifies immediate repair needs (cost-to-cure) and capital expenditure requirements over the loan term; critical for valuing the collateral and sizing reserves.
- **Typical form:** Full PCA/PCR narrative report with immediate repairs table and cost estimates.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file; inspector.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.07 Seismic Risk Report (if property is in high-seismic zone)
- **What:** Seismic probabilistic risk assessment (PML report) for properties in USGS seismic zone 3 or 4 (primarily California, Pacific Northwest, intermountain West).
- **Why it matters:** PML >20% often triggers special insurance requirements; high seismic risk affects insurability and collateral value.
- **Typical form:** PML report (probable maximum loss at 475-year return period); USGS zone map.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file; seismic engineer.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 08.08 Flood Zone Determination / FEMA Flood Map
- **What:** Standard Flood Hazard Determination Form (SFHDF) confirming whether the property is in a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA), plus the relevant FEMA flood map panel.
- **Why it matters:** Properties in SFHA require mandatory flood insurance; FEMA maps can also affect lender's insurance requirements and collateral value.
- **Typical form:** SFHDF from a flood determination company; FEMA FIRM map panel.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file; insurance file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.09 Soils / Geotechnical Report (if applicable — construction or land loans)
- **What:** Geotechnical investigation report assessing soil bearing capacity, expansive soil risk, liquefaction potential, and foundation recommendations.
- **Why it matters:** Soil defects can require costly remediation and delay or prevent construction; material for construction and land loan buyers.
- **Typical form:** Geotechnical report by licensed geotechnical engineer.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file; construction administration file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 08.10 Zoning Report (Third-Party)
- **What:** Third-party zoning compliance report confirming permitted use, current zoning classification, any non-conforming use status, and rebuild rights.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer must understand use limitations and the risk that a casualty could eliminate the right to rebuild.
- **Typical form:** Zoning report from a national zoning firm (e.g., Zoneomics, Zoning Reports, The Report Corp).
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 08.11 Architect's Plans and Specifications (construction loans)
- **What:** Approved architectural drawings, engineering plans, and specifications for the project under construction.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer needs to understand what is being built to assess completion risk and value the as-complete collateral.
- **Typical form:** PDF of permitted plan set; specifications binder.
- **Where to find it:** Construction administration file; architect; local building department.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 09 — Insurance

### 09.01 Current Property (Hazard) Insurance Policy
- **What:** The commercial property insurance policy covering the collateral, with the lender named as an additional insured and mortgagee / loss payee.
- **Why it matters:** Without adequate in-force insurance, a casualty loss could destroy the collateral with no insurance proceeds to pay down the loan.
- **Typical form:** Full insurance policy; declarations page; ACORD 25 or 28 certificate.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower's insurance broker; servicer insurance tracking file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 09.02 Evidence of Lender Named as Additional Insured and Mortgagee
- **What:** Endorsement or certificate confirming OKOA (and its successors and assigns) is named as additional insured and mortgagee / loss payee on the property policy.
- **Why it matters:** Without this endorsement, insurance proceeds go to the borrower — not the lender — in a loss event.
- **Typical form:** ACORD 28 (commercial property) with lender endorsement; standard mortgagee clause.
- **Where to find it:** Insurance policy; servicer file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 09.03 General Liability Insurance Policy
- **What:** The commercial general liability policy covering third-party bodily injury and property damage at the collateral property.
- **Why it matters:** Slip-and-fall or other liability claims at the property can result in judgments that attach as liens against the property.
- **Typical form:** Declarations page; ACORD 25 certificate with lender as additional insured.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower's insurance broker; servicer file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 09.04 Flood Insurance Policy (if property is in SFHA)
- **What:** NFIP or private flood insurance policy providing coverage in an amount satisfying FEMA mandatory purchase requirements.
- **Why it matters:** Mandatory for SFHA properties; lender liability for failing to require it.
- **Typical form:** NFIP declarations; private flood policy declarations.
- **Where to find it:** Insurance broker; servicer file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (SFHA properties only)

### 09.05 Builder's Risk / Course of Construction Insurance (construction loans)
- **What:** Insurance covering the structure during construction against damage or loss before the permanent property policy is in force.
- **Why it matters:** Permanent property insurance does not cover a structure under construction; a fire during construction without builder's risk leaves the collateral uninsured.
- **Typical form:** Builder's risk policy declarations; ACORD certificate.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower's insurance broker; construction administration file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (construction loans)

### 09.06 Rent Loss / Business Interruption Insurance (if income property)
- **What:** Insurance covering lost rental income during a period of restoration following an insured casualty.
- **Why it matters:** Cash flow interruption after a casualty can impair debt service even if the property is being rebuilt.
- **Typical form:** Rent loss endorsement or business interruption policy.
- **Where to find it:** Insurance broker; servicer file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 09.07 Insurance Compliance / Premium Payment Confirmation
- **What:** Evidence that all insurance premiums are current and policies have not lapsed.
- **Why it matters:** A lapsed policy is as bad as no policy. Buyer needs to know coverage is actually in force.
- **Typical form:** Servicer insurance compliance report; premium payment receipts; current policy expiration dates.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer insurance tracking system.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 09.08 Umbrella / Excess Liability Policy
- **What:** Umbrella or excess liability policy providing additional liability coverage above the primary general liability limit.
- **Why it matters:** For larger or higher-risk properties, the primary GL limit may be insufficient to cover a major liability event.
- **Typical form:** Umbrella/excess declarations; ACORD certificate.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower's insurance broker.
- **Severity if absent:** low

## Category 10 — Servicing & Payment History

### 10.01 Payment History — Full Loan Life (Month-by-Month)
- **What:** A month-by-month ledger of every scheduled payment, the amount due, the amount received, the date received, and the balance applied to principal and interest.
- **Why it matters:** The single most important operational data point. Buyer needs to see the complete payment track record.
- **Typical form:** Servicer payment history report (Excel or PDF); loan accounting system extract.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer / OKOA loan accounting system.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 10.02 Current Unpaid Principal Balance (UPB) Statement
- **What:** A certified statement of the outstanding principal balance as of a specific date, with supporting reconciliation.
- **Why it matters:** This is the number the buyer is paying for. An incorrect UPB overstates or understates the purchase price.
- **Typical form:** Servicer-certified UPB statement; loan payoff quote (if applicable).
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; OKOA loan accounting.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 10.03 Accrued and Unpaid Interest Calculation
- **What:** Calculation of all interest accrued but not yet paid as of the payoff / sale date, including any default interest if the loan is in default.
- **Why it matters:** Accrued interest is part of the purchase price calculation; inaccurate accrual creates disputes at closing.
- **Typical form:** Servicer interest accrual statement; per diem rate calculation.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; OKOA loan accounting.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 10.04 Late Charges / Default Interest Ledger
- **What:** Itemized record of all late charges and default interest accrued (whether or not collected) over the loan life.
- **Why it matters:** These are part of the loan's total debt balance and may be collectible from the borrower — buyer needs to know the full outstanding amount.
- **Typical form:** Servicer fee ledger; late charge log.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 10.05 Escrow / Reserve Account Balances
- **What:** Current balances in all escrow and reserve accounts held by the servicer — tax escrow, insurance escrow, interest reserve, capital expenditure reserve, environmental reserve, completion reserve.
- **Why it matters:** Reserve balances transfer to the buyer and are part of the deal economics. Buyer needs to know what it is receiving.
- **Typical form:** Servicer escrow/reserve balance report; account statements.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 10.06 Escrow / Reserve Activity Ledger
- **What:** Transaction-by-transaction ledger of all deposits to and disbursements from each escrow and reserve account.
- **Why it matters:** Validates current balances and confirms reserves were drawn for proper purposes.
- **Typical form:** Servicer ledger report.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 10.07 Loan Servicing Agreement / Sub-Servicing Agreement (if third-party servicer)
- **What:** The executed agreement under which a third-party servicer manages the loan on OKOA's behalf.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer needs to know whether the servicing relationship continues post-transfer and on what terms.
- **Typical form:** Executed servicing agreement.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA servicer file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 10.08 Servicer Transfer Notices / Consent Requirements
- **What:** Any notice or consent requirements triggered by a transfer of servicing under applicable law (RESPA, TILA) or the loan agreement.
- **Why it matters:** Failure to provide required servicer transfer notices exposes the buyer to regulatory liability.
- **Typical form:** RESPA servicing transfer notice requirements summary; loan agreement servicer transfer provisions.
- **Where to find it:** Compliance counsel review; loan agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 10.09 Draw Request Ledger (construction loans)
- **What:** Complete log of every draw request: date requested, amount requested, amount approved, amount funded, cumulative funded to date, and remaining loan commitment.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer needs a precise picture of what has been funded and what remains to be funded.
- **Typical form:** Draw request log (Excel); servicer construction loan system extract.
- **Where to find it:** Construction administration file; servicer.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (construction loans)

### 10.10 Borrower Advance / Letter of Credit Tracking
- **What:** Log of any protective advances made by OKOA (e.g., for unpaid taxes, insurance premiums) that are added to the loan balance, and any letters of credit outstanding under the loan.
- **Why it matters:** Protective advances affect the true loan balance and must be disclosed; LCs are contingent obligations the buyer inherits.
- **Typical form:** Servicer protective advance log; LC tracking schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 11 — Default / Forbearance / Modification History

### 11.01 Default Notices Issued
- **What:** Copies of all notices of default, notices of acceleration, cure notices, and any other default-related correspondence sent to the borrower or guarantors over the loan life.
- **Why it matters:** Documents the loan's credit history and any period of distress; may reveal unresolved defaults or waivers.
- **Typical form:** Copies of written notices with delivery confirmation (certified mail, email).
- **Where to find it:** Servicer correspondence file; OKOA loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if loan has had default events)

### 11.02 Cure or Waiver Correspondence
- **What:** Any letters, emails, or agreements in which OKOA waived a default event or accepted late payment without reservation of rights.
- **Why it matters:** Undisclosed waivers can be asserted by the borrower to defeat the buyer's enforcement rights.
- **Typical form:** Executed waiver letters; reservation of rights letters.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA loan file; servicer file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 11.03 Forbearance Agreements
- **What:** Any executed forbearance agreements suspending OKOA's enforcement rights in exchange for borrower obligations.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer inherits the forbearance — if still in effect, buyer's enforcement rights are limited.
- **Typical form:** Executed forbearance agreement with all amendments.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if any forbearance exists)

### 11.04 Loan Modification Agreements
- **What:** All executed loan modification agreements altering rate, maturity, amortization, or other material terms since origination.
- **Why it matters:** Modifications change the loan terms the buyer is purchasing. Undisclosed modifications are a material misrepresentation.
- **Typical form:** Executed modification agreements (signed by both parties, notarized if recorded).
- **Where to find it:** OKOA loan file; county recorder (if recorded).
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 11.05 Extension Agreements
- **What:** Any executed maturity extension agreements, including the new maturity date, any extension conditions (equity paydown, reserve replenishment), and extension fees collected.
- **Why it matters:** Buyer needs to know the true current maturity date; an extension may have been granted with conditions that are unmet.
- **Typical form:** Executed extension agreement.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 11.06 Payoff Demand / Payoff Statement History
- **What:** Any payoff demands or payoff statements previously issued to the borrower, including dates and amounts.
- **Why it matters:** Prior payoff demands may reveal negotiation history or a near-refinance that fell through, relevant context for buyer.
- **Typical form:** Executed payoff demand letters; payoff statement documents.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA loan file; servicer.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 11.07 Foreclosure Proceedings Records (if any)
- **What:** If foreclosure was initiated at any point: notice of default, notice of trustee's sale, lis pendens, any court filings, and the current status of those proceedings.
- **Why it matters:** Active or recently-dismissed foreclosure proceedings are material to the buyer's enforcement analysis and litigation risk.
- **Typical form:** Filed court documents; trustee sale notices; lis pendens.
- **Where to find it:** Court records; OKOA litigation file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if foreclosure was or is active)

### 11.08 Bankruptcy Filings by Borrower or Guarantor (if any)
- **What:** Any voluntary or involuntary bankruptcy petitions filed by the borrower or any guarantor, and the current status.
- **Why it matters:** An active or recently-discharged bankruptcy affects loan enforceability, automatic stay analysis, and guarantor liability.
- **Typical form:** PACER case docket; bankruptcy court filings; proof of claim filed by OKOA.
- **Where to find it:** PACER; OKOA litigation file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if any bankruptcy is or was pending)

### 11.09 Receiver / Receivership Orders (if property is under receivership)
- **What:** Court-ordered appointment of a receiver to manage the property and the receiver's reports, if applicable.
- **Why it matters:** A receiver changes the operational control structure; buyer must know whether receivership is ongoing and on what terms.
- **Typical form:** Court order appointing receiver; receiver status reports.
- **Where to find it:** Court records; OKOA litigation file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if receivership is active)

### 11.10 Watch List / Credit Monitoring Notes
- **What:** OKOA's internal credit watch list notes, watchlist classifications, and credit monitoring memos for this loan.
- **Why it matters:** Internal credit assessments may reveal concerns not apparent from payment history alone.
- **Typical form:** Internal credit memo or loan review report.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA credit file; portfolio monitoring records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 12 — Financial Statements & Underwriting File

### 12.01 Borrower's Audited Financial Statements (if available)
- **What:** Audited financial statements for the borrower entity for the two to three years prior to origination.
- **Why it matters:** Audited statements are the highest-quality evidence of borrower financial condition; absence may indicate a smaller operation.
- **Typical form:** Audited financial statements with auditor's report.

- **What:** If audited statements are not available, CPA-reviewed or compiled financial statements for the borrower entity.

- **Typical form:** CPA-reviewed or compiled statements.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 12.03 Borrower / Sponsor Tax Returns (2–3 Years)
- **What:** Federal tax returns for the borrower entity and principal sponsors for the two to three years prior to origination.
- **Why it matters:** Tax returns are the most independently verified evidence of income; lenders use them to verify self-reported income.
- **Typical form:** Signed federal tax returns; Form 1065, 1120S, or 1040 with all schedules.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 12.04 Current Borrower / Property Financial Statements (post-origination)
- **What:** The most recent annual and quarterly financial statements for the borrower and the property, as required by the loan covenants.
- **Why it matters:** Shows current financial condition; reveals deterioration since origination.
- **Typical form:** Annual and quarterly financial statements provided under loan covenant reporting requirements.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer annual reporting file; OKOA covenant compliance file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 12.05 Covenant Compliance Certificates
- **What:** Borrower-executed compliance certificates confirming compliance with loan covenants (DSCR, occupancy, reserve levels) for all reporting periods since origination.
- **Why it matters:** A broken covenant that was not declared a default by OKOA may be an implied waiver — or may reveal concealed credit issues.
- **Typical form:** Executed compliance certificates per the frequency required by the loan agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer covenant tracking file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 12.06 Original Credit Memorandum / Underwriting Memo
- **What:** OKOA's internal credit memorandum prepared at origination, including underwriting assumptions, loan committee approval, and risk analysis.
- **Why it matters:** Gives buyer the clearest view of OKOA's origination rationale, what risks were identified, and what assumptions were made.
- **Typical form:** Internal credit memo (PDF); may include financial model.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA credit file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 12.07 Loan Approval / Investment Committee Approval
- **What:** Evidence that OKOA's loan committee or investment committee approved the loan at origination, including the approved terms.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the loan was originated through a proper internal approval process.
- **Typical form:** Loan committee approval minutes; IC memo with approval signatures.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA credit file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 12.08 Financial Model / Pro Forma (origination)
- **What:** The financial model or pro forma used to underwrite the loan at origination — rents, expenses, NOI, cap rate, as-stabilized value, debt service coverage, and loan sizing.
- **Why it matters:** Documents the assumptions underlying the original underwriting; buyer can compare to current actuals.
- **Typical form:** Excel financial model; pro forma income statement.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA credit file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 12.09 Market Study / Rent Comparable Analysis (at origination)
- **What:** A market study or rent comparable analysis supporting the underwritten rents or stabilized occupancy assumptions.
- **Why it matters:** Supports the appraiser's and underwriter's value assumptions; relevant if the property has not yet stabilized.
- **Typical form:** Third-party market study; appraiser's rent comp grid.
- **Where to find it:** Appraisal appendix; origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** low

## Category 13 — Tax & Regulatory

### 13.01 Real Property Tax Bills — Current Year
- **What:** The most recent real property tax bill(s) for all tax parcels comprising the collateral, confirming the annual tax obligation and payment status.
- **Why it matters:** Taxes are the senior lien; buyer must know the annual obligation and whether it is current.
- **Typical form:** County tax collector tax bill; online tax payment confirmation.

- **What:** Evidence that property taxes have been paid in full and on time for the past two to three years.
- **Why it matters:** Tax delinquencies generate super-priority liens. A pattern of late payment is a credit concern.

- **Where to find it:** County tax collector; servicer.

### 13.03 Special Assessments / Mello-Roos / Assessment District Obligations
- **What:** Disclosure of any special assessments, Mello-Roos CFD bonds, benefit assessment districts, or improvement district obligations running with the property.
- **Why it matters:** These are property-level financial obligations that can be substantial and that run with the land — buyer inherits them.
- **Typical form:** Title commitment Schedule B; county assessment records; CFD annual report.

- **Severity if absent:** high

### 13.04 Transfer Tax Analysis / Tax-Free Transfer Structuring
- **What:** If the loan sale is structured as a whole loan transfer, analysis of any real property transfer tax implications if the collateral is in a transfer-tax-sensitive jurisdiction.
- **Why it matters:** A loan transfer itself generally does not trigger real property transfer tax, but a transfer of entity interests may; OKOA should flag jurisdiction-specific issues.

- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal / tax file.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 13.05 Borrower's Tax Identification Numbers
- **What:** Federal Employer Identification Numbers (EINs) for the borrower entity and any guarantor entities; Social Security Numbers for individual guarantors (held in secure file, not in data room).
- **Why it matters:** Required for 1099-related reporting, IRS compliance, and buyer's own KYC/AML obligations.
- **Typical form:** IRS EIN confirmation letter; W-9 form.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; compliance file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 13.06 Licensing and Permits — Business Operations
- **What:** Any operating licenses, business licenses, or special permits required for the borrower to operate the property (e.g., hotel license, liquor license, healthcare facility license).
- **Why it matters:** A license that cannot be transferred may impair the buyer's ability to operate the property post-foreclosure.
- **Typical form:** Copies of licenses; confirmation of transferability.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower; local licensing authority.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 14 — Litigation & Disclosures

### 14.01 Litigation Search Results (Borrower and Guarantors)
- **What:** Current litigation search results for the borrower entity and all guarantors identifying any pending lawsuits, judgments, or liens in the relevant jurisdictions.
- **Why it matters:** Pending litigation against the borrower or guarantors can impair the guaranty and signal financial distress or fraud.
- **Typical form:** Commercial litigation search report (e.g., Westlaw CourtExpress; LexisNexis Courtlink); state court docket searches.
- **Where to find it:** Commercial search firm (ordered for the sale).
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.02 Judgment Lien Searches (All Jurisdictions)
- **What:** Judgment lien searches against the borrower and guarantors in the property state, the state of organization, and any state where guarantors are known to have assets.
- **Why it matters:** Judgment liens can cloud title and impair guarantors' ability to pay.
- **Typical form:** Court docket search; county clerk judgment search.
- **Where to find it:** Commercial search firm.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.03 OKOA's Loan-Level Litigation File
- **What:** All legal correspondence, demand letters, and litigation documents relating to this specific loan — including any claims by the borrower against OKOA (lender liability claims).
- **Why it matters:** Lender liability claims travel with the loan. Buyer inherits any pending or threatened litigation by the borrower.
- **Typical form:** Litigation file contents; counsel memos.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if any active claims)

### 14.04 Regulatory Examination / Enforcement Actions
- **What:** Any regulatory examination findings or enforcement actions related to this loan or OKOA's handling of it.
- **Why it matters:** Regulatory issues may impair the loan's validity or impose remediation obligations.
- **Typical form:** Regulatory correspondence; enforcement order (if any).
- **Where to find it:** OKOA compliance file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 14.05 OFAC / Sanctions Compliance — Loan-Level
- **What:** Confirmation that no payment has been received from or made to a sanctioned party in connection with this loan.
- **Why it matters:** OFAC violations can result in forfeiture of the loan proceeds; buyer's own sanctions screening will flag issues.
- **Typical form:** OFAC compliance certification; screening records for all payments.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA compliance file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.06 Anti-Predatory Lending Representations
- **What:** OKOA's representations that the loan was not made in violation of applicable federal or state anti-predatory lending laws (HOEPA, state equivalents).
- **Why it matters:** A loan originated in violation of predatory lending law may be unenforceable or subject to rescission.
- **Typical form:** Representations in the loan purchase agreement; origination compliance checklist.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA compliance file; loan purchase agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 14.07 Usury Compliance Confirmation
- **What:** Confirmation that the loan's interest rate complies with applicable state usury limits (particularly relevant for bridge and hard-money loans).
- **Why it matters:** A usurious loan may be void or subject to penalty; certain states provide severe remedies.
- **Typical form:** Usury savings clause in the note; origination counsel usury opinion or analysis.
- **Where to find it:** Loan documents (usury savings clause); origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 15 — Closing & Funding Documentation

### 15.01 Closing / Settlement Statement (HUD-1 or ALTA Settlement Statement)
- **What:** The settlement statement from loan origination showing all closing costs, fees, origination charges, escrow deposits, and net proceeds.
- **Why it matters:** Documents what was funded and what the borrower received; relevant if the closing costs are disputed or if there are undisclosed concessions.
- **Typical form:** ALTA Settlement Statement or HUD-1; PDF from closing agent.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; title/escrow company.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 15.02 Disbursement Authorization / Funding Instruction
- **What:** The disbursement authorization signed by OKOA and the borrower authorizing the specific amounts funded at closing.
- **Why it matters:** Documents authorization for each disbursement; relevant in the event of a challenge to the loan amount.
- **Typical form:** Executed disbursement authorization.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 15.03 Closing Checklist and Counsel Sign-Off
- **What:** The closing checklist confirming all conditions precedent to funding were satisfied, signed off by OKOA's counsel.
- **Why it matters:** Evidence that all closing conditions were met; relevant to enforceability arguments.
- **Typical form:** Executed closing checklist; counsel closing certificate or bring-down letter.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder; origination counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 15.04 Origination Counsel Closing Opinion
- **What:** Legal opinion letter from OKOA's origination counsel confirming the enforceability of the loan documents, the borrower's authority, and the validity of the lien.
- **Why it matters:** An enforceability opinion from counsel at origination is strong evidence the documents were properly executed and enforceable.
- **Typical form:** Executed legal opinion letter addressed to OKOA and its successors and assigns.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.05 Wire Transfer Confirmation (Funding)
- **What:** Bank wire transfer confirmation showing that loan proceeds were actually funded to the borrower or closing agent.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the loan was actually funded (relevant to fraud or non-funding defenses by the borrower).
- **Typical form:** Wire transfer confirmation receipt.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA treasury / accounting records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 16 — Loan Sale Transaction Documents

### 16.01 Loan Purchase Agreement (Draft or Executed)
- **What:** The loan purchase and sale agreement between OKOA (seller) and the buyer, including representations and warranties, excluded liabilities, and conditions to closing.
- **Why it matters:** Governs the entire transaction. Representations and warranties determine OKOA's ongoing exposure post-sale.
- **Typical form:** Executed LPA (at closing); negotiated draft during diligence.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal file; transaction counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.02 OKOA Seller Representations and Warranties Schedule
- **What:** The schedules to the LPA disclosing exceptions to OKOA's representations and warranties (e.g., known defaults, pending litigation, known title defects).
- **Why it matters:** Undisclosed exceptions may give buyer a rep-and-warranty claim against OKOA post-closing.
- **Typical form:** Disclosure schedules attached to and incorporated in the LPA.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal file; transaction counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.03 Trade Confirmation / Term Sheet
- **What:** The executed trade confirmation or term sheet (if any) signed by both parties before the full LPA is executed, setting out price, purchase rate, closing date, and key conditions.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes the binding commercial terms ahead of full legal documentation; may be enforceable even if the LPA is not yet signed.
- **Typical form:** Executed term sheet; trade confirmation (for secondary market transactions).
- **Where to find it:** OKOA deal file; transaction counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.04 Allonge Endorsing Note to Buyer
- **What:** Allonge executed by OKOA endorsing the promissory note to the buyer (or in blank), to be delivered at closing.
- **Why it matters:** This is the instrument that actually transfers the debt obligation to the buyer.
- **Typical form:** Allonge executed by OKOA with appropriate endorsement.
- **Where to find it:** Prepared by transaction counsel; delivered at closing.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.05 Assignment of Mortgage / Deed of Trust to Buyer
- **What:** Executed assignment of mortgage or deed of trust from OKOA to buyer, to be recorded in the property-state county recorder's office.
- **Why it matters:** This is the instrument that transfers the lien to the buyer. Without recording, the buyer's lien interest is unperfected as against third parties.
- **Typical form:** Executed and notarized assignment of deed of trust; ready for recording at closing.
- **Where to find it:** Prepared by transaction counsel; delivered at closing.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.06 UCC-3 Assignment to Buyer
- **What:** UCC-3 amendment filing assigning OKOA's secured party position under all applicable UCC-1 financing statements to the buyer.
- **Why it matters:** Without a UCC-3 assignment, the buyer's security interest in personal property collateral is not publicly reflected as theirs.
- **Typical form:** UCC-3 assignment filing; prepared for filing at closing.
- **Where to find it:** Prepared by transaction counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.07 Assignment of Leases and Rents to Buyer
- **What:** Recorded assignment of the existing assignment of leases and rents from OKOA to buyer.
- **Why it matters:** Transfers the rent collection rights to the buyer; without it, the buyer cannot enforce the ALR directly.
- **Typical form:** Executed and recorded assignment of ALR.
- **Where to find it:** Prepared by transaction counsel; delivered at closing.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.08 Servicing Transfer Notice to Borrower
- **What:** Written notice to the borrower informing it that the loan has been sold and identifying the new servicer and remittance address for future payments.
- **Why it matters:** RESPA (if applicable) requires a 15-day advance notice. Failure to notify the borrower creates payment direction confusion and regulatory risk.
- **Typical form:** Formal written notice from OKOA (outgoing servicer) and buyer (incoming servicer).
- **Where to find it:** Prepared by servicer / transaction counsel; sent at or after closing.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.09 SNDA Assignment to Buyer (if SNDAs exist)
- **What:** If SNDAs were executed at origination, an assignment of those SNDAs to the buyer so the buyer has the benefit of tenant agreements.
- **Why it matters:** SNDAs run to the lender and its successors; formal assignment ensures buyer's standing under each SNDA.
- **Typical form:** Assignment of SNDA instruments.
- **Where to find it:** Prepared by transaction counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 16.10 Escrow / Reserve Account Transfer Instructions
- **What:** Written instructions for transferring all escrow and reserve balances to the buyer's designated accounts on the closing date.
- **Why it matters:** Reserve balances are part of the deal economics; transfer instructions ensure funds move accurately and promptly.
- **Typical form:** Account transfer letter; wire instructions to receiving bank.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; OKOA treasury.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.11 Closing Statement — Loan Sale (Purchase Price Reconciliation)
- **What:** A closing statement allocating the purchase price between UPB, accrued interest, reserve transfers, origination fee credits, and any purchase price adjustments.
- **Why it matters:** Documents the final agreed economics of the transaction; resolves any post-closing disputes.
- **Typical form:** Closing statement / settlement statement for the loan sale.
- **Where to find it:** Escrow agent; transaction counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.12 Broker / Intermediary Agreement (if applicable)
- **What:** Any executed brokerage or intermediary agreement pursuant to which a broker or loan sale platform (e.g., Broadmark, Mission Capital, Cantor) facilitated the sale, including fee arrangements.
- **Why it matters:** Undisclosed broker fees can create disputes; buyer needs to know if a third party has a claim on any sale proceeds.
- **Typical form:** Executed broker agreement; fee disclosure.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA deal file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium
