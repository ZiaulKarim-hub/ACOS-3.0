# Base Checklist — Lender Package (Takeout / Refinance Pitch)

> **Scope:** Outbound diligence supporting a pitch to a takeout / refinance lender.
> **Role:** Prior — applied via Phase 7a tailoring to produce per-deal scope.
> **Default action:** Every item is `in_scope` until tailoring marks it otherwise.
> **Audience:** CRE lender (bank, life co, agency, debt fund, family office) evaluating a refinance or takeout of OKOA's existing position.
> **Frame:** This is a sales package. Every item must earn its place by advancing the narrative, supporting underwriting, or preempting a credibility question.

---

## Category 01 — Executive Summary & Narrative

Items that set the frame before the new lender opens a single spreadsheet. If the story is not clear here, nothing else lands.

### 01.01 Executive summary memo (1–2 pages)
- **What:** Deal snapshot, sponsor bio thumbnail, property description, loan snapshot, key performance metrics, refinance thesis, and the ask.
- **Why it matters:** Must be self-contained; a busy CIO should be able to read this alone and understand the deal.
- **Typical form:** 1–2 page memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.02 Refinance thesis narrative
- **What:** Why this property is refinanceable now, why this lender is the right fit, what happens at takeout, and why the timeline is credible.
- **Why it matters:** Distinct from the executive summary; this is the persuasive argument, not the fact sheet.
- **Typical form:** 1–2 page narrative memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.03 Deal snapshot / cover sheet
- **What:** Property address, asset type, unpaid principal balance, original loan amount, origination date, maturity date, rate, debt service coverage ratio, loan-to-value, occupancy, proposed takeout amount, and proposed takeout lender type.
- **Why it matters:** Single-page routing sheet used inside the takeout lender's shop to circulate the deal.
- **Typical form:** Single-page PDF or DOCX cover sheet.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.04 Transaction timeline
- **What:** Origination date, key milestones (stabilization, capital expenditure completion, lease-up), current date, maturity date, proposed refinance close date.
- **Why it matters:** Anchors the bridge-to-permanent narrative and makes the urgency of refinancing legible.
- **Typical form:** Timeline graphic or table, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or asset management records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.05 OKOA relationship overview
- **What:** Brief description of OKOA's position, how the loan was sourced, and OKOA's history with the borrower (including any prior loans).
- **Why it matters:** Establishes credibility of OKOA's prior diligence; new lenders trust prior lenders who can narrate.
- **Typical form:** 1-page narrative memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.06 Table of contents with hyperlinks to sections
- **What:** Navigable table of contents linking to each section of the digital data room or package document.
- **Why it matters:** Functional requirement for a digital data room; saves the new lender's time and signals organizational quality.
- **Typical form:** Hyperlinked PDF or document index.
- **Where to find it:** Generated by OKOA as part of package assembly.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.07 Glossary of defined terms
- **What:** Definitions of deal-specific shorthand, non-standard metrics, or abbreviations used throughout the package.
- **Why it matters:** Useful for life companies and agencies whose analysts may not share private equity lender vocabulary.
- **Typical form:** 1-page appendix, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** Generated by OKOA as part of package assembly.
- **Severity if absent:** low

## Category 02 — Loan Performance History

The single most powerful signal of credit quality. A clean payment history is the first thing a takeout lender checks.

### 02.01 Full loan payment history ledger
- **What:** Monthly payment received, date received, amount applied to principal, interest, and escrow, and running unpaid principal balance for every payment period.
- **Why it matters:** Must be granular to the day; "on time" narratives without documentation are not credible.
- **Typical form:** Excel workbook or servicer-generated ledger, PDF export.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.02 Payment status summary
- **What:** Count of on-time payments, count of late payments, days-late distribution, and any missed payments.
- **Why it matters:** Quantified version of the ledger; allows the takeout lender to benchmark against portfolio norms.
- **Typical form:** Summary table, PDF or Excel.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.03 Default and cure history
- **What:** Any event-of-default notices issued, cure timeline, current cure status, and waiver letters if applicable.
- **Why it matters:** If there were no defaults, this item is satisfied by a one-line certification; if there were, full documentation is required.
- **Typical form:** Executed notices and waiver letters, PDF; certification memo if no defaults occurred.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.04 Loan modification history
- **What:** All executed amendments, modification agreements, term extensions, rate adjustments, and the business rationale for each.
- **Why it matters:** Modifications are not disqualifying if explained; unexplained ones are red flags.
- **Typical form:** Executed modification agreements with cover memo, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.05 Forbearance history
- **What:** Any forbearance agreement executed, its terms, duration, borrower's performance during forbearance, exit conditions, and whether exit conditions were met.
- **Why it matters:** COVID-era forbearance is normalized; what matters is whether the borrower performed per the forbearance terms.
- **Typical form:** Executed forbearance agreement and compliance certification, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.06 Escrow account analysis
- **What:** Tax escrow balance, insurance escrow balance, required monthly deposit, and projected shortfall or surplus at next disbursement.
- **Why it matters:** Shortfalls indicate property cash flow stress and signal that the property may not be self-funding its obligations.
- **Typical form:** Escrow analysis schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.07 Reserve account balances
- **What:** Replacement reserve, operating reserve, capital expenditure reserve, carry reserve (if structured), and holdback remaining (if any); required balances versus actual balances.
- **Why it matters:** Variances between required and actual reserve balances must be explained; deficiencies signal covenant non-compliance.
- **Typical form:** Reserve balance schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.08 Force-placed insurance history
- **What:** Any periods when the borrower's coverage lapsed and OKOA placed force-placed insurance, premiums charged, and resolution.
- **Why it matters:** Lapse history is a covenant-compliance signal and indicates borrower's willingness to maintain required coverage.
- **Typical form:** Force-placed insurance notices and resolution correspondence, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 02.09 Covenant compliance history
- **What:** Debt service coverage ratio covenant tests (quarterly or annual), occupancy covenant tests, any cure periods triggered and resolved.
- **Why it matters:** Especially important for construction-to-permanent or value-add loans with springing covenants.
- **Typical form:** Covenant compliance certificate or tracking schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 02.10 Lender correspondence log
- **What:** Any material notices sent or received, including waiver requests, covenant cure notices, force majeure claims, and casualty notices.
- **Why it matters:** Provides institutional memory of loan management quality; a complete log signals disciplined servicing.
- **Typical form:** Compiled correspondence log with document index, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 02.11 Maturity extension history
- **What:** Original maturity, any extension options exercised, conditions precedent to each extension and whether met, and current maturity date.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the borrower managed the loan as structured and that extension conditions were satisfied properly.
- **Typical form:** Extension option exercise notices and confirmation letters, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 03 — Current Loan Snapshot

The cold financial facts of OKOA's existing position as of the package date.

### 03.01 Current unpaid principal balance (UPB)
- **What:** Certified unpaid principal balance as of a specific date within 30 days of package delivery.
- **Why it matters:** Date stamp is mandatory; stale unpaid principal balance figures are misleading and create payoff calculation errors.
- **Typical form:** Certified balance statement, PDF letter from OKOA or servicer.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.02 Accrued and unpaid interest
- **What:** Interest accrued through the package date and any default interest accrued if applicable.
- **Why it matters:** The new lender needs to model payoff; accrued interest is part of the payoff amount and affects sources and uses.
- **Typical form:** Accrued interest schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.03 Current loan-to-value (LTV) based on most recent valuation
- **What:** Unpaid principal balance divided by as-is value from the most recent valuation; must cite the valuation source and date.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes the starting credit position for the new lender's underwriting model.
- **Typical form:** Single-metric certification or excerpt from underwriting workbook, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file or most recent appraisal.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.04 Current debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
- **What:** Trailing 12-month net operating income divided by annual debt service; must cite the trailing 12-month source and any adjustments.
- **Why it matters:** Primary credit metric for all institutional lenders; the starting point for their own underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Calculation worksheet or underwriting exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file or asset management records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.05 Current debt yield
- **What:** Trailing 12-month net operating income divided by unpaid principal balance.
- **Why it matters:** Increasingly the primary underwriting metric for institutional lenders; required by most debt funds.
- **Typical form:** Calculation exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.06 Current pay rate and accrual rate
- **What:** If payment-in-kind, split-rate, or default interest applies, both the pay rate and accrual rate must be shown.
- **Why it matters:** Ambiguity on the pay rate creates underwriting model errors and can mislead the new lender on debt service coverage.
- **Typical form:** Rate certification or loan summary exhibit, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Loan documents and servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.07 Loan maturity date and any extension options remaining
- **What:** Original maturity, current maturity after any extensions exercised, remaining extension options and their conditions.
- **Why it matters:** Maturity creates the refinance urgency narrative; lenders need to know exactly how much runway exists.
- **Typical form:** Loan summary table, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** Loan documents and OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.08 Scheduled monthly payment
- **What:** Principal plus interest plus escrow breakdown for each scheduled monthly payment.
- **Why it matters:** Needed to model the payoff timeline and verify debt service coverage calculations.
- **Typical form:** Payment schedule or amortization table, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.09 Prepayment provisions
- **What:** Yield maintenance, defeasance, step-down, open period, and current prepayment cost at the proposed refinance date.
- **Why it matters:** The takeout lender may help structure the prepayment cost into the refinance; it affects the sources and uses table.
- **Typical form:** Prepayment calculation exhibit, Excel; or excerpt from loan documents.
- **Where to find it:** Loan documents and OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.10 Loan structure summary
- **What:** Senior only, A/B, mezzanine, preferred equity, construction holdback, or participation interest — the full capital stack position of the existing loan.
- **Why it matters:** The new lender must understand how OKOA's position fits in the full stack and what gets paid off at takeout.
- **Typical form:** Capital stack diagram or summary table, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.11 Recourse summary
- **What:** Full recourse, partial recourse, or non-recourse with standard carveouts; springing recourse triggers and current status.
- **Why it matters:** Affects the new lender's view of guarantor exposure and how they model credit risk.
- **Typical form:** Recourse summary memo or loan summary exhibit, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Loan documents and OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.12 Outstanding holdbacks
- **What:** Original holdback amount, disbursed to date, remaining balance, and conditions to disbursement.
- **Why it matters:** If a holdback is unfunded, the new lender must model the cost to complete as part of their underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Holdback tracking schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's construction administration or loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 03.13 Current escrow and reserve balances
- **What:** Itemized escrow and reserve balances by account as of the package date.
- **Why it matters:** Feeds directly into the payoff calculation; the new lender must account for reserve credits and deficits.
- **Typical form:** Reserve and escrow balance schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.14 Payoff quote letter
- **What:** Formal payoff quote through a proposed payoff date, including per diem accrual.
- **Why it matters:** Some takeout lenders require a payoff quote before issuing a term sheet; it also confirms total payoff amount for sources and uses.
- **Typical form:** Payoff quote letter, PDF on OKOA letterhead.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing team generates on request.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 04 — Borrower & Sponsor Profile

The human story behind the deal. Institutional lenders underwrite the sponsor as seriously as the property.

### 04.01 Entity organizational chart
- **What:** Full ownership structure from the borrowing entity up to the ultimate beneficial owner(s), showing percentage interests at each level.
- **Why it matters:** Required for Know Your Customer / Anti-Money Laundering compliance by most institutional lenders; also clarifies who controls decisions.
- **Typical form:** Organizational chart diagram, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor or borrower's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 04.02 Certificate of formation / operating agreement for borrowing entity
- **What:** Current governing documents showing member interests and manager authority.
- **Why it matters:** The new lender will require these in their own diligence; including them in the package saves time and signals cooperation.
- **Typical form:** Executed operating agreement and state-issued certificate of formation, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.03 Key principal bios
- **What:** One-page professional summary for each principal with more than 20% beneficial interest; includes education, commercial real estate career history, and notable deals.
- **Why it matters:** The bio should be curated to highlight experience relevant to this asset type and market.
- **Typical form:** One-page bios, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.04 Sponsor track record
- **What:** Closed transactions list with property type, market, loan amount, equity invested, exit date, and realized return; minimum 5 years of history.
- **Why it matters:** This is the most persuasive single document in the package for an equity-strong borrower.
- **Typical form:** Track record table or schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 04.05 Current portfolio summary
- **What:** Properties currently owned or controlled, with asset type, market, current occupancy, current loan-to-value (if leveraged), and estimated equity.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that the sponsor has bandwidth and is not over-leveraged across the portfolio.
- **Typical form:** Portfolio schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.06 Sponsor references
- **What:** Two to three lender references with name, institution, phone number, prior loan amount, and relationship summary.
- **Why it matters:** Life companies and banks almost always call references; including them proactively demonstrates confidence.
- **Typical form:** Reference list, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 04.07 Organizational chart for property management and asset management entities
- **What:** Chart showing who is responsible for property operations day-to-day, including any third-party managers or asset managers.
- **Why it matters:** Institutional lenders want to know whether the sponsor is self-managing or using a third party and who ultimately controls operating decisions.
- **Typical form:** Organizational chart diagram, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor or property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 04.08 Key-man / guarantor identification
- **What:** Who signs the carveout guaranty, what is their net worth, and whether they are the same person as the operating principal.
- **Why it matters:** Guarantor identity and strength is a credit underwriting input; misalignment between operator and guarantor raises credit concerns.
- **Typical form:** Guarantor identification memo or personal financial statement header, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor; loan documents.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.09 Litigation history
- **What:** Any material litigation involving the sponsor, guarantor, or key principals in the last 5 years; certified disclosure statement.
- **Why it matters:** Undisclosed litigation discovered later is a deal-killer; proactive disclosure builds trust.
- **Typical form:** Litigation disclosure statement, signed PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.10 Bankruptcy history
- **What:** Any bankruptcy filings by principals, guarantors, or affiliated entities in the last 7 years; certified disclosure statement.
- **Why it matters:** Same logic as litigation; disclose and contextualize rather than suppress.
- **Typical form:** Bankruptcy disclosure statement, signed PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 05 — Sponsor Financial Strength

Balance sheet and liquidity evidence. Takeout lenders underwrite the guarantor even on non-recourse deals.

### 05.01 Personal financial statement (PFS)
- **What:** Current within 90 days; signed by each guarantor; broken out by cash, marketable securities, real estate (gross and net), other assets, and liabilities.
- **Why it matters:** Federal National Mortgage Association Form 1003 or institutional equivalent is preferred by most lenders; unsigned or stale statements are rejected.
- **Typical form:** Signed personal financial statement, PDF (FNMA Form 1003 or equivalent).
- **Where to find it:** Guarantor.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.02 Federal tax returns
- **What:** Most recent 2 years for individuals and all material operating entities; include all schedules.
- **Why it matters:** Tax returns are the only verifiable income evidence; a personal financial statement without returns is not accepted by institutional lenders.
- **Typical form:** Executed federal tax returns with all schedules, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Guarantor / sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.03 Liquidity statement
- **What:** Bank, brokerage, and money-market account statements within 30 days; highlights unrestricted liquidity.
- **Why it matters:** New lenders size guarantor liquidity against loan size; the common threshold is 10% of loan amount in liquid assets.
- **Typical form:** Bank and brokerage statements, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Guarantor.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.04 Real estate owned (REO) schedule
- **What:** Comprehensive schedule of all properties owned directly or through affiliates; includes address, property type, acquisition date, original cost, current value, current mortgage balance, annual net operating income, lender name, and maturity date.
- **Why it matters:** This is the most frequently requested and most frequently deficient item in lender packages.
- **Typical form:** Real estate owned schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Guarantor / sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.05 Contingent liability schedule
- **What:** Personal guaranties on third-party debt, pending litigation exposure, partnership obligations, and unfunded commitments.
- **Why it matters:** Without this schedule, the personal financial statement is incomplete; guarantors routinely under-disclose contingent liabilities.
- **Typical form:** Contingent liability schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Guarantor.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.06 Credit report authorization
- **What:** Signed authorization allowing the takeout lender to pull credit on guarantors.
- **Why it matters:** Some packages include the credit report itself (pulled by OKOA); others include only the authorization. Either format is acceptable; neither should be missing.
- **Typical form:** Signed credit authorization form, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Guarantor.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 05.07 Entity tax returns
- **What:** Two years of returns for the borrowing entity and any material co-borrower entities; K-1s included.
- **Why it matters:** Required by bank lenders and some debt funds; life companies may waive for strong borrowers.
- **Typical form:** Executed entity tax returns with K-1s, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor / borrower's accountant.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.08 Schedule of borrower's existing loans
- **What:** Loan-by-loan detail: lender, current balance, maturity date, payment status, and recourse.
- **Why it matters:** Cross-referenced against the real estate owned schedule to confirm completeness and detect hidden obligations.
- **Typical form:** Loan schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor / guarantor.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.09 Source and use of equity
- **What:** How the sponsor funds the equity in this deal — cash, recycled proceeds from prior sales, preferred equity, or other sources.
- **Why it matters:** Particularly important for value-add sponsors who rely on equity recycling; lenders want to understand the true equity basis.
- **Typical form:** Source and use memo or exhibit, PDF or Excel.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 05.10 Sponsor entity organizational chart with tax ID numbers
- **What:** Full organizational chart for all sponsor entities with federal tax identification numbers for each.
- **Why it matters:** Procedural but frequently requested by compliance teams to run entity-level searches.
- **Typical form:** Organizational chart with tax ID annotations, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor or sponsor's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 06 — Property / Collateral Profile

The physical and locational story of the asset.

### 06.01 Property summary sheet
- **What:** Address, county, parcel number(s), asset type (multifamily, office, retail, industrial, hotel, mixed-use), year built, year of last major renovation, total units or square feet, and lot size.
- **Why it matters:** The foundation of every underwriting model; provides the baseline physical facts the lender's analysts will use.
- **Typical form:** One-page property summary, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.02 Current physical condition narrative
- **What:** One-page description of current condition by asset category: exterior, roof, heating/ventilation/air conditioning, plumbing, electrical, common areas, and unit interiors for multifamily.
- **Why it matters:** If no recent property condition report is available, the sponsor or property manager certifies condition; lenders need a current baseline.
- **Typical form:** Condition narrative memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager or sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.03 Property photos
- **What:** Exterior (minimum 4 angles), interior (representative units or suites, common areas, amenities), site plan or floor plan photos.
- **Why it matters:** Photos must be dated; undated photos are treated as stale and raise credibility questions.
- **Typical form:** Dated photo file, PDF or zipped image folder.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager or OKOA's site visit records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.04 Aerial and street-level imagery
- **What:** Google Maps or satellite screenshot showing the site and immediate surroundings; walkability context.
- **Why it matters:** Institutional lenders review aerials before site visits; it contextualizes the neighborhood without requiring travel.
- **Typical form:** Aerial and street-view screenshots, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Google Maps, CoStar, or similar mapping tools.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.05 Site plan / floor plans
- **What:** Current as-built or current layout showing unit mix, common areas, and parking.
- **Why it matters:** Required for multifamily (unit mix), retail (tenant demising), and industrial (clear heights and dock doors).
- **Typical form:** Architectural drawings or current floor plans, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; architect's records; county building department.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.06 Parking summary
- **What:** Number of spaces, ratio per unit or per 1,000 square feet, and any shared parking agreements or parking covenants.
- **Why it matters:** Particularly important for urban retail and office; agencies have specific parking ratio minimums for multifamily.
- **Typical form:** Parking summary table or memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; site plan.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 06.07 Unit mix schedule (multifamily) or suite schedule (office/retail)
- **What:** Unit or suite type, count, square footage, current rent, and market rent.
- **Why it matters:** This is the rent roll equivalent for the physical asset description; required for any income property underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Unit mix or suite schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.08 Amenity description
- **What:** List of all amenities with condition notes — pool, gym, clubhouse, business center, electric vehicle charging, and others.
- **Why it matters:** Amenity quality is a rent-premium driver; institutional lenders factor it into market rent assumptions.
- **Typical form:** Amenity list or narrative, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 06.09 Zoning confirmation
- **What:** Current zoning classification, permitted uses, any non-conforming use status, and zoning certificate if available.
- **Why it matters:** Non-conforming use or zoning violations are title and insurance issues that can block closing.
- **Typical form:** Zoning letter or certificate from municipality; or third-party zoning report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Municipality or third-party zoning report provider.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.10 Location narrative
- **What:** Neighborhood description, proximity to employment centers, transit, retail, and comparable properties; one to two paragraphs.
- **Why it matters:** The narrative should make the submarket legible to a lender who may not know the market firsthand.
- **Typical form:** Location narrative section in pitch memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or market research.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.11 Environmental status summary
- **What:** Phase I Environmental Site Assessment date and conclusion, any Phase II history, and American Society for Testing and Materials compliance certification.
- **Why it matters:** If the Phase I is older than 18 months, note that an update is available or in progress; stale environmental reports delay closing.
- **Typical form:** Environmental status memo or Phase I report cover page, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; environmental consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.12 Flood zone status
- **What:** Federal Emergency Management Agency Flood Insurance Rate Map panel number, flood zone designation, and whether flood insurance is required and in force.
- **Why it matters:** Agencies (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac) have strict flood zone eligibility rules; non-compliance can disqualify agency execution.
- **Typical form:** Flood zone determination certificate, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; flood determination service.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 07 — Property Financial Performance

The operating record of the asset — the most scrutinized section in any lender package.

### 07.01 Trailing 12-month (T-12) income statement with monthly detail
- **What:** Gross potential rent, loss-to-lease, vacancy, concessions, other income, effective gross income, operating expenses by line item, and net operating income — presented with monthly columns.
- **Why it matters:** Monthly detail is non-negotiable; a lump-sum trailing 12-month is not sufficient for institutional underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Trailing 12-month operating statement, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.02 Trailing 3-month (T-3) income statement
- **What:** Same format as the trailing 12-month; provides the most recent operating trend.
- **Why it matters:** The trailing 3-month is annualized by many lenders to check for acceleration or deceleration in performance.
- **Typical form:** Trailing 3-month operating statement, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.03 Year-to-date income statement
- **What:** Calendar or fiscal year-to-date income statement through the most recent full month.
- **Why it matters:** Shows current-year trajectory versus the prior trailing 12-month and supports trend analysis.
- **Typical form:** Year-to-date operating statement, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.04 Prior 2 years of annual operating statements
- **What:** Full-year profit and loss for each of the 2 calendar or fiscal years preceding the trailing 12-month period.
- **Why it matters:** Allows a three-year trend analysis; agencies require 2 years of historical statements for most execution types.
- **Typical form:** Annual operating statements, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.05 Current rent roll
- **What:** All occupied and vacant units or suites; includes tenant name (or unit number for multifamily), lease start, lease expiration, contracted rent, market rent, unit size, and any concessions in place.
- **Why it matters:** Must be dated within 30 days of package delivery; rent rolls older than 60 days are disqualified by most institutional lenders.
- **Typical form:** Rent roll, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.06 Lease maturity / rollover schedule
- **What:** Graphical and tabular view of lease expirations by quarter or year for the next 5 years, expressed in units and square footage.
- **Why it matters:** Shows the new lender the lease-up risk profile; near-term rollover is the number one refinance risk for office and retail assets.
- **Typical form:** Rollover schedule chart and table, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.07 Occupancy history
- **What:** Monthly occupied units or occupied square footage as a percentage of total, for the last 24 months.
- **Why it matters:** The trend line is as important as current occupancy; lenders look for stability or upward trajectory.
- **Typical form:** Occupancy history chart or table, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.08 Concession schedule
- **What:** Current and recently expired concessions by unit type or tenant — months of free rent, rent abatements, and landlord work letters.
- **Why it matters:** Net effective rent versus face rent distinction is critical for lenders sizing debt against actual cash flow.
- **Typical form:** Concession schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.09 NOI bridge
- **What:** Reconciliation between as-underwritten net operating income (at origination) and current trailing 12-month net operating income; explains variances line by line.
- **Why it matters:** The net operating income bridge is the first thing a sophisticated lender reads to understand whether the deal performed as projected.
- **Typical form:** Net operating income bridge table, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's asset management records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.10 Expense ratio analysis
- **What:** Operating expenses as a percentage of effective gross income, compared to prior year and to market benchmarks.
- **Why it matters:** Useful for identifying whether expenses are being suppressed — a common practice in packages from borrowers trying to inflate net operating income.
- **Typical form:** Expense ratio analysis exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 07.11 Management fee verification
- **What:** Actual management fee charged versus market management fee; any self-management discount normalized.
- **Why it matters:** Lenders underwrite to market management fees regardless of actual; a normalization note must appear in the package.
- **Typical form:** Management fee normalization memo or underwriting exhibit, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file; property management agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.12 Utility expense detail
- **What:** Actual versus budgeted utility costs; any master-metered versus sub-metered breakdown.
- **Why it matters:** Utility expense is one of the most volatile operating line items and is frequently understated in lender packages.
- **Typical form:** Utility expense detail schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; utility bills.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 07.13 Real estate tax bills
- **What:** Most recent tax bills showing assessed value, annual tax obligation, and payment status.
- **Why it matters:** Tax expense is a critical underwriting input; assessed value reassessment risk must be disclosed and modeled.
- **Typical form:** Tax bills, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; county assessor's records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.14 Insurance expense
- **What:** Current annual premium, coverage type, and policy period.
- **Why it matters:** Insurance costs have spiked in many markets; lenders want to see normalized insurance expense, not a lucky one-year rate.
- **Typical form:** Insurance declarations page or expense summary, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Insurance broker or property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 08 — Property Capital Stack & Pro Forma

The current financial architecture and the proposed future architecture.

### 08.01 Current capital stack waterfall
- **What:** All tranches of debt (senior, A-note, B-note, mezzanine, subordinate) and equity (preferred equity, general partner equity, limited partner equity); amount, rate, maturity, and position for each.
- **Why it matters:** The new lender must understand their proposed position relative to the full existing stack.
- **Typical form:** Capital stack waterfall diagram or table, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file.

### 08.02 Existing lien summary
- **What:** All recorded liens on the property, including OKOA's mortgage, any subordinate liens, mechanic's liens, or tax liens.
- **Why it matters:** Title search excerpt or lien schedule; the new lender must know exactly what will be paid off at closing.
- **Typical form:** Lien schedule or title search excerpt, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Title company; OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 08.03 Proposed takeout capital stack
- **What:** Proposed senior loan amount, proposed loan-to-value, proposed debt service coverage ratio, and any proposed mezzanine or preferred equity remaining.
- **Why it matters:** This is the ask; it must be internally consistent with the pro forma and with the sources and uses table.
- **Typical form:** Proposed capital stack table, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 08.04 Pro forma underwriting workbook
- **What:** Stabilized net operating income, gross potential rent, vacancy assumption, expense assumptions, cap rate, stabilized value, proposed loan amount, loan-to-value, debt service coverage ratio, and debt yield.

- **Typical form:** Underwriting workbook, Excel (with PDF export).
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 08.05 Pro forma rent assumptions
- **What:** Market rent by unit type or suite, rent growth assumption, and stabilization timeline.
- **Why it matters:** Rent assumptions must be supported by comparables in Category 09; unsupported rent assumptions are the most common source of underwriting disputes.
- **Typical form:** Rent assumption exhibit within the pro forma workbook, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.06 Pro forma vacancy and concession assumptions
- **What:** Stabilized vacancy rate, concession assumption, and economic occupancy.
- **Why it matters:** Must be benchmarked against submarket vacancy data; unsupported vacancy assumptions undermine the pro forma's credibility.
- **Typical form:** Vacancy and concession assumption exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.07 Pro forma expense assumptions
- **What:** Each operating expense line item with its basis — per unit, per square foot, percentage of effective gross income, or flat amount; management fee at market.
- **Why it matters:** Lenders will re-underwrite; clean, sourced assumptions reduce friction and reduce the risk of a retraded term sheet.
- **Typical form:** Expense assumption exhibit within the pro forma workbook, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.08 Exit cap rate assumption and valuation
- **What:** Stabilized net operating income divided by exit cap rate; cap rate assumption sourced to comparable sales data.
- **Why it matters:** For value-add loans, the exit cap rate is the most contested assumption in the model.
- **Typical form:** Valuation exhibit within the pro forma workbook, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.09 Refinance proceeds use of proceeds
- **What:** How takeout proceeds will be used: payoff of OKOA loan (confirming sufficient proceeds), return of equity, funded reserves, and closing costs.
- **Why it matters:** The use-of-proceeds table ensures no gap between the payoff amount and takeout proceeds.
- **Typical form:** Sources and uses table, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.10 Equity contribution history
- **What:** Original equity invested at origination, any additional equity contributed for capital expenditures or operating shortfalls, and total equity basis.
- **Why it matters:** Shows the sponsor's skin in the game and whether they have been funding shortfalls out of pocket.
- **Typical form:** Equity contribution schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file; sponsor's records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 08.11 Waterfall projection at takeout
- **What:** Modeled distribution of takeout proceeds to each tranche in order of priority.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms that no tranche is impaired at the proposed takeout amount and that the payoff is fully executable.
- **Typical form:** Waterfall projection exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 09 — Updated Valuation & Market Support

Evidence that the value and market assumptions in the pro forma are grounded in reality.

### 09.01 Broker opinion of value (BOV)
- **What:** Current within 6 months; prepared by a recognized local brokerage; includes comparable sales analysis and income approach.
- **Why it matters:** A broker opinion of value is not an appraisal but is accepted by most debt funds and banks for preliminary underwriting; life companies and agencies typically require a full appraisal.
- **Typical form:** Broker opinion of value report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Local commercial real estate brokerage.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 09.02 MAI appraisal
- **What:** If available, current within 12 months (agencies may require within 6 months); as-is value and as-stabilized value.
- **Why it matters:** Include even if stale; note staleness and provide a broker opinion of value update. Life companies and agencies require a full appraisal.
- **Typical form:** MAI appraisal report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; appraisal management company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 09.03 Sales comparables
- **What:** Last 12 months; minimum 5 comparables; includes address, sale date, asset type, size, price, price per unit or per square foot, and cap rate if available.
- **Why it matters:** Comparables must bracket the subject property; no cherry-picking premium assets if the subject is a B-quality asset.
- **Typical form:** Sales comparables schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** CoStar, local brokerages, or OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 09.04 Lease comparables
- **What:** Recent executed leases in the submarket; includes address, tenant, lease date, lease term, face rent, net effective rent, concessions, and tenant improvement allowance.
- **Why it matters:** For multifamily: comparable community rent survey (physical and economic rates); for office and retail: executed lease comparables. Essential to supporting rent assumptions.
- **Typical form:** Lease comparables schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** CoStar, local brokerages, or OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 09.05 Submarket vacancy and absorption report
- **What:** Current submarket vacancy rate, net absorption (trailing 4 quarters), asking rents versus effective rents, and supply pipeline.
- **Why it matters:** Must include supply pipeline; new supply is the most common reason a refinance fails underwriting at a institutional lender.
- **Typical form:** Submarket vacancy and absorption report, PDF (CoStar, CBRE, JLL, or equivalent).
- **Where to find it:** CoStar or commercial real estate research providers.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 09.06 Market report
- **What:** Third-party market report (CoStar, CBRE, JLL, Cushman and Wakefield, or equivalent) covering the metropolitan area and submarket; dated within 6 months.
- **Why it matters:** Third-party authorship is important; broker-authored internal reports are supplementary, not primary evidence.
- **Typical form:** Third-party market report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** CoStar, CBRE, JLL, Cushman and Wakefield, or equivalent providers.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 09.07 Submarket trajectory narrative
- **What:** One-page qualitative analysis of submarket demand drivers, employment base, and 24-month outlook.
- **Why it matters:** The narrative should make a specific case for this submarket, not a generic market overview.
- **Typical form:** Narrative memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's market research; local commercial real estate brokerages.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 09.08 Supply pipeline analysis
- **What:** Map and schedule of proposed, under-construction, and recently delivered competitive supply within the trade area.
- **Why it matters:** A visual map is more persuasive than a table alone; shows the geographic concentration of competition relative to the subject property.
- **Typical form:** Supply pipeline map and schedule, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** CoStar or local commercial real estate research.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 09.09 Competitive set analysis
- **What:** Three to five most directly competitive properties; compare age, quality, occupancy, rents, and amenities versus the subject.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates where the subject sits in the competitive hierarchy; supports the market rent assumptions in the pro forma.
- **Typical form:** Competitive set analysis table or exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** CoStar; local brokerages; OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 09.10 Macro market indicators
- **What:** Employment growth, population growth, household formation, and income growth for the Metropolitan Statistical Area over the prior 3 years and a 3-year projection.
- **Why it matters:** Useful for life companies and agencies who underwrite market fundamentals as rigorously as property-level data.
- **Typical form:** Macro market indicator summary, PDF (CoStar, CBRE Economics, or equivalent).
- **Where to find it:** CoStar, CBRE, Bureau of Labor Statistics, or equivalent sources.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 09.11 Cap rate trend data
- **What:** Historical cap rate movement in the submarket for the asset type; sourced to CoStar, Real Capital Analytics, or equivalent.
- **Why it matters:** Supports the exit cap rate assumption in the pro forma; without it, the cap rate assumption is unsupported.
- **Typical form:** Cap rate trend chart or data exhibit, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** CoStar, Real Capital Analytics, CBRE, or equivalent providers.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 09.12 Highest and best use analysis
- **What:** Brief statement confirming that the current use represents highest and best use; relevant for mixed-use or redevelopment-adjacent assets.
- **Why it matters:** Required if the broker opinion of value or appraisal flags an alternative use scenario; otherwise a brief certification suffices.
- **Typical form:** Highest and best use memo or appraisal exhibit, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Appraisal; or OKOA's underwriting file for a brief certification.
- **Severity if absent:** low

- **Typical form:** Draw schedule or invoice summary, Excel or PDF.

### 10.03 Before/after photos
- **What:** Representative before-and-after photos for renovated units, common areas, or exterior improvements.

### 10.04 Renovation completion certificate or property manager sign-off
- **What:** Confirmation from a qualified third party that the renovated scope is complete.

### 10.07 Deferred maintenance schedule

- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan servicing records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 10.09 Green / sustainability capital

- **Typical form:** Green capital summary memo, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; utility records; sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** low

## Category 11 — Refinance Thesis & Takeout Source

The heart of the package. This section must convince the new lender that a specific, credible takeout path exists.

### 11.01 Refinance thesis document
- **What:** One to two pages articulating why the property is refinanceable, why now, and what the new lender receives in return — yield, credit quality, and collateral position.
- **Why it matters:** The thesis must directly address the new lender's underwriting criteria; a generic thesis fails.
- **Typical form:** Narrative memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 11.02 Takeout source identification
- **What:** Named prospective lender(s), type (bank, life company, agency, debt fund), and status (hard circle, soft circle, indicative terms received, or outreach only).
- **Why it matters:** The strength of takeout source identification is the primary variable in a lender package's persuasive power.
- **Typical form:** Takeout source identification memo or deal summary section, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file; lender relationship contacts.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 11.03 Takeout commitment letter or term sheet
- **What:** Signed commitment or signed term sheet from the proposed takeout lender, if available.
- **Why it matters:** A hard commitment transforms the package from a pitch into a near-certainty; even a non-binding term sheet substantially de-risks the ask.
- **Typical form:** Signed commitment letter or term sheet, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Takeout lender.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 11.04 Soft circle documentation
- **What:** Emails, meeting notes, or letter of intent from prospective takeout lenders expressing interest without commitment.
- **Why it matters:** Document every meaningful conversation; a documented soft circle is far more credible than an oral representation.
- **Typical form:** Email excerpts or meeting summary memos, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 11.05 Alternative takeout paths
- **What:** If the primary takeout source is soft, a list of two to three alternative lenders with rationale for why each is a viable takeout candidate.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that the refinance does not depend on a single lender relationship.
- **Typical form:** Alternative takeout paths memo or table, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file; lender relationship contacts.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 11.06 Bridge-to-permanent timeline
- **What:** Month-by-month schedule from today through takeout close; includes business plan milestones, refinance application date, and expected close date.
- **Why it matters:** The timeline must be credible; milestones that require extraordinary performance are red flags.
- **Typical form:** Timeline schedule or Gantt chart, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file; sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 11.07 Agency eligibility analysis (if applicable)
- **What:** For multifamily: Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac / Department of Housing and Urban Development eligibility checklist including occupancy, debt service coverage ratio, loan-to-value, property condition, and borrower eligibility.
- **Why it matters:** Agency executions are the highest-quality takeout for multifamily; an eligibility analysis is persuasive even if the sponsor has not yet applied.
- **Typical form:** Agency eligibility checklist or memo, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file; Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac program guides.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 11.08 CMBS eligibility analysis (if applicable)
- **What:** For office, retail, industrial, and hotel: Commercial Mortgage-Backed Securities underwriting criteria analysis (loan-to-value, debt service coverage ratio, debt yield, property type, market tier).
- **Why it matters:** Commercial Mortgage-Backed Securities execution is a standard takeout for stabilized assets; an eligibility analysis reduces the new lender's diligence burden.
- **Typical form:** CMBS eligibility checklist or memo, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file; CMBS conduit lender term sheets.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 11.09 Takeout lender selection rationale
- **What:** Why this specific lender type is the optimal takeout — yield requirements, property type preference, market presence, and relationship.
- **Why it matters:** Persuasive for packages where the primary takeout is a non-obvious choice.
- **Typical form:** Lender selection rationale section in the pitch memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 11.10 Extension option analysis
- **What:** If the primary takeout is delayed, which extension options remain, at what cost, and whether the business plan supports an extended hold.
- **Why it matters:** Back-stop narrative in case the refinance timeline slips; demonstrates that the deal does not collapse if the takeout is delayed by 6–12 months.
- **Typical form:** Extension option analysis exhibit, PDF or Excel.
- **Where to find it:** Loan documents; OKOA's deal file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 11.11 Borrower refinance readiness certification
- **What:** Signed sponsor statement that the borrower intends to refinance, has engaged a broker or lender, and has no material objections to the process.
- **Why it matters:** Useful for packages where borrower cooperation is a concern; signals that both parties are aligned on the path forward.
- **Typical form:** Signed certification letter, PDF on sponsor letterhead.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor.
- **Severity if absent:** low

## Category 12 — Proposed Terms

The specific ask. This section frames how the new lender should think about structuring the refinance.

### 12.01 Proposed loan amount
- **What:** Stated in dollars with loan-to-value, loan-to-cost (if applicable), and basis for the request (appraisal, broker opinion of value, or pro forma net operating income).
- **Why it matters:** The ask must be explicitly stated; no new lender will derive the ask from the pro forma on their own.
- **Typical form:** Proposed terms summary table, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 12.02 Proposed loan-to-value (LTV)
- **What:** Proposed loan amount divided by as-is or as-stabilized value; compared to the target lender type's typical loan-to-value thresholds.
- **Why it matters:** Benchmarking against the target lender's typical underwriting parameters demonstrates that the ask is market-calibrated.
- **Typical form:** Proposed terms summary table, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 12.03 Proposed debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
- **What:** Stabilized net operating income divided by proposed annual debt service; compared to the target lender's typical debt service coverage ratio floors.
- **Why it matters:** Must be calculated at the proposed rate (or stressed rate for floating-rate proposals).
- **Typical form:** Proposed terms summary table with calculation exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 12.04 Proposed debt yield
- **What:** Stabilized net operating income divided by proposed loan amount.
- **Why it matters:** Increasingly the primary sizing metric; should be benchmarked against market debt yield floors by lender type.
- **Typical form:** Proposed terms summary table, PDF or Excel.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 12.05 Proposed rate type
- **What:** Fixed rate, floating rate (index plus spread), or hybrid; if floating, proposed cap rate and cap structure.
- **Why it matters:** Rate type affects debt service coverage ratio; floating-rate proposals should show debt service coverage ratio at the cap rate.
- **Typical form:** Rate term exhibit within the proposed terms section, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 12.06 Proposed term and amortization
- **What:** Loan term in months or years, and amortization schedule (full interest-only, partial interest-only, or 30-year amortizing).
- **Why it matters:** Interest-only period is a key negotiating variable; include it explicitly in the ask.
- **Typical form:** Term and amortization exhibit within the proposed terms section, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 12.07 Proposed prepayment provisions
- **What:** Yield maintenance, step-down, or open period; if the takeout is to a lender who will sell into Commercial Mortgage-Backed Securities or a pool, prepayment flexibility matters.
- **Why it matters:** Prepayment terms affect the borrower's exit flexibility and are a key negotiating point.
- **Typical form:** Prepayment terms exhibit within the proposed terms section, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 12.08 Proposed recourse structure
- **What:** Full recourse, partial recourse, or non-recourse with carveouts; carveout guarantor identified.
- **Why it matters:** Must be consistent with the target lender type's typical recourse requirements.
- **Typical form:** Recourse terms exhibit within the proposed terms section, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 12.09 Proposed reserves and covenants
- **What:** Replacement reserve requirement, operating reserve requirement, debt service coverage ratio covenant level, and occupancy covenant level.
- **Why it matters:** Proposed covenant levels should be calibrated to the property's demonstrated performance; overly aggressive covenants will be rejected.
- **Typical form:** Reserve and covenant terms exhibit, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 12.10 Proposed closing timeline
- **What:** From term sheet to closing; includes appraisal lead time, title update, and loan document drafting.
- **Why it matters:** A credible timeline demonstrates that the parties have thought through the process and that the proposed close date is achievable.
- **Typical form:** Closing timeline schedule, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 12.11 Fee summary
- **What:** Origination fee, application fee, exit fee (if applicable), and broker fee (if a placement agent is involved).
- **Why it matters:** Transparency on fees builds credibility; buried fees discovered later damage trust and can create legal disputes.
- **Typical form:** Fee summary table, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or pitch package.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 12.12 Sources and uses at close
- **What:** Every dollar in (takeout proceeds, additional equity) and every dollar out (OKOA payoff, closing costs, reserves funded, equity return).
- **Why it matters:** The sources and uses table is the financial proof that the refinance is executable; it must balance to the dollar.
- **Typical form:** Sources and uses table, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file or underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

## Category 13 — Risk Mitigants & Stress Tests

Every takeout lender runs downside scenarios. Present the stress tests first, before they ask.

### 13.01 Sensitivity analysis table
- **What:** Debt service coverage ratio and loan-to-value sensitivity to changes in occupancy (plus or minus 5% and plus or minus 10%) and net operating income (plus or minus 5%, plus or minus 10%, and plus or minus 15%).
- **Why it matters:** Takeout lenders almost always ask for sensitivity tables; presenting them proactively is a credibility signal and reduces back-and-forth.
- **Typical form:** Sensitivity analysis table, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 13.02 Breakeven occupancy
- **What:** The occupancy rate at which net operating income covers proposed debt service; stated as a percentage.
- **Why it matters:** A low breakeven occupancy is the most concise way to demonstrate debt safety in a single number.
- **Typical form:** Breakeven occupancy calculation exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 13.03 Breakeven rent
- **What:** The average rent per unit or per square foot at which net operating income covers proposed debt service; compared to current rents and submarket floor rents.
- **Why it matters:** Particularly important for value-add multifamily where rent premiums drive the refinance case.
- **Typical form:** Breakeven rent calculation exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 13.04 Downside scenario analysis
- **What:** A named downside scenario (for example, "Recession Case" or "Lease-Up Delay") with modeled assumptions and resulting debt service coverage ratio, loan-to-value, and debt yield.
- **Why it matters:** One well-constructed downside scenario is more credible than multiple optimistic base cases.
- **Typical form:** Downside scenario model exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 13.05 Key risk identification
- **What:** Three to five identified risks specific to this deal with a plain-language description of each.
- **Why it matters:** Proactive risk identification is a sign of sophisticated sponsorship; lenders trust packages that acknowledge risk.
- **Typical form:** Risk identification section in the pitch memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 13.06 Risk mitigant narrative
- **What:** For each identified risk, a specific, evidence-based mitigant — not generic reassurances.
- **Why it matters:** The mitigant must be concrete: "replacement reserve fully funded at $X per unit" — not "reserves are adequate."
- **Typical form:** Risk mitigant section in the pitch memo, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's deal file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 13.07 Interest rate stress test
- **What:** If a floating rate is proposed: debt service coverage ratio at the current rate, at the cap rate, and at the rate cap strike. If a fixed rate is proposed: comparison to stressed rate assumptions.
- **Why it matters:** Rate risk is the most topical stress test in a high-rate environment and is asked by virtually every institutional lender.
- **Typical form:** Interest rate stress test exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 13.08 Lease-up stress test (if applicable)
- **What:** Modeled time-to-stabilization under base, upside, and downside lease-up velocity; debt service coverage ratio at each scenario.
- **Why it matters:** Required for any lease-up or value-add asset; not needed for fully stabilized properties.
- **Typical form:** Lease-up stress test exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 13.09 Tenant concentration risk analysis
- **What:** For commercial properties: top five tenants by revenue share, lease term, and credit quality. For multifamily: geographic or income concentration.
- **Why it matters:** Concentration risk is a standard underwriting ask for office and retail; it is also increasingly asked for multifamily in high-income or student-housing markets.
- **Typical form:** Tenant concentration analysis exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Rent roll; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 13.10 Environmental risk narrative
- **What:** If the Phase I Environmental Site Assessment identified any recognized environmental conditions, provide a status update and remediation plan.
- **Why it matters:** Recognized environmental conditions are not necessarily deal-killers; disclose and document the remediation status.
- **Typical form:** Environmental risk narrative memo, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; environmental consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 13.11 Liquidity stress
- **What:** Borrower liquidity at or after takeout close; confirms the borrower is not immediately illiquid post-refinance.
- **Why it matters:** Some lenders require a minimum post-close liquidity covenant; demonstrating adequate post-close liquidity proactively removes a common condition.
- **Typical form:** Post-close liquidity analysis exhibit, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Sponsor's personal financial statement; liquidity statement.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 14 — Third-Party Reports

Independent expert evidence. The package should include all reports generated during OKOA's origination due diligence and any updates commissioned since.

### 14.01 Appraisal
- **What:** Most recent MAI appraisal; includes as-is and as-stabilized values; note date; flag if stale (older than 12 months) and whether an update is available.
- **Why it matters:** Required by life companies and agencies; debt funds and banks may accept a broker opinion of value for preliminary underwriting.
- **Typical form:** MAI appraisal report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; appraisal management company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.02 Phase I Environmental Site Assessment
- **What:** Current American Society for Testing and Materials E1527-21 Phase I Environmental Site Assessment; note preparer, date, and conclusion (no recognized environmental conditions, recognized environmental condition, controlled recognized environmental condition, historical recognized environmental condition, or business environmental risk).
- **Why it matters:** A stale Phase I (older than 18 months) must be noted; many lenders require a new Phase I or an update letter.
- **Typical form:** Phase I Environmental Site Assessment report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; environmental consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 14.03 Property Condition Report / Physical Needs Assessment
- **What:** Most recent property condition report or physical needs assessment; note preparer, date, and immediate repairs identified; replacement reserve adequacy conclusion.
- **Why it matters:** Required by agencies and most institutional lenders; include even if stale — note the date and any known changes in condition since the report.
- **Typical form:** Property condition report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; property condition report consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.04 Survey
- **What:** Most recent American Land Title Association / National Society of Professional Surveyors survey; note preparer and date; flag any exceptions (encroachments, easements, boundary disputes).
- **Why it matters:** An American Land Title Association survey is required for most institutional closings; including it saves the new lender's diligence timeline.
- **Typical form:** American Land Title Association / National Society of Professional Surveyors survey, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; surveying firm.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 14.05 Zoning report or letter
- **What:** Confirmation of current zoning classification, permitted uses, parking compliance, and non-conforming status if applicable.
- **Why it matters:** Particularly important for mixed-use and historically non-conforming assets; zoning problems can derail a closing.
- **Typical form:** Zoning report or zoning letter from municipality, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; third-party zoning report provider or municipality.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 14.06 Market study
- **What:** Third-party commissioned market study, if available; note preparer, date, and demand conclusions.
- **Why it matters:** Commissioned market studies are required by the Department of Housing and Urban Development and some life companies; include if available.
- **Typical form:** Third-party market study report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; market research firms.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 14.07 Seismic / PML report (if applicable)
- **What:** For assets in seismic zones: Probable Maximum Loss percentage; note whether the lender's Probable Maximum Loss threshold is met.
- **Why it matters:** Required by many institutional lenders for assets in American Society of Civil Engineers Seismic Design Categories C, D, E, or F.
- **Typical form:** Probable Maximum Loss report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; seismic engineering firm.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 14.08 ADA compliance report or certification
- **What:** Current Americans with Disabilities Act accessibility compliance status; any known Americans with Disabilities Act exposure.
- **Why it matters:** Increasingly requested by life companies and agencies; Americans with Disabilities Act exposure can affect insurance and valuation.
- **Typical form:** Americans with Disabilities Act compliance report or certification letter, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's origination diligence file; accessibility consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 14.09 Elevator inspection certificate (if applicable)
- **What:** Current certificate of inspection for all elevators; compliance with local authority having jurisdiction.
- **Why it matters:** Required by certain state and local jurisdictions as a condition to insurance or certificate of occupancy renewal.
- **Typical form:** Elevator inspection certificate, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; local authority having jurisdiction.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 14.10 Roof inspection report (if applicable)
- **What:** For assets with roof replacement or repair in the capital expenditure history: an independent inspection confirming current condition and remaining useful life.
- **Why it matters:** Useful to validate capital expenditure claims and confirm that deferred maintenance on the roof has been addressed.
- **Typical form:** Roof inspection report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Roofing contractor or property condition report consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 15 — Title & Insurance

Clean title and in-force insurance are preconditions to closing. Present them proactively.

### 15.01 Current title commitment or title report
- **What:** Date-stamped within 90 days; issued by a nationally recognized title insurer; shows vested owner, legal description, and all exceptions.
- **Why it matters:** The new lender will require a new title policy at closing, but an interim title report confirms no new liens or cloud on title since origination.
- **Typical form:** Title commitment or title report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Title company.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.02 Existing title insurance policy
- **What:** OKOA's lender title policy at origination; shows coverage amount, effective date, and endorsements.
- **Why it matters:** The new lender will issue their own policy; the existing policy is provided for reference and as evidence of clean title at origination.
- **Typical form:** Lender title insurance policy, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.03 Lien position confirmation
- **What:** Confirmation of OKOA's lien position (first, second, etc.); subordination and non-disturbance agreements if applicable.
- **Why it matters:** The new lender must confirm that OKOA's position will be paid off at closing and that no subordinate liens survive the payoff.
- **Typical form:** Lien position confirmation letter or title endorsement, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Title company; OKOA's loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.04 Mechanic's lien search
- **What:** Current search (within 30 days) showing no unresolved mechanic's liens; if liens are present, status of each.
- **Why it matters:** Active capital expenditure projects create mechanic's lien exposure; the search must be current to be meaningful.
- **Typical form:** Mechanic's lien search report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Title company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.05 Current property and casualty insurance binder
- **What:** In-force policy declarations page; shows coverage amounts (replacement cost and business interruption), carrier, policy period, and named insured.
- **Why it matters:** The binder must show OKOA (or a successor lender) as mortgagee and additional insured.
- **Typical form:** Insurance declarations page or binder, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Insurance broker or property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.06 Flood insurance policy (if applicable)
- **What:** In-force National Flood Insurance Program or private flood policy if the property is in a Special Flood Hazard Area.
- **Why it matters:** If the property is in a Special Flood Hazard Area and flood insurance is not in force, the deal cannot close with an institutional lender.
- **Typical form:** Flood insurance policy declarations page, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Insurance broker or property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.07 Liability insurance certificate
- **What:** General liability coverage, coverage limits, and carrier.
- **Why it matters:** Minimum coverage amounts are set by most institutional lenders — $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate is common.
- **Typical form:** Certificate of liability insurance (ACORD form), PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Insurance broker or property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 15.08 Umbrella / excess liability policy (if applicable)
- **What:** Additional coverage above the general liability policy.
- **Why it matters:** Lenders with high loan amounts often require umbrella coverage; show in force if applicable.
- **Typical form:** Umbrella policy declarations page, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Insurance broker or property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 15.09 Insurance loss history
- **What:** Any insurance claims in the last 5 years; amount claimed, amount paid, and resolution.
- **Why it matters:** Material loss history (fire, water, wind) must be disclosed and paired with repair documentation.
- **Typical form:** Loss run report from insurer, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Insurance broker.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 15.10 UCC search
- **What:** Search of Uniform Commercial Code financing statements filed against the borrowing entity and the property.
- **Why it matters:** Particularly important for properties with equipment financing or mezzanine debt; confirms no undisclosed security interests.
- **Typical form:** Uniform Commercial Code search report, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Secretary of State's office; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 16 — Operations & Management

Operational competence is a credit factor, not just an asset management detail.

### 16.01 Property management agreement
- **What:** Current agreement; parties, term, management fee, scope of services, and termination rights.
- **Why it matters:** The new lender will require a subordination and assignment of the management agreement at closing.
- **Typical form:** Executed property management agreement, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.02 Property manager profile
- **What:** Company background, years in operation, assets under management, and references in this submarket.
- **Why it matters:** Self-managed properties require a principal's operating resume in lieu of a management company profile.
- **Typical form:** Company profile or capabilities statement, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.03 Leasing agreement (commercial only)
- **What:** Exclusive leasing agent agreement for office, retail, or industrial assets; parties, term, and commission schedule.
- **Why it matters:** Active leasing representation is a value-add narrative component; it confirms the sponsor is investing in lease-up.
- **Typical form:** Executed leasing agreement, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA's loan file; leasing broker.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.04 On-site staffing plan
- **What:** Number of on-site staff, roles, tenure, and any staffing changes in the last 12 months.
- **Why it matters:** High staff turnover is an early warning sign for multifamily operational quality.
- **Typical form:** Staffing plan summary, PDF or DOCX.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 16.05 Major vendor contracts
- **What:** Landscaping, waste removal, heating/ventilation/air conditioning service, elevator maintenance, and pest control contracts; term and annual cost.
- **Why it matters:** Long-term vendor contracts at favorable rates are a positive operating narrative; above-market contracts are a liability.
- **Typical form:** Vendor contract summary schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 16.06 Utility provider agreements
- **What:** Any master utility agreements, bulk electricity or gas contracts, or Ratio Utility Billing System programs.
- **Why it matters:** Ratio Utility Billing System programs pass utility costs to tenants and improve net operating income stability; relevant for agency underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Utility agreement summary or contract copies, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 16.07 Technology and amenity service agreements
- **What:** Cable or fiber bulk agreements, smart home technology contracts, and electric vehicle charging agreements.
- **Why it matters:** Relevant for newer multifamily assets where amenity income is a material net operating income component.
- **Typical form:** Technology and amenity agreement summary, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 16.08 Operating budget for current year
- **What:** Line-item budget versus trailing 12-month actuals; variance explanation for items that exceed budget by more than 10%.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates financial management discipline and provides a forward net operating income reference point for the new lender.
- **Typical form:** Operating budget versus actuals schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 17 — Tax & Regulatory

Tax and regulatory compliance problems can derail a refinance at commitment or closing.

### 17.01 Current real estate tax bills
- **What:** Most recent tax bills for all parcels; paid-up status confirmed; due dates for the next installment.
- **Why it matters:** Delinquent taxes are a lien that subordinates to nothing; taxes must be current at closing.
- **Typical form:** Tax bills, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** County assessor's or tax collector's office; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 17.02 Tax payment receipt
- **What:** Proof of payment for the most recently due tax installment.
- **Why it matters:** Institutional lenders require proof of payment, not just a statement that taxes are current.
- **Typical form:** Tax payment receipt or cancelled check, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; county tax collector.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 17.03 Tax assessment history
- **What:** Assessed value for the last 3 years; any recent reassessment notice; any active appeal.
- **Why it matters:** Reassessment risk is a material expense risk; an active appeal can be positive (reducing taxes) or signal historical overpayment.
- **Typical form:** Tax assessment history schedule, Excel or PDF; or county assessor records.
- **Where to find it:** County assessor's office; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 17.04 Special assessment schedule
- **What:** Any special assessment districts, municipal benefit assessments, or community development district assessments; remaining balance and annual payment.
- **Why it matters:** Special assessments are liens that survive a sale or refinance; they must be disclosed and modeled into the expense pro forma.
- **Typical form:** Special assessment schedule, Excel or PDF.
- **Where to find it:** County or municipal records; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 17.05 Business license and certificate of occupancy
- **What:** Current business license (where required) and certificate of occupancy for all structures on the property.
- **Why it matters:** A property operating without a certificate of occupancy is an uninsurable and unlendable risk; it can block closing.
- **Typical form:** Business license and certificate of occupancy, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Local municipality or building department; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 17.06 Regulatory agreement compliance (if applicable)
- **What:** Any affordability restrictions, income restrictions (Low Income Housing Tax Credit, Section 8 Housing Assistance Payments contract, or inclusionary zoning), and compliance status.
- **Why it matters:** Regulatory agreements restrict rents and can restrict refinancing; full disclosure is mandatory and non-disclosure is a fraud risk.
- **Typical form:** Regulatory agreement and compliance certification, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower's counsel; state housing finance agency.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 17.07 Historic tax credit compliance (if applicable)
- **What:** If the property received historic tax credits, the compliance period status and recapture risk.
- **Why it matters:** Historic tax credit recapture is a contingent liability that must be disclosed; it can affect the structure of the refinance.
- **Typical form:** Historic tax credit compliance certification or counsel memo, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower's counsel; historic tax credit syndicator.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 17.08 Transfer tax analysis
- **What:** Estimated transfer taxes due at takeout closing (if any); note whether the proposed structure triggers transfer tax.
- **Why it matters:** Transfer taxes are a closing cost variable; some refinance structures are designed to minimize transfer tax exposure.
- **Typical form:** Transfer tax analysis memo, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower's counsel; closing attorney.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 17.09 Homeowners association or condominium association (if applicable)
- **What:** Association name, current dues, dues payment status, reserve fund balance, and any pending special assessments.
- **Why it matters:** For condominiumized or homeowners-association-governed assets; homeowners association delinquency is an impediment to refinancing.
- **Typical form:** Homeowners association certificate or dues statement, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Homeowners association or condominium association management.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 17.10 Pending code violations or enforcement actions
- **What:** Any open building department, fire marshal, or health department violations; status and cure plan.
- **Why it matters:** Open violations must be disclosed; cure plans with timelines are acceptable, but undisclosed violations discovered at closing are deal-killers.
- **Typical form:** Code violation disclosure memo with cure plan, PDF.
- **Where to find it:** Local building department; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high
