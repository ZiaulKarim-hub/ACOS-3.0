# Base Checklist — Loan Participation

> **Scope:** Outbound diligence for selling a participation interest in an existing loan.
> **Role:** Prior — applied via Phase 7a tailoring to produce per-deal scope.
> **Default action:** Every item is `in_scope` until tailoring marks it otherwise.

---

## Category 01 — Loan Documents & Enforceability Chain

### 01.01 Original Promissory Note (Executed)
- **What:** The fully executed, original promissory note evidencing the debt obligation.
- **Why it matters:** A participant is acquiring an interest in the note; enforceability begins here. A copy without origination confirms no party holds the wet-ink original.
- **Typical form:** Original instrument or certified copy; allonge chain if endorsed.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / custodian / lead lender's vault.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.02 Allonge(s) and Endorsement Chain
- **What:** All endorsements transferring the note from originator through any intermediate holders to OKOA.
- **Why it matters:** An unbroken endorsement chain is required to establish OKOA's status as a holder in due course and its right to transfer a participation interest.
- **Typical form:** Endorsement stamped on note or attached allonge(s).
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / note custodian.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.03 Loan Agreement / Credit Agreement (with all amendments)
- **What:** The primary loan agreement defining all economic and covenant terms, including all amendments, modifications, and waivers.
- **Why it matters:** Participation economics flow from the loan agreement; a participant must verify the loan terms it is buying into are as represented.
- **Typical form:** Executed agreement + amendment schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.04 Mortgage / Deed of Trust (with all amendments)
- **What:** The recorded security instrument encumbering the real property collateral, plus any amendments or modifications of record.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the lien exists, is properly recorded, and matches the loan description.
- **Typical form:** Recorded instrument with recording stamp; copy from county recorder.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / county recorder's office.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.05 Assignment of Leases and Rents (ALR)
- **What:** Recorded instrument assigning the borrower's interest in leases and rental income to the lender as additional collateral.
- **Why it matters:** In a default scenario, the ALR gives the lender (and thus participants) a direct claim on property cash flow.
- **Typical form:** Recorded instrument.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / county recorder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.06 Security Agreement (Personal Property)
- **What:** Agreement granting a security interest in personal property collateral (equipment, fixtures, accounts, etc.) beyond the real property lien.
- **Why it matters:** Ensures no collateral gap; particularly relevant for hospitality, industrial, or owner-operated properties.
- **Typical form:** Executed security agreement, often appended to or incorporated in the mortgage.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.07 Environmental Indemnity Agreement
- **What:** Agreement under which the borrower (and often guarantors) indemnify the lender against environmental liabilities.
- **Why it matters:** Shifts environmental remediation risk to borrower; a participant sharing in the loan shares in the environmental exposure without this indemnity.
- **Typical form:** Executed stand-alone agreement or exhibit to loan documents.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.08 Loan Closing Certificate
- **What:** Closing certificate confirming satisfaction of conditions precedent as of the original closing.
- **Why it matters:** Documents that the loan closed in compliance with its own terms.
- **Typical form:** Certificate executed by borrower / counsel at closing.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.09 Borrower Counsel Legal Opinion (Original Closing)
- **What:** Legal opinion from borrower's counsel confirming due authorization, execution, enforceability, and no conflicts as of closing.
- **Why it matters:** Participant relies on this opinion as part of its own enforceability analysis; absence requires participant's counsel to re-opine.
- **Typical form:** Formal legal opinion letter.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.10 Lender Counsel Legal Opinion (if obtained)
- **What:** Legal opinion from lead lender's counsel on enforceability, perfection, and usury compliance.
- **Why it matters:** Further evidences enforceability; some participants require it.
- **Typical form:** Formal legal opinion letter.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.11 Commitment Letter / Term Sheet
- **What:** The executed commitment letter or term sheet that governed the original loan negotiation.
- **Why it matters:** Identifies any side agreements, carve-outs, or economic terms not fully captured in final documents.
- **Typical form:** Executed letter or term sheet.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 01.12 Loan Closing Statement / Settlement Statement (HUD-1 or equivalent)
- **What:** Itemized statement of all funds disbursed and received at closing.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms original loan amount, any holdbacks, reserve funding, and fee structure.
- **Typical form:** HUD-1, ALTA settlement statement, or lender-prepared closing statement.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder / accounting file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.13 Holdback / Escrow / Reserve Documentation
- **What:** All documents governing funded reserves (interest reserves, capital expenditure reserves, tenant improvement / leasing commission reserves, completion reserves).
- **Why it matters:** Reserve balances affect the effective loan amount and the participant's economic position; underfunded reserves are a credit risk.
- **Typical form:** Reserve and holdback agreement or schedule; current reserve balance statement.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / servicer records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.14 Intercreditor Agreement (if mezzanine or subordinate debt exists)
- **What:** Agreement governing the rights and remedies between the senior lender and any subordinate debt holder (mezzanine lender, preferred equity, B-note holder).
- **Why it matters:** Intercreditor terms can subordinate or modify a participant's effective recovery in a default; failure to disclose is a material omission.
- **Typical form:** Executed intercreditor agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / borrower's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.15 Co-Lender Agreement / A/B Note Agreement (if applicable)
- **What:** Agreement governing rights between A-note and B-note holders, or between co-originating lenders, if the loan was structured with a bifurcated note.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes waterfall priority between note classes; affects participant's actual recovery position.
- **Typical form:** Executed co-lender or A/B note agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.16 Prior Participation Sale Documents (Existing Participants)
- **What:** All executed participation agreements, transfer documents, and any amendments covering any existing participation interests in this loan.
- **Why it matters:** New participant must understand the full existing ownership structure and any pre-existing governance rights.
- **Typical form:** Prior participation agreements, assignment and assumption agreements.
- **Where to find it:** Participation file / lead lender's records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

## Category 02 — Collateral Perfection (Real Property)

### 02.01 Recorded Mortgage / Deed of Trust (County-Certified Copy)
- **What:** County-certified copy of the recorded security instrument showing recording book, page, and instrument number.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the lien was properly recorded and is therefore enforceable against third parties and in bankruptcy.
- **Typical form:** Certified copy with recording stamp.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder / loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.02 Recording Confirmation and Lien Priority Evidence
- **What:** Evidence that the mortgage is the first-priority (or stated-priority) lien on the property, including any subordination agreements.
- **Why it matters:** A participant's collateral protection depends on lien priority.
- **Typical form:** Title search / title commitment / title policy showing lien position.
- **Where to find it:** Title company / loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.03 Title Insurance Policy (Lender's Policy — ALTA)
- **What:** ALTA lender's title insurance policy insuring the mortgage as a valid first lien, including all endorsements obtained at closing.
- **Why it matters:** Provides insurance coverage against title defects; participant inherits the policy benefit if properly noted.
- **Typical form:** ALTA lender's policy with endorsement schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder / title company.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.04 Title Endorsements Schedule
- **What:** Complete list of all endorsements to the title policy (ALTA 3.1 zoning, ALTA 9 restrictions/encumbrances, ALTA 22 location, etc.).
- **Why it matters:** Endorsements define the scope of title coverage; missing endorsements can leave exposure gaps.
- **Typical form:** Endorsement schedule attached to title policy.
- **Where to find it:** Title policy.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.05 Mechanics' Lien / Construction Lien Subordination or Waiver (if construction loan)
- **What:** Lien waivers, subordination agreements, or title endorsements protecting against mechanics' and materialmen's liens.
- **Why it matters:** In a construction or renovation context, unfiled mechanics' liens can achieve priority over the mortgage in many states.
- **Typical form:** Unconditional lien waivers / title endorsement ALTA 32 or 33.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder / title company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.06 Survey (ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey)
- **What:** Current ALTA/NSPS survey of the property showing boundaries, improvements, encroachments, easements, and access.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the collateral matches legal description; identifies physical encumbrances not visible in a title search.
- **Typical form:** Signed and sealed survey by a licensed surveyor.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder / borrower.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.07 Legal Description (from Recorded Instrument)
- **What:** Verbatim legal description of the collateral parcel(s) as recorded in the security instrument.
- **Why it matters:** Must match across the note, mortgage, title policy, and survey; a mismatch creates an enforceability gap.
- **Typical form:** Exhibit A to the mortgage.
- **Where to find it:** Recorded mortgage.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.08 Subordination, Non-Disturbance, and Attornment Agreements (SNDAs)
- **What:** Agreements from tenants (and their lenders, if any) establishing lien priority, non-disturbance protections, and the tenant's obligation to attorn to a successor landlord.
- **Why it matters:** Without SNDAs, major tenants may have lease-termination rights upon foreclosure, destroying collateral value.
- **Typical form:** Executed SNDAs, typically one per major tenant.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / property management files.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.09 Estoppel Certificates (Tenants)
- **What:** Written tenant certifications confirming lease terms, rent, security deposits, defaults, and absence of landlord-side breaches.
- **Why it matters:** Prevents tenant claims of undisclosed side agreements or offsets against rent that would impair cash flow.
- **Typical form:** Executed estoppel certificates, typically dated within 90 days of origination or last renewal.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / property management files.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 03 — Collateral Perfection (Personal Property / UCC)

### 03.01 UCC-1 Financing Statement (Filed)
- **What:** Filed UCC-1 financing statement perfecting the lender's security interest in personal property collateral.
- **Why it matters:** Without a filed UCC-1, the security interest in personal property is unperfected and vulnerable to a bankruptcy trustee's strong-arm powers.
- **Typical form:** Filed UCC-1 with Secretary of State file stamp and number.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / Secretary of State database.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.02 UCC Search Results (Borrower Entity — State and County)
- **What:** UCC search results confirming OKOA's lien position, identifying any prior or conflicting UCC filings.
- **Why it matters:** Identifies competing creditors; confirms lien priority in personal property.
- **Typical form:** Official search report from Secretary of State; county-level search if fixture filing is relevant.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder / lead lender's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.03 UCC-3 Continuation Statements (if loan older than 5 years)
- **What:** Filed UCC-3 continuation statement(s) maintaining the effectiveness of the original UCC-1 beyond the 5-year lapse period.
- **Why it matters:** A UCC-1 lapses after 5 years unless continued; lapse makes the security interest unperfected.
- **Typical form:** Filed UCC-3 with Secretary of State confirmation.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.04 Fixture Filing (if applicable)
- **What:** A UCC-1 fixture filing in the county real property records for collateral that is or will become affixed to the real property.
- **Why it matters:** Perfects the security interest in fixtures against real property claimants; particularly important for hospitality FF&E, commercial HVAC, and specialized equipment.
- **Typical form:** County-recorded UCC-1 fixture filing.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder / loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 03.05 Control Agreements (Deposit Accounts, Securities Accounts)
- **What:** Deposit account control agreements (DACAs) or securities account control agreements granting the lender control over borrower accounts.
- **Why it matters:** Control is the exclusive method of perfecting a security interest in deposit accounts; DACAs are also used to sweep operating reserves into a blocked account structure.
- **Typical form:** Executed tripartite control agreement among lender, borrower, and depository bank.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 04 — Title & Survey

### 04.01 Current Title Search / Bring-Down Search
- **What:** A title search (or bring-down of the original search) confirming no new liens, judgments, or encumbrances have been recorded since the title policy date.
- **Why it matters:** Post-closing liens (mechanic's liens, tax liens, judgment liens) can prime the mortgage or complicate enforcement.
- **Typical form:** Title update / O&E report / bring-down endorsement.
- **Where to find it:** Title company (ordered by lead lender's counsel).
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.02 Title Policy Endorsement — Participant Noted as Additional Insured (or separate policy)
- **What:** Endorsement to the existing title policy noting the participant's interest, or a separate participation endorsement confirming coverage extends to the participant.
- **Why it matters:** Without this, the participant may not be able to make a direct title insurance claim.
- **Typical form:** Title policy endorsement; or confirmation from title company that existing policy covers participations.
- **Where to find it:** Title company / lead lender's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.03 Zoning Compliance Letter or Zoning Report
- **What:** Letter or report confirming the property's current zoning classification, permitted uses, and compliance (or legal non-conforming) status.
- **Why it matters:** A zoning violation or non-conforming use that cannot be rebuilt after casualty impairs collateral value.
- **Typical form:** Municipal zoning letter or third-party zoning report.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder / borrower.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 04.04 Easements, Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions (CC&Rs)
- **What:** All recorded easements, deed restrictions, and CC&Rs affecting the property.
- **Why it matters:** Undisclosed easements or restrictions can limit use, reduce value, or impair enforcement.
- **Typical form:** Recorded instruments; schedule B-II exceptions in title policy.
- **Where to find it:** Title policy Schedule B / county recorder.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 04.05 Access and Ingress/Egress Confirmation
- **What:** Confirmation that the property has legally adequate access to a public road, either directly or via a recorded access easement.
- **Why it matters:** A landlocked property is essentially unenforceable as collateral.
- **Typical form:** Survey note; title endorsement (ALTA 17); recorded access easement.
- **Where to find it:** Survey / title policy.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 05 — Borrower & Sponsor Documentation

### 05.01 Borrower Entity Formation Documents
- **What:** Articles of formation, certificate of organization, or certificate of incorporation for the borrowing entity.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the borrower exists as a legal entity and is authorized to borrow.
- **Typical form:** Secretary of State-certified formation documents.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.02 Borrower Operating Agreement / Bylaws (with all amendments)
- **What:** The governing document establishing management rights, ownership, and voting within the borrowing entity.
- **Why it matters:** Required to confirm authority to execute loan documents and identify any change-of-control provisions that may affect the loan.
- **Typical form:** Operating agreement (LLC) or bylaws (corporation) with amendment schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.03 Organizational Chart (Borrower / Ownership Cascade)
- **What:** Chart showing the full ownership structure from the borrowing entity up through the ultimate beneficial owners.
- **Why it matters:** Identifies principals; required for anti-money-laundering and Bank Secrecy Act compliance; confirms no undisclosed co-borrowers or pledgors.
- **Typical form:** Annotated org chart with ownership percentages.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.04 Good Standing Certificates (Borrower)
- **What:** Secretary of State certificates confirming the borrower is in good standing in its state of formation and in the state where the property is located (foreign qualification).
- **Why it matters:** An entity not in good standing may lack capacity to sue, be sued, or enforce contracts.
- **Typical form:** Secretary of State certificate, dated within 90 days of closing (or current refresh).
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder / Secretary of State.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.05 Borrower Incumbency Certificate and Authorizing Resolutions
- **What:** Certificate identifying authorized signatories and the resolutions authorizing the loan transaction.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the individuals who signed the loan documents had authority to do so.
- **Typical form:** Executed incumbency certificate; board or member resolutions.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.06 Borrower W-9 / Tax Identification Number
- **What:** IRS Form W-9 or equivalent confirming the borrower's taxpayer identification number.
- **Why it matters:** Required for 1099 reporting and anti-fraud verification.
- **Typical form:** Executed W-9.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 05.07 Sponsor Background Verification / Credit Report
- **What:** Background check and credit report on the sponsor(s) / principal(s).
- **Why it matters:** Discloses prior defaults, bankruptcies, judgments, or criminal history that affect creditworthiness.
- **Typical form:** Third-party background report; consumer credit report (with authorization).
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 05.08 Sponsor Net Worth and Liquidity Verification
- **What:** Verified evidence of sponsor's net worth and liquid assets as of underwriting.
- **Why it matters:** Underpins guarantor capacity; a participant relies on this analysis when evaluating recourse.
- **Typical form:** Personal financial statement; bank statements; brokerage statements.
- **Where to find it:** Underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.09 Borrower AML / KYC Documentation
- **What:** Anti-money-laundering and Know Your Customer documentation for the borrower and its beneficial owners.
- **Why it matters:** Participant may have its own AML obligations; also confirms lead lender performed required diligence.
- **Typical form:** KYC checklist; OFAC screening; beneficial ownership certification (FinCEN).
- **Where to find it:** Compliance / origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 06 — Guarantor Documentation

### 06.01 Guaranty Agreement(s) (with all amendments)
- **What:** All executed guaranty agreements — payment, completion, carry, and/or limited guaranties — covering the loan.
- **Why it matters:** Guaranties are a key recourse mechanism for the participant; understanding scope, carve-outs, and limitations is essential.
- **Typical form:** Executed guaranty agreements.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.02 Guarantor Formation Documents and Good Standing
- **What:** If any guarantor is an entity (not an individual), its formation documents and good standing certificates.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the entity guarantor exists and has capacity to guarantee.
- **Typical form:** Secretary of State filings.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.03 Guarantor Personal Financial Statement (or Entity Financial Statements)
- **What:** Current personal financial statement (individual guarantors) or financial statements (entity guarantors), including real estate schedule.
- **Why it matters:** A guaranty is only as valuable as the guarantor's ability to pay; participant must assess this independently.
- **Typical form:** Signed personal financial statement; audited or reviewed entity financials.
- **Where to find it:** Underwriting file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.04 Guarantor Credit Report
- **What:** Credit report on individual guarantors.
- **Why it matters:** Discloses existing obligations and payment history; identifies competing claims on guarantor assets.
- **Typical form:** Tri-merge credit report (with borrower authorization).
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 06.05 Carve-Out Guaranty Analysis
- **What:** Summary or marked-up analysis of the bad-boy carve-out provisions in the guaranty — what acts trigger full-recourse, and what acts trigger partial liability.
- **Why it matters:** Carve-out scope varies widely; participant must understand what conduct by the borrower will trigger guarantor liability.
- **Typical form:** Internal analysis memo or counsel's summary.
- **Where to find it:** Underwriting / legal file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 07 — Property / Asset Documentation

### 07.01 Rent Roll (Current)
- **What:** Current rent roll showing all leases — tenant name, space, lease commencement and expiration, base rent, escalations, options, security deposit, and rent abatements or concessions.
- **Why it matters:** Drives income underwriting; participant needs to verify in-place cash flow supports debt service.
- **Typical form:** Excel or PDF rent roll, dated within 30 days.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower / property manager / servicer.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.02 Executed Lease Agreements (All Tenants, including Amendments)
- **What:** Fully executed lease agreements for all tenants occupying the property, including all amendments, extensions, termination agreements, and side letters.
- **Why it matters:** Rent roll alone is insufficient; lease terms (co-tenancy clauses, kick-out clauses, exclusives) can materially alter actual income.
- **Typical form:** Executed leases with amendment schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.03 Property Operating Statements (Trailing 12 and 24 Months)
- **What:** Actual property income and expense statements for the trailing 12 and 24 months.
- **Why it matters:** Validates underwritten net operating income against actual performance.
- **Typical form:** Income statement / general ledger extract.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager / borrower / servicer.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.04 Current Occupancy Certificate or Certificate of Occupancy
- **What:** Government-issued certificate confirming the property is legally permitted for its current use and occupancy level.
- **Why it matters:** An invalid certificate of occupancy can prevent tenanting or operation, collapsing collateral value.
- **Typical form:** Municipal certificate of occupancy; or waiver/explanation if not obtainable.
- **Where to find it:** Borrower / closing binder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.05 Property Management Agreement
- **What:** Executed agreement between the borrower and the property manager, including any assignment or pledge of management agreement to lender.
- **Why it matters:** Sets management fees (which affect net cash flow) and establishes who controls day-to-day operations; lender's assignment protects against unauthorized manager substitution.
- **Typical form:** Executed management agreement; assignment of management agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 07.06 Capital Expenditure Plan and Budget
- **What:** Borrower's capital improvement plan for the property, including budget, timeline, and funding source.
- **Why it matters:** Unfunded or unplanned capital needs create reserve risk and can impair collateral value; participant must understand exposure.
- **Typical form:** Capital expenditure schedule; lender-approved renovation budget.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file / loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 07.07 Property Tax Bills and Payment Confirmation
- **What:** Current and prior-year property tax bills, plus evidence of timely payment.
- **Why it matters:** Delinquent property taxes create a lien that is superior to the mortgage in most jurisdictions.
- **Typical form:** Municipal tax bills; payment receipts; servicer escrow statements.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer / borrower / county tax records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 08 — Third-Party Reports & Inspections

### 08.01 Appraisal Report (MAI, FIRREA-Compliant)
- **What:** Full FIRREA-compliant MAI appraisal of the collateral property, including as-is, as-stabilized, and as-completed values (as applicable).
- **Why it matters:** Establishes loan-to-value basis; participant uses it to verify collateral coverage.
- **Typical form:** Complete written appraisal report.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 08.02 Appraisal Engagement Letter and Independence Confirmation
- **What:** Engagement letter confirming the appraiser was retained directly by the lender and meets independence requirements.
- **Why it matters:** An appraiser engaged by the borrower may not satisfy FIRREA independence requirements; affects regulatory reliance.
- **Typical form:** Engagement letter.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 08.03 Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA)
- **What:** ASTM E1527-compliant Phase I ESA identifying recognized environmental conditions (RECs) at the property.
- **Why it matters:** Environmental liability can exceed property value; lender and participant exposure is joint.
- **Typical form:** Phase I report by licensed environmental consultant.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 08.04 Phase II Environmental Site Assessment (if Phase I found RECs)
- **What:** Intrusive investigation and sampling confirming or ruling out contamination identified in the Phase I.
- **Why it matters:** RECs without Phase II follow-up leave material liability unquantified.
- **Typical form:** Phase II report; remediation plan if required.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if Phase I flagged RECs)

### 08.05 Property Condition Report / Assessment (PCA)
- **What:** Third-party property condition assessment identifying deferred maintenance, capital needs, and immediate repair requirements.
- **Why it matters:** Identifies capital shortfalls that could impair property value or cash flow.
- **Typical form:** Full PCA report with immediate repair and replacement reserve schedules.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.06 Seismic Risk Report (if property is in seismic zone)
- **What:** Probable maximum loss (PML) analysis for seismic risk, required for properties in seismic zones A or B.
- **Why it matters:** Lenders typically require seismic insurance if PML exceeds 20%; undisclosed seismic risk affects collateral value.
- **Typical form:** Third-party PML report.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** high (in applicable zones)

### 08.07 Flood Zone Determination and Flood Elevation Certificate
- **What:** FEMA flood zone determination letter confirming the property's flood zone classification; flood elevation certificate if in a Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA).
- **Why it matters:** Properties in SFHAs require federal flood insurance; undisclosed flood exposure creates insurance and collateral risk.
- **Typical form:** Standard flood zone determination; FEMA elevation certificate.
- **Where to find it:** Closing binder / flood determination vendor.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.08 Construction / Renovation Inspection Reports (if construction or value-add loan)
- **What:** Third-party inspector's draw-period inspection reports confirming work in place, budget-to-completion, and lien waiver collection.
- **Why it matters:** In construction loans, inspection reports are the primary control against over-advancing or funding disputed work.
- **Typical form:** Periodic inspector reports; draw request packages.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / construction manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 09 — Insurance

### 09.01 Property Insurance Policy (All-Risk / Special Form)
- **What:** Current all-risk or special-form property insurance policy naming the lender as mortgagee and loss payee, with coverage amounts meeting loan agreement minimums.
- **Why it matters:** Casualty loss without adequate insurance can wipe out collateral value; lender (and participant) is exposed.
- **Typical form:** Current declarations page; ACORD certificate of insurance; mortgagee notation confirmation.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / borrower's insurance broker.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 09.02 General Liability Insurance Policy
- **What:** Commercial general liability policy naming the lender as an additional insured, with minimum coverage meeting loan agreement requirements.
- **Why it matters:** Protects against third-party bodily injury or property damage claims arising at the collateral property.
- **Typical form:** Current declarations page; additional insured endorsement.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / borrower's insurance broker.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 09.03 Flood Insurance Policy (if SFHA)
- **What:** Federal or private flood insurance policy if the property is in a Special Flood Hazard Area.
- **Why it matters:** Required by federal law for regulated lenders; absence creates compliance and coverage risk.
- **Typical form:** NFIP policy or private equivalent; declarations page.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / borrower's insurance broker.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if SFHA)

### 09.04 Builder's Risk Insurance (if construction or renovation)
- **What:** Builder's risk policy covering the project during construction or renovation, naming the lender as loss payee.
- **Why it matters:** Property insurance typically excludes construction-phase losses; builder's risk fills this gap.
- **Typical form:** Builder's risk declarations page; lender loss payee endorsement.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / borrower's insurance broker.

## Category 14 — Participation Agreement & Governance

### 14.01 Participation Agreement (Executed, with All Amendments)
- **What:** The definitive participation agreement governing the sale of a participation interest to the participant — including all economic, governance, and operational terms.
- **Why it matters:** This is the foundational document for the participation transaction; without an executed agreement there is no participation.
- **Typical form:** Executed participation agreement; amendment schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Participation file / lead lender's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 14.02 Voting and Consent Mechanics Schedule
- **What:** Explicit schedule or matrix identifying every loan-level action and the corresponding consent standard: (a) lead lender discretion, (b) participant consent required, (c) unanimous consent required.
- **Why it matters:** Consent mechanics define the participant's governance rights; ambiguity creates future disputes over who controls workout decisions, modifications, and enforcement.
- **Typical form:** Schedule or exhibit to the participation agreement; or stand-alone consent matrix.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement / participation file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 14.03 Participant Consent Threshold Documentation
- **What:** Documentation specifying whether participant consent thresholds are based on dollar amount (pro-rata share), number of participants, or other criteria, and what constitutes a "major decision" triggering consent rights.
- **Why it matters:** Major decisions (material modification, enforcement action, acceptance of a deed in lieu) must have a clear consent standard.
- **Typical form:** Definitions section of participation agreement; consent threshold schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 14.04 Register of All Participants (Current)
- **What:** Current register of all participation interest holders — name, participation percentage, contact information, and date of admission.
- **Why it matters:** New participant must understand the full governance constituency and any pre-existing participations that affect voting mechanics.
- **Typical form:** Participant register maintained by lead lender.
- **Where to find it:** Lead lender / participation file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 14.05 Participant Admission and Transfer Restrictions
- **What:** Terms governing future transfers of the participation interest — right of first refusal, lead lender consent, eligible transferee requirements, notice procedures.
- **Why it matters:** A participant that cannot transfer freely has an illiquid interest; restrictions must be fully understood pre-closing.
- **Typical form:** Transfer restriction provisions in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.06 Lead Lender Discretion vs. Mandatory Consent Actions — Full Matrix
- **What:** Comprehensive enumeration of: (i) actions the lead lender may take unilaterally without participant notice; (ii) actions requiring participant notice but not consent; (iii) actions requiring participant consent; (iv) actions requiring unanimous consent.
- **Why it matters:** This matrix defines the de facto governance of the participation; surprises here are the most common source of participation disputes.
- **Typical form:** Exhibit to participation agreement; supplemental consent protocol.
- **Where to find it:** Participation file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 14.07 Participation Closing Date and Effective Date Confirmation
- **What:** Documentation of the participation's effective date (for economic entitlement) and closing date (for legal transfer).
- **Why it matters:** Economic entitlement (accrued interest, fees) from the effective date; ownership transfer on the closing date; ambiguity creates accounting disputes.
- **Typical form:** Participation agreement; closing statement; effective date certificate.
- **Where to find it:** Participation file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.08 Form of Assignment and Assumption Agreement (for future transfers)
- **What:** Pre-agreed form of assignment and assumption agreement for use in any future transfer of the participation interest.
- **Why it matters:** Standardizes future transfers; ensures lead lender's consent requirements and representation obligations are preserved.
- **Typical form:** Exhibit to participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 14.09 Governing Law and Jurisdiction (Participation Agreement)
- **What:** Choice of law and jurisdiction provisions in the participation agreement.
- **Why it matters:** Different state laws give participants vastly different rights; participant must evaluate under the governing law.
- **Typical form:** Governing law clause in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.10 Successor Lead Lender Mechanics
- **What:** Provisions addressing what happens if the lead lender becomes insolvent, is acquired, loses required licenses, or is otherwise unable to perform its lead responsibilities.
- **Why it matters:** Without successor mechanics, a lead lender failure leaves participants without a servicer and potentially in an enforcement limbo.
- **Typical form:** Successor lead lender provisions in participation agreement; or stand-alone succession agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.11 Non-Petition and Limited Recourse Provisions (if SPE borrower)
- **What:** Non-petition covenants and limited recourse provisions in the participation agreement if the borrower is a single-purpose entity.
- **Why it matters:** Protects the SPE bankruptcy remoteness structure; participant must agree not to file an involuntary petition against the borrower entity.
- **Typical form:** Non-petition covenant in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.12 Regulatory Compliance Representations (Lead Lender)
- **What:** Representations by the lead lender that the loan was originated and is being serviced in compliance with all applicable laws (lending laws, fair lending, banking regulations).
- **Why it matters:** Participation in a non-compliant loan exposes the participant to regulatory liability; representations allow contractual recourse.
- **Typical form:** Representation and warranty section of participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 15 — Lead Lender Conduct & Disclosures

### 15.01 Lead Lender Standard of Care Disclosure
- **What:** Explicit statement of the standard of care the lead lender owes to participants — typically "same care as if acting for its own account" or a modified gross negligence standard.
- **Why it matters:** Defines the legal threshold for lead lender liability to participants; a below-market standard (willful misconduct only) leaves the participant largely unprotected.
- **Typical form:** Standard of care provisions in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.02 Lead Lender Retained Economic Interest (Skin in the Game)
- **What:** Confirmation of what percentage of the loan OKOA retains after the participation sale.
- **Why it matters:** Participant alignment depends on the lead lender retaining meaningful exposure; a zero-retention lead has no economic incentive to manage the loan carefully.
- **Typical form:** Participation agreement statement of OKOA's retained interest; updated participant register.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement / participant register.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.03 Servicing Fee Disclosure (Lead Lender Retained Fee)
- **What:** Disclosure of all fees and compensation retained by OKOA as lead lender and servicer — servicing fee, administration fee, modification fees, extension fees, workout fees.
- **Why it matters:** Undisclosed lead lender fee retention creates a conflict of interest and reduces yield to participant.
- **Typical form:** Fee schedule in participation agreement; servicing fee disclosure letter.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.04 Conflict of Interest Disclosures (Lead Lender)
- **What:** Disclosure of any conflicts of interest between OKOA and the borrower, guarantors, or the property — equity co-investment, relationship lending, affiliate relationships.
- **Why it matters:** Undisclosed conflicts can lead to decisions that benefit the lead at the participant's expense.
- **Typical form:** Conflict disclosure letter or representation in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.05 Protective Advance Policy and History
- **What:** Policy governing non-pro-rata protective advances (advances for taxes, insurance, or other property protection not shared pro-rata with participants), plus a history of any advances made to date.
- **Why it matters:** Protective advances made by the lead lender can create a senior claim that primes the participant's pro-rata interest in recovery proceeds.
- **Typical form:** Protective advance provisions in participation agreement; advance history schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement / servicer records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.06 Default Management Protocol — Lead Lender Authority
- **What:** Provisions specifying the lead lender's authority in a default scenario — who controls enforcement, workout, foreclosure, and REO disposition.
- **Why it matters:** In a default, the participant has the most exposure and the least control; the protocol defines whether the participant can compel or block enforcement.
- **Typical form:** Default management provisions in participation agreement; enforcement protocol schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.07 Information and Reporting Obligations (Lead to Participant)
- **What:** Complete schedule of information the lead lender is obligated to provide to participants — frequency, content, format, and delivery timeline (monthly servicer reports, annual financial statements, event-driven notices).
- **Why it matters:** A participant with inadequate information cannot monitor its investment or exercise consent rights in a timely manner.
- **Typical form:** Information covenant provisions in participation agreement; reporting schedule exhibit.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.08 Lead Lender Representations and Warranties (as of Participation Sale Date)
- **What:** All representations and warranties made by OKOA to the participant as of the participation closing date — loan status, no undisclosed defaults, no undisclosed modifications, document completeness.
- **Why it matters:** The participant's primary contractual recourse for misrepresentation flows from these representations.
- **Typical form:** R&W section of participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.09 Indemnification Provisions (Lead Lender to Participant)
- **What:** Indemnification obligations of the lead lender to the participant for losses arising from lead lender misconduct, gross negligence, or breach of representations.
- **Why it matters:** Defines the practical recourse available to the participant if the lead lender causes harm.
- **Typical form:** Indemnification section of participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.10 Cross-Default and Cross-Collateralization Disclosures
- **What:** Disclosure of any provision in the loan documents or related agreements under which a default on a separate loan, or a lien on a different asset, affects this loan.
- **Why it matters:** A participant in one loan may unknowingly take on exposure to a cross-defaulted portfolio; material non-disclosure here is actionable.
- **Typical form:** Disclosure letter; representations in participation agreement; cross-default schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement / loan file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.11 Material Loan Events Since Origination — Disclosure Schedule
- **What:** Comprehensive disclosure of all material events since loan origination — defaults, modifications, forbearances, litigation, environmental events, casualty losses, condemnation proceedings.
- **Why it matters:** A participant cannot diligence what it does not know; a complete disclosure schedule is the mechanism for full disclosure.
- **Typical form:** Disclosure schedule attached to participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

## Category 16 — Pro-Rata Mechanics, Waterfalls & Fee Splits

### 16.01 Principal Payment Waterfall
- **What:** Exact mechanics for distributing scheduled and unscheduled principal payments among participants — timing, calculation, rounding, and partial payment treatment.
- **Why it matters:** Even small errors in principal waterfall mechanics compound over time and create balance disputes.
- **Typical form:** Payment waterfall provisions and calculation examples in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.02 Interest Payment Waterfall
- **What:** Mechanics for distributing interest payments — including accrual basis, payment timing, day count convention, and default rate allocation.
- **Why it matters:** Interest distribution errors are the most common source of participation accounting disputes.
- **Typical form:** Interest distribution provisions in participation agreement; calculation methodology exhibit.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.03 Default Interest and Late Fee Allocation
- **What:** Allocation of default interest, late charges, and default-related fees between the lead lender and participants.
- **Why it matters:** Some participation agreements allocate default interest entirely to the lead lender as compensation for workout burden; others share pro-rata; the participant must know which applies.
- **Typical form:** Default fee allocation provisions in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.04 Foreclosure and Enforcement Proceeds Waterfall
- **What:** Priority and allocation of net proceeds from foreclosure sale, deed-in-lieu, or other enforcement action — including order of payment (costs, default interest, principal, prepayment premium, fees).
- **Why it matters:** In a loss scenario, recovery depends entirely on waterfall priority; participants with subordinated recovery positions need to know this before closing.
- **Typical form:** Enforcement proceeds waterfall in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.05 REO (Real Estate Owned) Income and Expense Allocation
- **What:** Allocation of income and expenses from any property acquired through foreclosure or deed-in-lieu, pending disposition.
- **Why it matters:** REO holding costs can be material; without a clear allocation, participants may bear unexpected expenses.
- **Typical form:** REO provisions in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.06 Modification Fee and Extension Fee Splits
- **What:** Allocation of fees charged to the borrower for loan modifications and extension options between the lead lender and participants.
- **Why it matters:** Lead lenders sometimes retain 100% of modification and extension fees as servicing compensation; participants should know this ahead of closing.
- **Typical form:** Fee split schedule in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.07 Prepayment Premium Allocation
- **What:** Allocation of any prepayment premium collected from the borrower between the lead lender and participants.
- **Why it matters:** Prepayment premiums can be significant on longer-term loans; a participant that does not share in them loses yield.
- **Typical form:** Prepayment provisions in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.08 Expense Reimbursement Mechanics (Lead Lender Costs)
- **What:** Provisions governing the lead lender's right to be reimbursed by participants for loan administration, legal, and enforcement costs — timing, cap, and approval process.
- **Why it matters:** Uncapped expense reimbursement rights can significantly reduce participant yield, particularly in a workout situation.
- **Typical form:** Expense reimbursement provisions in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.09 Protective Advance Repayment Priority
- **What:** Provisions establishing that non-pro-rata protective advances made by the lead lender are repaid first, before pro-rata distributions, with interest.
- **Why it matters:** Unrecovered protective advances effectively dilute the participant's net recovery; the participant must know this priority structure in advance.
- **Typical form:** Protective advance priority provisions in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.10 Waterfall Reconciliation Statement (Current)
- **What:** Current statement reconciling all amounts paid, accrued, and outstanding as between OKOA and each participant under the waterfall, from the effective date of the participation through the data room date.
- **Why it matters:** Without a current reconciliation, the participant cannot verify that prior distributions were made correctly or that no amounts are in dispute.
- **Typical form:** Servicer-prepared waterfall reconciliation; or lead lender accounting statement.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer / lead lender's accounting.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.11 Distribution Timing and Mechanics (Settlement Date, Wiring Instructions)
- **What:** Procedures governing when and how distributions are made to participants — business day conventions, wiring instructions, cut-off times, and notification requirements.
- **Why it matters:** Operational mechanics affect cash flow timing; participants need to know when to expect funds.
- **Typical form:** Distribution mechanics provisions in participation agreement; wiring instruction exhibit.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 17 — Participant Rights & Remedies

### 17.01 Participant Information Rights (Ongoing)
- **What:** Contractual rights of the participant to receive loan and property information on an ongoing basis — content, frequency, and format.
- **Why it matters:** Monitoring rights are the participant's primary ongoing governance tool; inadequate information rights leave the participant unable to manage its investment.
- **Typical form:** Information covenant in participation agreement; reporting schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 17.02 Participant Right to Audit Lead Lender Records
- **What:** Provision granting the participant the right to audit the lead lender's loan records, servicer reports, and waterfall calculations.
- **Why it matters:** Audit rights are the backstop for verifying accuracy of servicer reports and waterfall distributions.
- **Typical form:** Audit right provision in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 17.03 Participant Buyout / Put Rights
- **What:** Any provision giving the participant the right to put its participation interest back to the lead lender (or another buyer) at a pre-agreed price or formula, or under defined trigger events.
- **Why it matters:** Put rights are a critical liquidity protection; a participant without put rights is entirely illiquid.
- **Typical form:** Buyout/put right provisions in participation agreement; put trigger schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 17.04 Lead Lender Call Right (Forced Buyout of Participant)
- **What:** Any provision giving the lead lender the right to call the participant's interest — force-buying out the participant at a pre-agreed formula.
- **Why it matters:** A call right in the lead lender's favor can terminate the participant's investment at an inopportune time.
- **Typical form:** Call right provisions in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 17.05 Participant Right to Direct Enforcement (if Lead Defaults)
- **What:** Provisions specifying the participant's rights if the lead lender fails to take required enforcement action — right to compel, right to replace the lead, or direct enforcement rights.
- **Why it matters:** Without direct enforcement rights, a participant whose lead lender refuses to foreclose may have no practical remedy.
- **Typical form:** Enforcement rights provisions in participation agreement; lead lender default cure period.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 17.06 Participant Remedies Against Lead Lender (Breach/Default)
- **What:** Full enumeration of remedies available to the participant if the lead lender defaults on its obligations — specific performance, indemnification, termination, replacement.
- **Why it matters:** Without defined remedies, the participant must rely on general contract law, which is slower and more expensive.
- **Typical form:** Remedies provisions in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 17.07 Dispute Resolution Mechanism
- **What:** Arbitration, mediation, or litigation provisions in the participation agreement — including governing forum, waiver of jury trial, and timeframes.
- **Why it matters:** Dispute resolution forum and process can dramatically affect the cost and speed of resolving a participation dispute.
- **Typical form:** Dispute resolution and jury waiver provisions in participation agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation agreement.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 17.08 Participant Step-In Rights (Mezzanine Overlay — if applicable)
- **What:** If the participation sits alongside a mezzanine loan structure, step-in rights allowing a participant to cure a mezzanine default and take control of the mezzanine borrower's equity pledge.

## Category 18 — Litigation & Disclosures

### 18.01 Borrower Litigation Search Results
- **What:** State and federal court search results for active or threatened litigation against the borrower entity and principal sponsors.
- **Why it matters:** Undisclosed litigation can affect borrower solvency, property rights, or the lender's enforcement position.
- **Typical form:** Third-party litigation search report; PACER federal search; state court search.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file / lead lender's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 18.02 Guarantor Litigation Search Results
- **What:** State and federal court search results for active or threatened litigation against guarantors.
- **Why it matters:** Guarantors with pending judgments or in litigation have reduced effective capacity.
- **Typical form:** Same as borrower litigation search.
- **Where to find it:** Origination file / lead lender's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 18.03 Property-Level Litigation Disclosure
- **What:** Any litigation involving the property directly — boundary disputes, tenant lawsuits, condemnation proceedings, neighbor disputes.
- **Why it matters:** Property litigation can impair marketability, value, or use.
- **Typical form:** Borrower disclosure; lead lender's investigation memo.
- **Where to find it:** Loan file / property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 18.04 Lender-Side Litigation Involving This Loan
- **What:** Any litigation in which OKOA itself is a party, related to this loan — borrower counterclaims, guarantor suits, participation disputes with other lenders.
- **Why it matters:** Active litigation against the lead lender on this loan is material information for the participant.
- **Typical form:** OKOA counsel disclosure.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 18.05 Bankruptcy Search Results (Borrower and Guarantors)
- **What:** PACER bankruptcy search confirming no pending or recent bankruptcy petitions for the borrower entity or guarantors.
- **Why it matters:** An automatic stay in bankruptcy halts all enforcement; a participant must know if this risk is live.
- **Typical form:** PACER bankruptcy search report.
- **Where to find it:** Lead lender's counsel / origination file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 18.06 Government / Regulatory Action Disclosures

- **Where to find it:** Loan file / borrower disclosure.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 19.01 Participation Certificate (if certificated)

- **What:** Closing statement for the participation sale — purchase price calculation, accrued interest adjustment, fees, and net proceeds.
- **Why it matters:** Documents the economic settlement of the participation transfer; basis for accounting entries by both parties.
- **Typical form:** Lead lender-prepared closing statement.
- **Where to find it:** Participation file.

### 19.03 OKOA Board / Investment Committee Approval (Participation Sale)
- **What:** OKOA's internal approval authorizing the sale of the participation interest, including any conditions imposed.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms OKOA is authorized to sell the participation; relevant to any regulatory examination.
- **Typical form:** Board resolution; investment committee approval memo.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA internal records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 19.04 Participant's Authorization to Purchase
- **What:** Participant's board resolution, investment committee approval, or equivalent authorization to purchase the participation interest.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms the participant had authority to close; protects against later claims of unauthorized purchase.
- **Typical form:** Board resolution or investment committee authorization.
- **Where to find it:** Participant / participation file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

- **Where to find it:** Data room.
- **Severity if absent:** high

- **Where to find it:** Participation file.

### 19.09 Regulatory Filing Confirmations (if applicable)

- **Where to find it:** Participation file.
