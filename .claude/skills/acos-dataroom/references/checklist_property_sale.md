# Base Checklist — Property Sale

> **Scope:** Outbound diligence for selling real property (often post-foreclosure / REO).
> **Role:** Prior — applied via Phase 7a tailoring to produce per-deal scope.
> **Default action:** Every item is `in_scope` until tailoring marks it otherwise.

---

## Severity Rubric

- `critical` — No buyer will close without this (e.g., title commitment, environmental Phase I).
- `high` — Standard buyer expectation (rent roll, operating statements).
- `medium` — Reasonable diligence ask (utility easement details, older studies).
- `low` — Nice to have; frequently omitted without issue.

---

## Category 01 — Title

### 01.01 Preliminary Title Report / Title Commitment
- **What:** Current title commitment issued by a licensed title insurer, showing Schedule A (insured amount, policy type, vesting) and Schedule B (exceptions and exclusions).
- **Why it matters:** The foundational document of every real estate sale — buyers will not underwrite without it.
- **Typical form:** Title commitment (ALTA form), dated within 30–60 days of listing.
- **Where to find it:** Title company (First American, Fidelity, Stewart, Chicago Title, or local equivalent).
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.02 Underlying Title Policy (Owner's Policy)
- **What:** The prior owner's policy of title insurance that OKOA received at acquisition or at trustee sale.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes the insured chain of title at OKOA's acquisition and exposes any carve-outs the insurer required at that time.
- **Typical form:** ALTA Owner's Policy or trustee sale endorsement.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA closing file; original lender's title file if policy was issued at loan closing.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.03 Schedule B-II Exceptions — Full Exception Documents
- **What:** Copies of every document referenced in Schedule B-II (CC&Rs, easements, restrictions, prior plats).
- **Why it matters:** Buyers must read each exception to assess its impact on use, financing, and value; a bare reference to a recorded instrument is insufficient.
- **Typical form:** Recorded instrument copies (county recorder certified or as-recorded PDF).
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; title company exception package.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.04 CC&Rs and Governing Declaration
- **What:** Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions recorded against the property.
- **Why it matters:** CC&Rs may restrict use, mandate assessments, grant neighbors approval rights, or impose architectural controls that directly affect a buyer's development or operating plans.
- **Typical form:** Recorded declaration, including all recorded amendments.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; HOA management company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.05 HOA / POA Documents and Financials
- **What:** Articles of incorporation, bylaws, current budget, reserve study, delinquency status, meeting minutes (last 24 months), pending litigation disclosure.
- **Why it matters:** Underfunded reserves or pending special assessments become a buyer's problem post-close; litigation can cloud title or encumber the property.
- **Typical form:** Package from HOA management company; state-mandated disclosure form where applicable.
- **Where to find it:** HOA management company; recorded documents.
- **Severity if absent:** high (if HOA exists); low (if no HOA)

### 01.06 Recorded Easements — Full Instruments
- **What:** Copies of every recorded easement (access, utility, drainage, conservation, view corridor, reciprocal parking, cross-access).
- **Why it matters:** Easements run with the land and can limit what a buyer can build, where they can build, and how they can operate the property.
- **Typical form:** Recorded grant of easement or recorded plat dedication.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; title exception package.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.07 Unrecorded Easements and Access Agreements
- **What:** Informal or contractual access and use agreements that are not in the public record but affect the property's use.
- **Why it matters:** An unrecorded agreement may not appear in title search, creating a surprise liability post-close.
- **Typical form:** License agreement, letter agreement, or verbal confirmation memo.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA property files; prior operator records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.08 Mechanics' and Materialmen's Lien Search
- **What:** Search of recorded mechanics' liens or notices of commencement filed against the property within the applicable lien period.
- **Why it matters:** Unpaid contractors from a prior owner's construction project can file liens that survive foreclosure in some jurisdictions.
- **Typical form:** County recorder lien search; title company search.
- **Where to find it:** Title company; county recorder.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.09 Judgment and Tax Lien Search
- **What:** Search for federal and state tax liens, judgment liens, and UCC filings against the prior owner (as person, not just as property encumbrances).
- **Why it matters:** Certain liens (particularly federal tax liens) can survive foreclosure or attach to property through successor-in-interest rules.
- **Typical form:** Title search supplemental; IRS/state agency lien search.
- **Where to find it:** Title company; county recorder; federal lien registry.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.10 Vesting Deed — OKOA's Deed into Property
- **What:** The recorded deed conveying title to OKOA (trustee's deed upon sale, sheriff's deed, or warranty deed at purchase).
- **Why it matters:** Establishes OKOA's standing as seller and the nature of deed warranty it can offer (trustee deeds convey no warranty; buyers need to understand what they're getting).
- **Typical form:** Recorded deed (county recorder certified copy).
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; OKOA closing file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.11 Prior Deeds (Chain of Title — Last 10 Years)
- **What:** All recorded conveyances of the property for the past decade.
- **Why it matters:** Prior transfers can reveal missing liens, arms-length pricing questions, or title defects not yet reflected in a commitment.
- **Typical form:** Recorded deed copies.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; title plant.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.12 ALTA / Extended Coverage Endorsements Available
- **What:** List of endorsements that the title insurer is willing to issue (survey, zoning, access, contiguity, environmental lien, etc.).
- **Why it matters:** Sophisticated buyers and their lenders will require specific endorsements; knowing what is available avoids last-minute surprises.
- **Typical form:** Title insurer endorsement commitment schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Title company commitment.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.13 Foreclosure Proceedings Record (REO)
- **What:** Complete record of the foreclosure: notice of default, notice of trustee sale / lis pendens, proof of publication, trustee's affidavit, redemption period documentation, and any court orders (judicial foreclosure).
- **Why it matters:** A buyer's title insurer will underwrite the strength of OKOA's title through the foreclosure. Defects in process can create a cloud that makes the property uninsurable or unfinanceable.
- **Typical form:** County recorder filings; court docket for judicial foreclosures.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; OKOA legal files; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (REO); not applicable (non-distressed)

### 01.14 Redemption Period Expiration Confirmation
- **What:** Written confirmation (title company memo or counsel letter) that the statutory redemption period has fully expired and no redemption rights remain.
- **Why it matters:** In states with post-sale redemption rights, a buyer who closes before expiration takes subject to those rights.
- **Typical form:** Counsel letter; title company underwriting memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (REO in redemption-right states); low (non-redemption states)

## Category 02 — Survey and Legal Description

### 02.01 ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey
- **What:** Current survey prepared by a licensed surveyor and certified to OKOA, the buyer, and the title company, meeting 2021 ALTA/NSPS minimum standards.
- **Why it matters:** The ALTA survey is the only way to confirm boundaries, identify encroachments, locate easements physically on the parcel, confirm acreage, and satisfy most institutional lenders.
- **Typical form:** Stamped and sealed ALTA survey drawing with Table A items.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA files (if existing); commission new survey if absent or >5 years old.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.02 Legal Description (from Title Commitment)
- **What:** The metes-and-bounds, lot-and-block, or other recorded legal description, as it appears in the current title commitment.
- **Why it matters:** Discrepancies between the legal description in the deed, survey, and title commitment can derail closing.
- **Typical form:** Schedule A of title commitment; exhibit to deed.
- **Where to find it:** Title commitment; recorded vesting deed.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.03 Prior Survey(s) (if ALTA not current)
- **What:** Any existing surveys, including older ALTA surveys, boundary surveys, or topographic surveys.
- **Why it matters:** Prior surveys provide baseline information and can be updated more efficiently than commissioning from scratch.
- **Typical form:** Stamped survey drawing.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA files; prior owner's files; local surveyor records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 02.04 Flood Zone Determination / FEMA Map
- **What:** Current FEMA National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) flood zone determination letter and reference to applicable FIRM panel.
- **Why it matters:** Flood zone designation affects insurance cost, lender requirements, and in some zones, federal mandatory purchase requirements.
- **Typical form:** Flood zone determination certificate (standard form); FEMA FIRM map excerpt.
- **Where to find it:** FEMA Flood Map Service Center; title company; licensed flood zone determination service.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.05 Parcel Map / Assessor Parcel Map
- **What:** County assessor's or recorder's parcel map showing the subject parcel's configuration and surrounding context.
- **Why it matters:** Confirms APN match, parcel configuration, and adjacencies.
- **Typical form:** Assessor parcel map (APN map).
- **Where to find it:** County assessor's office; GIS portal.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 02.06 Topographic / Grading Survey (Land and Development Sites)
- **What:** Topographic survey showing existing grades, contours, spot elevations, and drainage patterns.
- **Why it matters:** Critical for development feasibility, grading cost estimates, and stormwater design.
- **Typical form:** Topo survey drawing; civil engineer grading plan.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA files; civil engineer; prior developer's files.
- **Severity if absent:** high (land/development); low (stabilized income property)

### 02.07 Subdivision Plat and Lot Tie / Lot Line Adjustment Documents
- **What:** Recorded subdivision plat, any lot tie agreements combining parcels, or lot line adjustments affecting the subject.
- **Why it matters:** Determines whether all parcels are legally combined for development purposes and whether any subdivision approval conditions run with the land.
- **Typical form:** Recorded plat; recorded lot line adjustment.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; planning department.
- **Severity if absent:** high (multi-parcel assemblages); low (single-parcel stabilized assets)

## Category 03 — Environmental

### 03.01 Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA)
- **What:** Phase I ESA prepared by a qualified environmental professional (QEP) per ASTM E1527-21, including recognized environmental conditions (RECs), controlled RECs (CRECs), and historical RECs (HRECs).
- **Why it matters:** Buyers and their lenders universally require a current Phase I; no institutional buyer will close without one. Establishes CERCLA "innocent landowner" defense baseline.
- **Typical form:** Phase I ESA report (ASTM E1527-21 format).
- **Where to find it:** OKOA environmental files; commission new if >180 days old.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.02 Phase II Environmental Site Investigation
- **What:** Soil, groundwater, or soil-vapor sampling and laboratory analysis to characterize RECs identified in Phase I.
- **Why it matters:** Where Phase I identifies RECs, buyers will demand Phase II before committing capital; absence transfers unknown risk to buyer and generally reduces pricing.
- **Typical form:** Phase II ESA report with boring logs, lab results, and conclusions.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA environmental files; prior environmental consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if Phase I identified RECs); not applicable (if Phase I is clean)

### 03.03 Phase III Remedial Action Plan / Cleanup Documentation
- **What:** Remedial design, implementation records, closure letter from regulatory agency, and any institutional controls (ICs) imposed post-cleanup.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers need to know whether contamination was remediated to residential or commercial standards and what ongoing monitoring or deed restrictions apply.
- **Typical form:** Remedial Action Plan (RAP); No Further Action (NFA) or closure letter.
- **Where to find it:** State environmental agency file; OKOA environmental consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if Phase II confirmed contamination); not applicable otherwise

### 03.04 Regulatory Database / Government Records Review
- **What:** Review of federal and state environmental databases (RCRA, CERCLIS, LUST, underground storage tank registries, Superfund NPL) for listings affecting the property or nearby sites.
- **Why it matters:** Off-site contamination can migrate onto the subject property; nearby listed sites affect financing and marketability.
- **Typical form:** Database search report (EDR, Radius Map, or equivalent).
- **Where to find it:** Included in Phase I; can be pulled separately.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.05 Asbestos Survey / Asbestos Containing Materials (ACM) Report
- **What:** Building survey identifying the presence, condition, and friability of asbestos-containing materials (ACM) in all improvements.
- **Why it matters:** OSHA and EPA requirements mandate ACM disclosure and abatement before demolition or renovation; undisclosed ACM creates significant buyer liability.
- **Typical form:** Asbestos survey report with sample lab results.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA files; building inspector; prior owner's O&M plan.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (pre-1980 buildings); high (1980–2000 buildings); low (post-2000)

### 03.06 Asbestos Operations & Maintenance (O&M) Plan
- **What:** Written plan for managing in-place ACM that is not immediately abated, including inspection schedules and worker notification requirements.
- **Why it matters:** A buyer inheriting in-place ACM must receive and maintain the O&M plan or face OSHA violations.
- **Typical form:** O&M plan binder.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA property files; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high (if ACM identified); not applicable otherwise

### 03.07 Lead-Based Paint Disclosure and Testing (Residential)
- **What:** EPA-required lead-based paint disclosure form for pre-1978 residential properties, plus any XRF or lab paint chip testing results.
- **Why it matters:** Federal disclosure is legally required for residential sales; failure to disclose is a federal violation and seller liability exposure.
- **Typical form:** EPA "Protect Your Family from Lead in Your Home" pamphlet delivery acknowledgment; lead paint testing report.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA property files; environmental consultant.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (pre-1978 residential); not applicable otherwise

### 03.08 Mold Assessment and Remediation Report
- **What:** Assessment report identifying mold conditions, causation (moisture intrusion, HVAC), and remediation scope; plus remediation completion documentation.
- **Why it matters:** Mold creates health liability, insurance issues, and lender concerns; undisclosed mold is a material defect in most states.
- **Typical form:** Mold assessment report (IICRC S520 / ACGIH standards); remediation clearance testing.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA files; property manager; industrial hygienist.
- **Severity if absent:** high (if known mold history or visible moisture damage); medium otherwise

### 03.09 Radon Testing Results
- **What:** Short-term or long-term radon measurement results for the property.
- **Why it matters:** Elevated radon (above 4 pCi/L EPA action level) requires mitigation; required disclosure in many states for residential properties.
- **Typical form:** Radon test report (EPA-approved measurement device).
- **Where to find it:** OKOA files; prior owner; local health department.
- **Severity if absent:** high (residential in radon-prone zones); low (commercial)

### 03.10 Underground Storage Tank (UST) Status
- **What:** Documentation of current or former USTs: state registration certificates, leak detection records, closure reports, and soil sampling if tanks were removed.
- **Why it matters:** USTs are among the most common sources of environmental contamination; undisclosed former USTs are a serious liability.
- **Typical form:** State UST registry records; tank closure report.
- **Where to find it:** State environmental agency UST database; OKOA files; fire marshal records.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if USTs exist or previously existed); medium otherwise

### 03.11 Vapor Intrusion Assessment
- **What:** Assessment of whether subsurface contamination (on-site or off-site) is generating vapors that migrate into buildings, with indoor air quality testing results.
- **Why it matters:** Vapor intrusion is increasingly regulated; lenders require documentation in areas with known groundwater contamination.
- **Typical form:** Vapor intrusion screening report; sub-slab and indoor air sampling results.
- **Where to find it:** Environmental consultant; state agency file.
- **Severity if absent:** high (if Phase I or II flagged vapor-generating compounds); low otherwise

### 03.12 Wetlands Delineation and Jurisdictional Determination
- **What:** Wetlands delineation report by a qualified wetland scientist and, if applicable, a USACE Approved Jurisdictional Determination (AJD).
- **Why it matters:** Regulated wetlands restrict development, require permits, and can significantly reduce developable area. An AJD from the Army Corps provides regulatory certainty.
- **Typical form:** Wetlands delineation report; USACE JD letter.
- **Where to find it:** Environmental consultant; USACE district office.
- **Severity if absent:** high (land and development sites); low (fully improved urban properties)

### 03.13 Stormwater Permits and SWPPP (Active Construction Sites)
- **What:** Active NPDES Construction General Permit, Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP), and inspection logs.
- **Why it matters:** Permit violations carry fines and must be transferred to the buyer; an undisclosed open permit is a liability.
- **Typical form:** State NPDES permit; SWPPP binder.
- **Where to find it:** State environmental agency; site superintendent.
- **Severity if absent:** high (active construction); not applicable otherwise

## Category 04 — Property Condition

### 04.01 Property Condition Report (PCR) / Property Condition Assessment (PCA)
- **What:** Full PCA prepared by a licensed engineer per ASTM E2018-15 standards, covering structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, roof, life-safety, ADA, and deferred maintenance.
- **Why it matters:** Most institutional buyers and all their lenders require a PCA. It is the primary basis for pricing deferred maintenance, negotiating repair credits, and underwriting capex reserves.
- **Typical form:** ASTM E2018-15 PCA report with Immediate Repairs and Replacement Reserves tables.
- **Where to find it:** Engineering firm; OKOA files.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 04.02 Roof Inspection Report and Warranty Documentation
- **What:** Dedicated roof inspection by a licensed roofing contractor, including remaining useful life estimate, leak history, and any existing manufacturer warranty.
- **Why it matters:** Roof replacement is typically the largest single deferred maintenance item; buyers will demand a credited amount or repair escrow if the roof is at or near end of life.
- **Typical form:** Roof inspection report; warranty certificate (NDL or manufacturer).
- **Where to find it:** Roofing contractor; OKOA maintenance records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.03 Structural Engineering Report (if Issues Suspected)
- **What:** Structural engineer's assessment of foundation, framing, and load-bearing elements, including any crack monitoring or remediation documentation.
- **Why it matters:** Structural defects can render a building unlendable and affect insurability; buyers will demand remediation evidence or price reduction.
- **Typical form:** Structural engineering report with PE stamp.
- **Where to find it:** Structural engineer; OKOA files.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if known issues); medium otherwise

### 04.04 HVAC Inspection and Maintenance Records
- **What:** Inspection of all HVAC equipment with remaining useful life estimates, plus maintenance service records for the prior 24–36 months.
- **Why it matters:** HVAC replacement is a major capital expenditure; deferred maintenance during a prior owner's default period is common and must be documented.
- **Typical form:** HVAC inspection report; service records; equipment inventory.
- **Where to find it:** HVAC contractor; property manager; OKOA records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.05 Elevator Inspection and Certification
- **What:** Current state elevator inspection certificate and inspection reports for all elevators, escalators, and lifts.
- **Why it matters:** Elevators are regulated life-safety systems; expired certifications create liability and may require shutdown.
- **Typical form:** State elevator inspection certificate; inspection log.
- **Where to find it:** State elevator inspection database; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high (if elevator present); not applicable otherwise

### 04.06 Fire Suppression and Life-Safety Inspection Records
- **What:** Annual inspection reports for fire sprinklers, fire alarms, extinguishers, emergency lighting, and exit signs, plus any fire marshal violation notices and resolution documentation.
- **Why it matters:** Life-safety deficiencies are regulatory violations that must be cured; they affect insurance and can trigger occupancy issues.
- **Typical form:** Third-party fire inspection reports; fire marshal correspondence.
- **Where to find it:** Fire suppression contractor; fire marshal records; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.07 Plumbing and Domestic Water System Condition
- **What:** Inspection of domestic water supply, distribution piping (material type — copper, galvanized, PVC), water heaters, and waste lines; sewer scope video if deferred maintenance is suspected.
- **Why it matters:** Galvanized piping failures, sewer line root intrusion, and water heater deterioration are common post-default deferred maintenance items.
- **Typical form:** Plumbing inspection report; sewer scope video.
- **Where to find it:** Plumbing contractor; OKOA maintenance records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.08 Electrical System Inspection and Panel Documentation
- **What:** Inspection of main electrical service, distribution panels, wiring condition, and any code-compliance issues (Federal Pacific, aluminum wiring, etc.).
- **Why it matters:** Electrical deficiencies are insurance and life-safety issues; certain panel types are uninsurable and must be disclosed.
- **Typical form:** Electrical inspection report; panel schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Licensed electrician; OKOA records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.09 ADA Compliance Assessment
- **What:** Assessment of compliance with ADA Title III (commercial) or applicable accessibility standards, including common areas, parking, restrooms, and path of travel.
- **Why it matters:** ADA non-compliance creates ongoing litigation exposure (demand letters, DOJ complaints); buyers' lenders increasingly require documentation.
- **Typical form:** ADA compliance report with gap analysis and remediation cost estimate.
- **Where to find it:** ADA consultant; civil engineer.
- **Severity if absent:** high (commercial); medium (multifamily common areas)

### 04.10 Deferred Maintenance Log and Estimates (REO)
- **What:** Itemized list of deferred maintenance attributable to the default and REO period, with contractor bids or cost estimates for each item.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers will demand this as a basis for price negotiation; sellers who prepare it first control the narrative and prevent buyers from generating inflated estimates.
- **Typical form:** Deferred maintenance schedule with contractor quotes.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA property management team; on-site inspection.
- **Severity if absent:** high (REO); not applicable otherwise

### 04.11 Seismic / Geotechnical Report (High-Risk Zones)
- **What:** Geotechnical engineering report (soils report) including bearing capacity, liquefaction potential, and seismic site classification; seismic retrofit report if applicable.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers in seismic zones require soil classification for lender underwriting; liquefaction or landslide hazard zones require additional disclosure and may affect development.
- **Typical form:** Geotechnical report with PE stamp; seismic retrofit compliance report.
- **Where to find it:** Geotechnical engineer; OKOA files; city building department.
- **Severity if absent:** high (Seismic Zone 3 or 4 / UBC; SDC D, E, or F / IBC); low otherwise

### 04.12 Pest and Termite Inspection Report
- **What:** Wood-destroying organism (WDO) inspection report from a licensed pest control operator, including active infestations and evidence of prior treatment.
- **Why it matters:** Termite and other WDO damage can be structural; required by lender in many markets; required by law in certain states for residential.
- **Typical form:** State-mandated WDO inspection form (e.g., California NPMA-33 or equivalent).
- **Where to find it:** Licensed pest control operator.
- **Severity if absent:** high (wood-frame construction); medium otherwise

## Category 05 — Zoning, Entitlements, and Land Use

### 05.01 Zoning Verification Letter from Local Authority
- **What:** Written confirmation from the planning or zoning department of the property's current zoning classification, permitted uses, and any use permits or variances in effect.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers and lenders require authoritative confirmation of zoning; relying solely on assessor records or online GIS is insufficient for institutional diligence.
- **Typical form:** Zoning verification letter (municipality-issued); zoning certificate.
- **Where to find it:** City/county planning or zoning department.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.02 Zoning Code Printout — Applicable Standards
- **What:** Printout of the applicable zoning code sections governing the property's current district, including permitted uses, conditional uses, dimensional standards, parking requirements, and setbacks.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers need to confirm that the current use (or their intended use) is permitted by right or by conditional use permit; development feasibility depends on dimensional standards.
- **Typical form:** Municipal code excerpt; planning department handout.
- **Where to find it:** City/county municipal code (online); planning department.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.03 General Plan / Land Use Designation
- **What:** Excerpt from the applicable general plan, comprehensive plan, or master plan showing the subject property's land use designation.
- **Why it matters:** General plan designation drives long-term entitlement potential; a property zoned industrial but designated residential on the general plan may have upzone potential (or conflict risk).
- **Typical form:** General plan land use map excerpt; planning department confirmation.
- **Where to find it:** City/county planning department; general plan document (often online).
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 05.04 Conditional Use Permit (CUP) / Special Use Permit (SUP)
- **What:** All active conditional or special use permits, including conditions of approval, term (if any), and transferability provisions.
- **Why it matters:** CUPs are often personal to the operator and may not be transferable; conditions may impose operational restrictions that affect value.
- **Typical form:** Recorded or approved CUP resolution with conditions.
- **Where to find it:** City/county planning department; OKOA property files.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if use depends on CUP); not applicable otherwise

### 05.05 Variance(s)
- **What:** All approved variances from dimensional or use standards, including the approved deviation, conditions, and any sunset provisions.
- **Why it matters:** Non-conforming variances may lapse if the use changes; they create risk if the improvement was built relying on a variance that cannot be renewed.
- **Typical form:** Variance approval letter/resolution.
- **Where to find it:** City/county planning department.
- **Severity if absent:** high (if structure or use relies on variance)

### 05.06 Nonconforming Use / Nonconforming Structure Documentation
- **What:** Documentation confirming legal nonconforming status (grandfathered use or structure), including analysis of applicable amortization or reconstruction rules.
- **Why it matters:** If the nonconforming use or structure is destroyed or discontinued, it may not be permitted to be rebuilt or restarted under current zoning — material risk to buyers.
- **Typical form:** Planning department letter; zoning attorney opinion letter.
- **Where to find it:** City/county planning department.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if nonconforming); not applicable otherwise

### 05.07 Development Agreement (DA)
- **What:** Any recorded development agreement between a prior owner and the local government, including vested rights, infrastructure obligations, payment schedules, and term.
- **Why it matters:** Development agreements run with the land; obligations (fees, improvements) bind the buyer. Vested rights can also be a selling point.
- **Typical form:** Recorded development agreement.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; city planning department.
- **Severity if absent:** high (development sites); low (stabilized income property)

### 05.08 Entitlement Approvals (Tentative Map, Final Map, EIR)
- **What:** All entitlement approvals in place: tentative tract or parcel maps (TTM/TPM), final maps, environmental impact reports (EIR), negative declarations, Mitigated Negative Declarations (MND), and any mitigation monitoring programs.
- **Why it matters:** For land and development sites, entitlements are primary value drivers; their status, conditions, and expiration dates are critical to buyer underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Planning commission resolution; recorded final map; EIR document with certified adoption resolution.
- **Where to find it:** City/county planning and public works; county recorder (final maps).
- **Severity if absent:** critical (entitled land); not applicable otherwise

### 05.09 Density / FAR / Height Analysis
- **What:** Summary analysis of allowable density, floor area ratio (FAR), height limits, and lot coverage for the site under current entitlements and zoning.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers and their architects need to quickly understand the development envelope; an error here can materially affect underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Architect's or planner's massing study; planning department handout.
- **Where to find it:** Architect; planning department; zoning code.
- **Severity if absent:** high (development sites); medium (value-add properties)

### 05.10 Historic Designation Status
- **What:** Documentation of whether the property is individually listed, a contributing structure in a historic district, or subject to a historic preservation easement.
- **Why it matters:** Historic designation restricts alterations, can require expensive restoration, affects demolition rights, and may trigger design review approvals.
- **Typical form:** National Register / state/local historic register listing; historic easement.
- **Where to find it:** State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO); local landmarks commission; title.
- **Severity if absent:** high (if designation is present or suspected); low otherwise

## Category 06 — Permits, Code Compliance, and Violations

### 06.01 Building Permits — All Open and Closed
- **What:** Complete permit history from the local building department, including all building, electrical, mechanical, and plumbing permits issued, their inspection status, and finalization (certificate of occupancy or final inspection sign-off).
- **Why it matters:** Open or un-finalized permits are a title and lender issue; work performed without permits creates code violation exposure for the buyer.
- **Typical form:** Building department permit history printout.
- **Where to find it:** City/county building department; online permit portal.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.02 Certificate of Occupancy (CO)
- **What:** Current certificate of occupancy (or equivalent: certificate of completion, occupancy permit) for all buildings and tenant spaces.
- **Why it matters:** Without a valid CO, the building or space legally cannot be occupied; lenders require COs for all income-producing spaces.
- **Typical form:** City/county building department CO.
- **Where to find it:** Building department; OKOA files.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (occupied buildings); high (vacant buildings)

### 06.03 Notice of Violation (NOV) / Code Enforcement History
- **What:** All outstanding Notices of Violation, Code Enforcement citations, and abatement orders; plus resolution documentation for any prior violations.
- **Why it matters:** Open violations must be cured before close or escrowed; they create liability and can prevent financing. Buyers inherit unresolved violations.
- **Typical form:** Code enforcement department report; NOV letters.
- **Where to find it:** City/county code enforcement; building department.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if open violations exist); high (search needed even if none known)

### 06.04 Fire Department Inspection Reports and Violations
- **What:** Current fire department inspection report and any outstanding fire code violations or correction notices.
- **Why it matters:** Unresolved fire code violations can result in occupancy suspension and create liability; buyers need to know scope and cost of cure.
- **Typical form:** Fire marshal inspection report; correction notice.
- **Where to find it:** Local fire department / fire marshal.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.05 Health Department Permits and Inspection Records (Food / Hospitality)
- **What:** Health department permits, inspection scores, and any outstanding violations for food service, pool, spa, or other health-regulated operations on the property.
- **Why it matters:** Health department permits are operator-specific; buyers must understand what new licenses they must obtain and whether any violations exist.
- **Typical form:** Health department permit; inspection scorecard.
- **Where to find it:** Local health department.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if food service or hospitality); not applicable otherwise

### 06.06 Liquor License (if Applicable)
- **What:** Liquor license documentation, license type, transferability, and any disciplinary proceedings.
- **Why it matters:** Liquor licenses are jurisdiction-specific, limited in number, and often non-transferable; their status can materially affect a hospitality or retail asset's value.
- **Typical form:** State liquor authority license certificate.
- **Where to find it:** State liquor authority; property operator.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (hospitality, bar/restaurant); not applicable otherwise

### 06.07 Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) Transition Plan / Compliance History
- **What:** Any prior ADA compliance audit, transition plan, demand letters, or settlement agreements.
- **Why it matters:** Existing ADA demand letters create known litigation risk that must be disclosed; settlement agreements bind the property owner.
- **Typical form:** ADA audit report; demand letters; settlement agreement.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal files; prior owner's files.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.08 Occupancy / Density Load Documentation
- **What:** Official occupancy load calculations and posted occupancy notices for all primary assembly or commercial spaces.
- **Why it matters:** Over-occupancy is a code violation; buyers of hospitality, retail, or office properties need to confirm the posted load matches their intended use.
- **Typical form:** Building department occupancy calculation; posted occupancy placard.
- **Where to find it:** Building department; building manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 07 — Leases and Tenant Materials

### 07.01 Current Rent Roll (Certified, as of Closing Month)
- **What:** Unit-by-unit or tenant-by-tenant schedule showing: unit number/suite, tenant name, lease commencement and expiration, base rent, rent escalations, square footage, security deposit held, and lease status (current, month-to-month, holdover, vacant).
- **Why it matters:** The rent roll is the single most important operating document for an income property; it is the basis for all revenue underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Excel or property management software export; OKOA-certified.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; OKOA asset management.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.02 Executed Lease Agreements — All Current Tenants
- **What:** Fully executed copies of all current leases, including base lease, all amendments, assignment and sublease consents, and any side letters or letter agreements.
- **Why it matters:** The lease governs every right and obligation between the landlord and tenant; buyers must read every lease to underwrite the tenancy.
- **Typical form:** Executed lease documents (original or certified copy).
- **Where to find it:** OKOA property files; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.03 Lease Abstracts
- **What:** Structured summaries of each lease's material economic and legal terms (term, rent, options, termination rights, landlord obligations, tenant improvement allowances outstanding, assignment restrictions).
- **Why it matters:** Abstracts allow buyers and their counsel to quickly screen the lease portfolio and identify issues without reading every full lease.
- **Typical form:** Excel or Word lease abstract matrix.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; OKOA legal team; prepare if absent.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.04 Tenant Estoppel Certificates
- **What:** Signed certificates from each tenant confirming the lease terms, rent status, absence of landlord default, security deposit amounts, and any claims or rights.
- **Why it matters:** Estoppels protect buyers from tenants later asserting claims (e.g., "landlord owed me a TI allowance") that pre-close communications may not have revealed; required by most institutional lenders.
- **Typical form:** Standard BOMA or lender-form estoppel certificate.
- **Where to find it:** Obtained from tenants during the transaction.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (office, retail, industrial); high (multifamily)

### 07.05 Subordination, Non-Disturbance, and Attornment Agreements (SNDAs)
- **What:** Existing SNDAs between tenants and any prior lenders (including OKOA as lender), plus confirmation of whether new SNDAs will be required by the buyer's lender.
- **Why it matters:** SNDAs govern tenant rights in a foreclosure or sale; a tenant without an SNDA may not be required to honor the lease post-close.
- **Typical form:** Executed SNDA agreement.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA loan files; lease files.
- **Severity if absent:** high (anchor tenants, credit tenants); medium (small tenants)

### 07.06 Security Deposit Schedule and Proof of Funds
- **What:** Itemized schedule of all security deposits held, by tenant, including form (cash, letter of credit), and bank account or custody confirmation.
- **Why it matters:** Security deposits are tenant property; they must be transferred at closing, and buyers must confirm the amounts held match lease obligations.
- **Typical form:** Security deposit ledger; bank statement showing deposit account balance; letter of credit originals.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; OKOA accounting.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.07 Default and Cure Notices — All Active and Recent
- **What:** All notices of default (monetary or non-monetary) delivered to or by tenants within the last 36 months, including cure documentation or termination notices.
- **Why it matters:** Prior uncured defaults, or defaults that were quietly waived, can reveal problems with tenancies that do not appear on the rent roll.
- **Typical form:** Default notices; cure letters; termination notices.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal files; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.08 Lease Termination Agreements and Buyout Documentation
- **What:** Any lease termination agreements, early termination payment records, and corresponding accounting for lease termination costs.
- **Why it matters:** Terminated leases create vacancies that affect occupancy underwriting; buyers need to understand what the termination terms were and whether any obligations survive.
- **Typical form:** Executed termination agreement; accounting records.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal files; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 07.09 Tenant Improvement (TI) Allowance Status and Outstanding Obligations
- **What:** Schedule of all TI allowances granted under current leases, amounts disbursed, and any outstanding obligations the landlord has not yet funded.
- **Why it matters:** Unfunded TI obligations are a liability the buyer inherits; they must be disclosed and either cured or credited in purchase price.
- **Typical form:** TI allowance schedule; disbursement records.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA accounting; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high (office, retail); medium (multifamily, industrial)

### 07.10 Free Rent / Rent Abatement Schedule
- **What:** Schedule of any free rent periods, rent abatements, or stepped rent arrangements still in effect under current leases.
- **Why it matters:** Rent rolls typically show contract rent, not effective rent; buyers need to know the actual cash flow until abatements burn off.
- **Typical form:** Lease rent schedule; ARGUS input file.
- **Where to find it:** Lease abstracts; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.11 Parking Agreements and Shared Parking Easements
- **What:** All agreements governing tenant parking rights, including reciprocal parking easements, offsite parking agreements, and parking ratio allocations.
- **Why it matters:** Parking deficiencies are a material issue for office, retail, and multifamily; unrecorded parking agreements may not bind future owners.
- **Typical form:** Recorded or unrecorded parking agreement; lease exhibit.
- **Where to find it:** Lease files; title exception documents.
- **Severity if absent:** high (office, retail); medium (multifamily, industrial)

### 07.12 Percentage Rent Reports (Retail)
- **What:** Annual percentage rent calculation statements from retail tenants who pay additional rent based on sales volume.
- **Why it matters:** Percentage rent is part of effective rent; buyers need the history to underwrite sales performance and the likelihood of percentage rent continuing.
- **Typical form:** Tenant sales certification; landlord calculation statement.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; retail tenant reports.
- **Severity if absent:** high (retail with percentage rent clauses); not applicable otherwise

### 07.13 Exclusive Use and Co-Tenancy Provisions (Retail)
- **What:** Documentation of all exclusive use clauses (granting a tenant exclusivity in a use category) and co-tenancy provisions (allowing rent reduction or termination if anchor tenants vacate).
- **Why it matters:** Exclusive use violations expose the landlord to litigation; co-tenancy triggers can collapse NOI if an anchor leaves.
- **Typical form:** Lease provisions; lease abstract flags.
- **Where to find it:** Lease documents; lease abstracts.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (multi-tenant retail); not applicable otherwise

### 07.14 RUBS (Ratio Utility Billing System) Documentation (Multifamily)
- **What:** Documentation of the RUBS methodology used to allocate utility costs to tenants, including the formula, compliance with applicable state regulations, and billing history.
- **Why it matters:** RUBS can constitute a significant revenue stream; the methodology must comply with state law or tenants can challenge billings and seek refunds.
- **Typical form:** RUBS policy document; utility billing statements; state compliance analysis.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; utility billing company.
- **Severity if absent:** high (multifamily using RUBS); not applicable otherwise

### 07.15 Fair Housing Compliance Documentation (Multifamily)
- **What:** Evidence of fair housing compliance: leasing policies, advertising materials, application screening criteria, and any prior complaints or HUD/DFEH proceedings.
- **Why it matters:** Fair housing violations are federal and state civil rights matters; undisclosed complaints or discriminatory screening policies create buyer liability.
- **Typical form:** Leasing policy manual; screening criteria documentation; HUD complaint disposition letters.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; OKOA legal files.
- **Severity if absent:** high (multifamily); medium (commercial)

### 07.16 Holdover Tenant Status and Eviction Proceedings (REO)
- **What:** Status of all unauthorized occupants, holdover tenants, and active unlawful detainer (UD) actions; documentation of service and any court orders.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers cannot take possession free and clear if holdovers remain; active UD proceedings must be disclosed and may delay closing.
- **Typical form:** UD complaint filings; sheriff's service records; court hearing schedule.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal files; foreclosure counsel; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (REO with known occupants); not applicable otherwise

## Category 08 — Operating Financials

### 08.01 Trailing 12-Month (T-12) Operating Statement
- **What:** Month-by-month income and expense statement for the 12 months preceding the listing date, at the property level.
- **Why it matters:** T-12 is the primary basis for buyers' and lenders' NOI underwriting; every institutional buyer will require it.
- **Typical form:** Accrual or cash basis P&L from property management software (Yardi, AppFolio, RealPage, MRI, etc.).

### 08.02 Trailing 3-Month (T-3) Operating Statement
- **What:** Most recent 3-month income and expense detail, showing current run rate.
- **Why it matters:** T-3 shows trending performance; important when the property is rebounding post-REO or ramp-up period.

- **Where to find it:** Property manager; OKOA accounting.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.03 Prior 2-Year Annual Operating Statements
- **What:** Annual income and expense statements for the two full fiscal years preceding the trailing period.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers need historical context to identify seasonal patterns, non-recurring items, and expense trends.

### 08.04 Current-Year Operating Budget
- **What:** Approved operating budget for the current fiscal year, with year-to-date variance analysis.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates OKOA's management plan and allows buyers to assess whether the property is tracking to plan.

- **Where to find it:** OKOA asset management.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 08.05 General Ledger / Chart of Accounts (on Request)
- **What:** Detailed general ledger supporting the T-12 and annual statements, available for buyer's accountant review.
- **Why it matters:** Sophisticated buyers' accountants may want to trace specific line items; refusal to provide raises red flags.
- **Typical form:** G/L export from accounting system.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA accounting.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 08.06 CAM Reconciliation Statements (Office / Retail / Industrial)
- **What:** Annual CAM, insurance, and real estate tax reconciliation statements for each tenant subject to NNN or modified gross leases, for the prior 2 years.

- **Where to find it:** Property manager; OKOA accounting.
- **Severity if absent:** high (NNN leases); not applicable otherwise

### 08.07 ARGUS or DCF Model (if Available)
- **What:** ARGUS Enterprise file or equivalent DCF model used to underwrite the property, showing assumptions, inputs, and output summary.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers will build their own models, but seller's ARGUS file accelerates diligence and demonstrates transparency.

- **Where to find it:** OKOA asset management; broker.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 08.08 Hospitality Performance Reports — STR / STAR Data (Hospitality)
- **What:** Smith Travel Research (STR) STAR reports showing the property's RevPAR, ADR, and occupancy versus competitive set, for the trailing 24 months.
- **Why it matters:** STR/STAR is the hospitality industry standard for benchmarking performance; lenders will require it and buyers will cite it extensively.
- **Typical form:** STR STAR report (monthly/annual).
- **Where to find it:** Hotel operator; STR (can be purchased by buyer directly).
- **Severity if absent:** critical (hospitality); not applicable otherwise

### 08.09 Utility Expense Detail (by Meter / Account)
- **What:** Monthly utility bills (electric, gas, water/sewer, trash) for the trailing 12 months, by meter or account, and any utility allocation methodology.
- **Why it matters:** Utility costs are a major operating expense; buyers need detail to assess efficiency and model CAM pass-throughs accurately.
- **Typical form:** Utility bill copies; utility company account statements.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; OKOA accounting.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.10 Property Management Fee Schedule and Agreement
- **What:** Current property management agreement, including fee structure (percentage of EGI, hourly, flat fee), term, and termination provisions.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers need to know the management cost and whether the management agreement survives sale or terminates; out-of-market fees affect underwriting.
- **Typical form:** Executed property management agreement.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA asset management files.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 09.03 Contractor Warranties and Guarantees in Force
- **What:** All active contractor warranties, manufacturer warranties, and extended service guarantees for major capital improvements.

- **Why it matters:** Buyers underwriting a value-add play need to confirm scope, cost, and feasibility; permits in process may bind the buyer.

- **Severity if absent:** high (value-add assets); not applicable otherwise

## Category 13 — Utilities

### 13.01 Utility Service Accounts and Account Numbers
- **What:** List of all utility service accounts (electric, gas, water, sewer, trash) serving the property, with provider names, account numbers, and billing addresses.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers must open new accounts at close; a list prevents service interruptions during the transition period.
- **Typical form:** Account list (Excel); utility bill header page.
- **Where to find it:** Property manager; OKOA accounting.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 13.02 Utility Easements and Franchise Agreements
- **What:** Recorded and unrecorded easements granted to utility providers for power lines, pipelines, telecommunications, water mains, and sewer lines crossing or serving the property.
- **Why it matters:** Utility easements constrain where a buyer can build and what rights the utility has to enter and maintain infrastructure.
- **Typical form:** Recorded utility easement; franchise agreement.
- **Where to find it:** Title exception package; county recorder; utility provider.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 13.03 Water and Sewer Connection Documentation
- **What:** Proof of water and sewer connection, including tap fees paid, capacity reservation, and any connection agreements with the utility district.
- **Why it matters:** On development sites, water and sewer availability (or lack thereof) is a fundamental feasibility determinant; connection capacity limits can cap achievable density.
- **Typical form:** Utility connection permit; capacity reservation letter; Will Serve letter.
- **Where to find it:** Local water district; wastewater authority.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (land/development); medium (stabilized assets)

### 13.04 Septic System Documentation (Rural / Non-Municipal)
- **What:** Septic system permits, as-built drawings, most recent inspection report, and pumping records.
- **Why it matters:** Failed septic systems are environmental liabilities and code violations; buyers need to confirm system adequacy for intended use and density.
- **Typical form:** County health department septic permit; inspection report.
- **Where to find it:** County health department; OKOA files.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if septic system present); not applicable otherwise

### 13.05 Well Water and Water Quality Testing (Rural)
- **What:** Well permit, as-built drawing, pump test results, and water quality testing (bacteria, nitrates, minerals).
- **Why it matters:** Inadequate well yield or contaminated water supply are material defects that affect habitability, financing, and value.
- **Typical form:** County water well permit; lab water quality report.
- **Where to find it:** State water resources agency; county health department.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if well present); not applicable otherwise

### 13.06 Irrigation and Water Rights Documentation
- **What:** Documentation of any water rights (riparian, appropriative, groundwater), irrigation district membership, and water delivery contracts.
- **Why it matters:** Water rights are property rights in the West; their value can exceed land value for agricultural properties; their absence affects development feasibility in water-scarce areas.
- **Typical form:** State water resources agency permit; irrigation district membership certificate.
- **Where to find it:** State water resources agency; irrigation district.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (agricultural land); medium (arid-region development sites); low otherwise

## Category 14 — Marketing Materials and Comparable Sales

### 14.01 Offering Memorandum (OM) / Investment Highlights Package
- **What:** Formal broker-prepared OM including property summary, location analysis, financial summary, and investment thesis.
- **Why it matters:** The OM is the primary marketing document; buyers will rely on its representations, and inaccuracies create liability.
- **Typical form:** PDF OM; broker deal book.
- **Where to find it:** Listing broker; OKOA asset management.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.02 Broker Opinion of Value (BOV) / Appraisal
- **What:** Third-party appraisal or broker opinion of value supporting the listing price, including methodology (income, sales comparison, cost approach) and comparables used.
- **Why it matters:** Supports OKOA's pricing; buyers may order their own appraisal but having a third-party value as a reference point aids negotiation.
- **Typical form:** USPAP-compliant MAI appraisal; BOV letter from listing broker.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA asset management; listing broker; original lender's appraisal file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.03 Comparable Sales Analysis (Comps)
- **What:** Analysis of comparable property sales within the relevant submarket, including sale date, price per unit/SF/key, cap rate, and deal structure.
- **Why it matters:** Comps are the primary support for market pricing; buyers will compile their own, but sharing seller's comps demonstrates transparency and anchors discussion.
- **Typical form:** Comp table (Excel); CoStar or Reonomy comp report.
- **Where to find it:** Listing broker; OKOA asset management; CoStar; LoopNet.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 14.04 Market Report / Submarket Analysis
- **What:** Third-party market report covering vacancy rates, absorption, rent growth trends, and supply pipeline for the relevant product type and submarket.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers use market data to underwrite rent growth assumptions; a credible market report accelerates buyer conviction.
- **Typical form:** CBRE, JLL, Cushman, or Colliers market report; CoStar submarket analytics.
- **Where to find it:** Listing broker; OKOA asset management; market research firms.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 14.05 Aerial Photography and Property Photos
- **What:** Current aerial photography (drone) and ground-level property photographs, including interior, exterior, unit interiors, common areas, and amenities.
- **Why it matters:** Professional photography is standard in institutional marketing; poor visual materials signal under-investment and reduce buyer interest.
- **Typical form:** High-resolution digital photos; drone video.
- **Where to find it:** Listing broker; professional photographer.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.06 Property Measurement / Floor Plans (Rentable / Usable SF)
- **What:** Certified floor plans and rentable/usable square footage calculations, measured to BOMA standards (for commercial) or as-built for multifamily.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers' lenders underwrite per-SF and per-unit; if measured SF differs from represented SF, it can create disputes and affect financing.
- **Typical form:** Architect-certified floor plans; BOMA measurement report.
- **Where to find it:** Architect; property manager; listing broker.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.07 Listing and Marketing Agreements
- **What:** Executed listing agreement with the broker, including commission structure, exclusivity, term, and tail provisions.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers need to understand who is paying the commission and whether there are dual-agency issues; commission obligations survive to close.
- **Typical form:** Exclusive right-to-sell listing agreement.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA asset management; listing broker.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 15 — REO-Specific and Post-Foreclosure

### 15.01 Full Foreclosure Proceedings Record
- **What:** Complete foreclosure file: all pre-foreclosure notices, notice of default, trustee sale notice, proof of publication, affidavit of mailing, credit bid documentation, and trustee's deed upon sale (or sheriff's deed for judicial foreclosure).
- **Why it matters:** Buyers' title insurers will review the entire foreclosure record to underwrite title; any procedural defect can render title uninsurable.
- **Typical form:** Complete county recorder filing package; court docket for judicial foreclosure.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; OKOA legal / foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.02 Credit Bid Amount and Justification
- **What:** Documentation of the credit bid made at trustee sale, including the amount bid, basis for the bid, and how it compared to the outstanding indebtedness.
- **Why it matters:** Credit bid amount establishes OKOA's cost basis; buyers may use it as a data point in pricing negotiations.
- **Typical form:** Trustee sale proceeding records; OKOA board or investment committee memo.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal files; trustee records.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 15.03 REO Possession Documentation and Lockout Records
- **What:** Documentation of OKOA's process for securing possession after trustee sale, including cash-for-keys agreements, voluntary surrender documentation, and lockout records.
- **Why it matters:** Any occupant who was not properly dispossessed may have residual legal rights; buyers need to confirm OKOA has clean possession.
- **Typical form:** Cash-for-keys agreement; surrender agreement; locksmith records; possession affidavit.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal files; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high (REO with prior occupants); not applicable otherwise

### 15.04 Eviction Records and Unlawful Detainer Proceedings
- **What:** Court filings, service records, judgment records, and writ of possession documentation for all evictions pursued post-foreclosure.
- **Why it matters:** Incomplete or defective eviction proceedings can mean former tenants retain colorable rights; any buyer inheriting active UDs needs full status.
- **Typical form:** Court UD filings; judgment and writ of possession; sheriff lockout records.
- **Where to find it:** Court clerk; OKOA legal files.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (active UD); high (recently completed UD)

### 15.05 Post-Foreclosure Property Condition Survey
- **What:** Condition survey conducted immediately after OKOA took possession, documenting the property's state at that time (vandalism, stripped fixtures, deferred maintenance, missing equipment).
- **Why it matters:** Documents what OKOA inherited vs. what it caused; establishes baseline for deferred maintenance attributable to the prior owner's default period.
- **Typical form:** Walk-through inspection report with photos; OKOA asset manager survey notes.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA asset management; property manager.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.06 Personal Property and Fixture Documentation
- **What:** Inventory of personal property and fixtures that remain with the real property, plus documentation of items removed by the prior owner/tenant (especially fixtures that may have been stripped).
- **Why it matters:** Disputes over what constitutes a fixture vs. personal property are common post-foreclosure; a documented inventory protects OKOA from buyer claims.
- **Typical form:** Fixture inventory list with photos; police report (if theft occurred).
- **Where to find it:** OKOA property manager; OKOA legal files.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 15.07 Prior Loan Documents (Summary Only — Not Full Diligence)
- **What:** Summary of the original loan (loan amount, origination date, maturity date, default date) and the foreclosure trigger event (the "what happened" narrative). Not a full loan file — just enough context for buyer questions.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers will ask how the asset came to OKOA; having a clean narrative prevents speculation and addresses questions about the prior operator's stewardship.
- **Typical form:** One-page REO summary memo; OKOA investment committee note.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA credit files; OKOA asset management.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 15.08 Bankruptcy Proceedings (if Prior Borrower Filed)
- **What:** Documentation of any bankruptcy filed by the prior borrower or guarantor, including case number, chapter, discharge or dismissal status, and any automatic stay periods affecting the foreclosure timeline.
- **Why it matters:** Bankruptcy can create title complications; buyers' title insurers will want to confirm that the foreclosure was not conducted in violation of the automatic stay.
- **Typical form:** Bankruptcy court docket; OKOA counsel confirmation of proper process; title company underwriting memo.
- **Where to find it:** PACER (federal court); OKOA legal files.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (if bankruptcy occurred); not applicable otherwise

### 15.09 Receiver's Reports and Accounting (if Receivership was Ordered)
- **What:** Court-appointed receiver's reports, income and expense accountings, and final receiver discharge order.
- **Why it matters:** If a receiver operated the property pre-foreclosure, their accounting establishes the financial baseline and reveals any anomalies in the property's operations during that period.
- **Typical form:** Receiver reports; court-filed accountings; discharge order.
- **Where to find it:** Court docket; OKOA legal files; receiver's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high (if receivership was ordered); not applicable otherwise

### 15.10 REO Rehabilitation Budget and Completion Status
- **What:** OKOA's REO rehabilitation budget (scope, contractor bids, permit status), actual spend to date, and current completion percentage.
- **Why it matters:** Buyers of REO-in-progress assets need to know what work is done, what is contracted but incomplete, and what is unstarted — to underwrite remaining capex and timeline.
- **Typical form:** Rehabilitation budget spreadsheet; draw log; percentage-complete schedule.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA development / asset management.
- **Severity if absent:** high (REO with active rehab); medium otherwise

## Category 16 — Property Sale Transaction Documents

### 16.01 Purchase and Sale Agreement (PSA) / Letter of Intent (LOI)
- **What:** Executed PSA or LOI (during LOI phase) governing the sale terms: purchase price, earnest money, diligence period, closing conditions, representations and warranties, and indemnification provisions.
- **Why it matters:** The PSA is the governing transaction document; its terms determine OKOA's obligations, risk exposure, and timeline.
- **Typical form:** Negotiated PSA; AICR or custom form.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal counsel; listing broker.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.02 Seller's Representations and Warranties Schedule
- **What:** The R&W schedule attached to the PSA, itemizing all affirmative representations made by OKOA regarding title, litigation, leases, environmental, compliance, and other material matters.
- **Why it matters:** R&Ws create liability exposure for OKOA post-close; they must be drafted carefully and supported by the diligence materials in the data room.
- **Typical form:** Exhibit to PSA.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.03 Seller's Disclosure Schedules
- **What:** Disclosure schedules supplementing the PSA's R&Ws, itemizing known exceptions, violations, pending litigation, environmental conditions, and material facts.
- **Why it matters:** Proper disclosure schedules protect OKOA from post-close R&W claims by placing buyers on notice of known conditions.
- **Typical form:** Exhibit to PSA (schedule of exceptions).
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 16.04 As-Is / Where-Is Disclaimer and Buyer Acknowledgment
- **What:** Express "as-is, where-is" sale provision and buyer's written acknowledgment of the condition of the property and waiver of reliance on seller representations.
- **Why it matters:** As-is provisions protect OKOA (especially in REO) from post-close claims of undisclosed defects; buyer acknowledgment strengthens enforceability.
- **Typical form:** PSA provision; standalone acknowledgment letter.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (REO); high (non-distressed)

### 16.05 Escrow Instructions
- **What:** Executed escrow instructions signed by buyer and seller, specifying closing conditions, prorations, holdbacks, and escrow holder duties.
- **Why it matters:** Escrow instructions govern the mechanics of closing; inconsistency between escrow instructions and the PSA can create delays or disputes.
- **Typical form:** Escrow company standard form with riders.
- **Where to find it:** Escrow company; OKOA legal counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.06 Closing Checklist and Condition Precedent Tracker
- **What:** Transaction management checklist tracking all closing conditions, diligence deliveries, third-party approvals, and document execution status.
- **Why it matters:** Manages deal process and ensures no closing condition is missed; gaps can delay or kill a transaction.
- **Typical form:** Excel closing checklist; deal management platform tracker.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA legal counsel; listing broker; escrow company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.07 FIRPTA Certification or Withholding Documentation
- **What:** Seller's FIRPTA certificate (IRS Form 8288-B withholding certificate or seller's affidavit of non-foreign status) confirming that OKOA is not a foreign person subject to withholding.
- **Why it matters:** FIRPTA withholding (15% of gross sales price) applies if the seller is a foreign person; failure to certify or withhold creates buyer liability with the IRS.
- **Typical form:** IRS Form 8288-B; seller's affidavit of non-foreign status.
- **Where to find it:** OKOA tax counsel; escrow company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 16.08 1031 Exchange Documentation (if Applicable)
- **What:** Qualified intermediary (QI) appointment, exchange agreement, and identification and exchange period tracking documentation.
- **Why it matters:** If OKOA is executing a 1031 exchange, the timing and procedural requirements are strict; errors result in loss of tax-deferral.
- **Typical form:** QI exchange agreement; 45-day ID notice; 180-day exchange period log.
- **Where to find it:** Qualified intermediary; OKOA tax counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high (if 1031 planned); not applicable otherwise

### 16.09 Natural Hazard Disclosure (NHD) Report (California)
- **What:** State-mandated natural hazard disclosure report confirming whether the property is in a fire hazard severity zone, flood zone, seismic zone, liquefaction zone, or landslide zone.
- **Why it matters:** California law requires NHD delivery before close; failure to deliver is a per se seller liability; other states have equivalent disclosure requirements.
- **Typical form:** NHD report (Homeowners Natural Hazard Disclosure or commercial equivalent).
- **Where to find it:** NHD provider (JCP LGS, PropertyID, or equivalent); title company.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (California residential and commercial); high (other NHD states)

### 16.10 Transfer Disclosure Statement (TDS) or Equivalent (Residential)
- **What:** State-mandated transfer disclosure form (California TDS, or equivalent in other states) for residential 1–4 unit properties, documenting known defects and material facts.
- **Why it matters:** TDS is a non-waivable statutory requirement for California residential sales; failure to deliver creates rescission rights for the buyer.
- **Typical form:** California TDS form (or state equivalent).
- **Where to find it:** California Association of Realtors forms; OKOA legal counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical (1–4 unit residential); not applicable otherwise

## Property-Type Considerations

The following items supplement the base checklist for specific product types. The skill's Phase 7a tailoring layer applies these based on the deal's property type flag.

### Multifamily Supplements
- Rent-controlled unit count and current allowable rent schedule (jurisdictions with rent control).
- Just-cause eviction policy documentation and tenant notification records.
- Section 8 / HUD contracts and HAP agreements (if subsidized units exist).
- Affordable housing covenant or LIHTC compliance file (if restricted units exist).
- Move-in/move-out inspection records and security deposit accounting by unit.
- Pet policy and deposits; pet fee income documentation.

### Office Supplements
- Tenant improvement allowance pipeline schedule for upcoming lease renewals.
- Expense stop and base year analysis for gross or modified gross leases.
- Parking ratio documentation and parking income breakdown.
- Building efficiency ratio (rentable to usable) certification.
- Tenant communication records regarding building services and complaints.

### Retail Supplements
- Anchor tenant sales performance data (if available).
- Signage restrictions and criteria document.
- Customer traffic count data (if available from anchor or prior operator).
- Outparcel or ground lease documentation.
- Landlord's exclusive use audit — confirm no current violations.

### Industrial Supplements
- Dock high / grade level / drive-in door count and clear height certification.
- Truck court depth and trailer storage capacity documentation.
- Rail spur documentation and switching agreements.
- Hazardous material storage permits (if tenants use hazmat).
- Power availability documentation (heavy industrial uses).

### Hospitality Supplements
- Brand's property improvement plan (PIP) and compliance status.
- Management company agreement (if separate from franchise).
- Guest satisfaction scores (RevPAR Index / STR quality scores).
- Food and beverage profit and loss separate from rooms revenue.
- Group booking commitments and group pace report.

### Land Supplements
- Soils report / percolation test results.
- Utility availability letters (Will Serve) from all providers.
- Traffic study (if entitlement required one).
- Preliminary grading and drainage plan.
- Phase I ESA (required even for unimproved land).
