# Base Checklist — Foreclosure Auction

> **Scope:** Outbound diligence supporting a foreclosure / trustee sale auction.
> **Role:** Prior — applied via Phase 7a tailoring to produce per-deal scope.
> **Default action:** Every item is `in_scope` until tailoring marks it otherwise.
> **Jurisdiction note:** Cover both non-judicial (trustee sale) and judicial foreclosure items. Tailoring marks the inapplicable set as `not_applicable`.

---

## Severity Rubric

- `critical` — Without this, the sale is procedurally vulnerable to challenge or void.
- `high` — Required for a defensible sale.
- `medium` — Reasonable diligence ask.
- `low` — Optional but strengthens the record.

## Category 01 — Default Declaration & Notice of Default

### 01.01 Loan Agreement — Executed Copy
- **What:** The original promissory note and loan agreement evidencing the debt obligation.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes the lender's standing to foreclose and the contractual basis for default.
- **Typical form:** Executed promissory note; deed of trust or mortgage.
- **Where to find it:** Loan origination file; county recorder (trust deed).
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.02 Notice of Default — Recorded Copy with Recording Stamp
- **What:** The formal recorded instrument declaring the borrower in default under the deed of trust or mortgage.
- **Why it matters:** The NOD is the statutory trigger that starts the cure and notice periods. A defective or unrecorded NOD voids the sale in most non-judicial states.
- **Typical form:** Recorded Notice of Default (Form varies by state; e.g., California Civil Code §2924).
- **Where to find it:** County recorder's office; title plant.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.03 Declaration of Default — Signed by Lender or Authorized Agent
- **What:** Internal or third-party declaration certifying the specific events of default (payment default, covenant breach, maturity, etc.).
- **Why it matters:** Creates the documentary foundation for the NOD; must align with the default identified in the NOD.
- **Typical form:** Affidavit or declaration signed by a loan officer or servicer representative.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer file; trustee file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.04 Loan History Showing Default, Cure Failure, Acceleration
- **What:** Full payment ledger or loan history from origination through the date of NOD showing missed payments, applied late charges, and failed cure attempts.
- **Why it matters:** Bidders and courts require evidence that the default is real, persistent, and that cure deadlines passed without resolution.
- **Typical form:** Servicer payment history report; loan accounting ledger.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer system of record; loan servicing file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 01.05 Cure Period Calculation Memo
- **What:** Written analysis confirming that the statutory cure period has fully elapsed before the Notice of Sale was issued.
- **Why it matters:** Premature issuance of the Notice of Sale is a common challenge ground that can void the sale.
- **Typical form:** Paralegal or trustee memo with date calculations.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file; foreclosure counsel file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.06 Reinstatement Demand Letter (If Issued)
- **What:** Any written reinstatement figure provided to the borrower during the cure window.
- **Why it matters:** Documents what the borrower was offered to cure, supports the cure failure narrative.
- **Typical form:** Reinstatement demand letter on servicer or counsel letterhead.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer file; correspondence log.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.07 Breach Letter / Event of Default Notice to Borrower (Pre-NOD)
- **What:** Any pre-NOD written notice to the borrower identifying the default and demanding cure before formal acceleration.
- **Why it matters:** Some states and loan agreements require a contractual pre-NOD notice period. Omitting it can expose the sale to challenge.
- **Typical form:** Demand letter; notice of default under loan documents.
- **Where to find it:** Correspondence file; servicer file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.08 Default Interest Accrual Ledger
- **What:** A running ledger tracking the accrual of default-rate interest from the date of default through the anticipated sale date, reconciled to the loan agreement's default interest provision.
- **Why it matters:** Default interest is frequently challenged. A pre-built accrual ledger tied to the note demonstrates the calculation basis and reduces the surface area for dispute.
- **Typical form:** Servicer interest accrual spreadsheet; counsel-prepared schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 01.09 Late Fee Accrual Schedule
- **What:** Itemized schedule of all late fees charged from the first missed payment through the NOD date, tied to the note's late fee provision (rate, grace period, cap if any).
- **Why it matters:** Late fees may be capped or limited by state statute or the loan documents. An un-itemized late fee lump sum is a common borrower challenge point.
- **Typical form:** Servicer fee schedule; loan history extract.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer system.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 01.10 Pre-Foreclosure Loss Mitigation Outreach Log
- **What:** Documentation of any pre-foreclosure loss mitigation outreach to the borrower — required for HUD-insured loans and prudent best practice for any owner-occupied property, regardless of insurance status.
- **Why it matters:** Some states have mandatory pre-foreclosure counseling or outreach requirements. Even where not required, a documented outreach record insulates OKOA against lender liability claims and provides evidence of good faith.
- **Typical form:** Call log; letter log; HUD counseling referral confirmation.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer file; lender compliance file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 02 — Acceleration & Payoff Demand History

### 02.01 Acceleration Letter
- **What:** The lender's written notice to the borrower declaring the entire loan balance immediately due and payable.
- **Why it matters:** Acceleration is a prerequisite to foreclosure in most jurisdictions. Without it, the lender may only be entitled to past-due installments, not the full balance.
- **Typical form:** Acceleration notice on lender or servicer letterhead, referencing specific loan and default events.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer file; foreclosure counsel file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.02 Proof of Delivery of Acceleration Letter
- **What:** Evidence that the acceleration letter was delivered to the borrower (certified mail return receipt, process server affidavit, email confirmation if permitted).
- **Why it matters:** Delivery is required for the notice to be effective in most states.
- **Typical form:** USPS certified mail receipt; process server affidavit.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer file; counsel file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.03 Payoff Demand — Full Payoff as of Sale Date
- **What:** A formal payoff demand stating the total amount required to satisfy the loan in full, itemized by principal, interest, default interest, fees, and costs through a stated per diem.
- **Why it matters:** Establishes the floor for the credit bid calculation and gives bidders the data they need to underwrite the purchase price.
- **Typical form:** Payoff demand letter or payoff statement.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; lender accounting.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.04 Payoff Demand History Log
- **What:** Record of all prior payoff demands issued, including dates and amounts, to show continuity of the debt and absence of fraudulent numbers.
- **Why it matters:** Borrowers sometimes challenge payoff amounts as inflated; a history log demonstrates consistency.
- **Typical form:** Servicer payoff log; correspondence log.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer system.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 02.05 Senior Lien Payoff Demand (If Foreclosing Junior)
- **What:** Where OKOA holds a junior lien and is foreclosing subject to a senior lien, the payoff demand or current balance statement for the senior lien.
- **Why it matters:** Bidders must understand the senior encumbrance they are taking subject to. Failure to disclose is a material omission.
- **Typical form:** Senior lender payoff statement; tax record showing senior lien amount.
- **Where to find it:** Senior lender; title commitment Schedule B.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 02.06 Per-Diem Accrual Worksheet Through Anticipated Sale Date
- **What:** A daily accrual worksheet showing principal balance, note-rate per diem, default-rate per diem, and running totals projected through the expected sale date, with a column for each postponement scenario.
- **Why it matters:** Sale postponements extend the accrual window and can meaningfully change the credit bid. Pre-building the per-diem worksheet eliminates recalculation delays on sale day and gives bidders current payoff context.
- **Typical form:** Counsel or servicer spreadsheet with selectable sale date.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.07 Costs-of-Sale Itemization — Pre-Sale Estimate
- **What:** A pre-sale itemization of all expected costs of sale (trustee fee, publication costs, posting costs, attorney's fees, title update, service of process) that will be added to the credit bid, with citations to the contractual or statutory authority for each line item.
- **Why it matters:** Only authorized costs may be added to the credit bid. An advance itemization confirms that every included cost has a contractual or statutory basis, reducing challenge exposure at and after the sale.
- **Typical form:** Trustee fee schedule; counsel cost estimate memorandum.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 02.08 SCRA — Servicemembers Civil Relief Act Search
- **What:** A search confirming whether any borrower, co-borrower, or guarantor is an active-duty servicemember entitled to protections under the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (50 U.S.C. §3953), including the requirement for a court order before a non-judicial foreclosure may proceed against a servicemember.
- **Why it matters:** Foreclosing against an active-duty servicemember without a court order is a federal violation that voids the sale and exposes OKOA to significant liability, including punitive damages and attorney's fees.
- **Typical form:** SCRA search via Defense Manpower Data Center (DMDC); counsel certification.
- **Where to find it:** DMDC website (servicememberscivilreliefact.com or dmdc.osd.mil); foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

## Category 03 — Notice of Sale

### 03.01 Notice of Sale — Recorded Copy with Recording Stamp
- **What:** The formal recorded instrument setting the date, time, and location of the trustee or sheriff sale.
- **Why it matters:** Recordation of the NOS is a mandatory statutory step in non-judicial states. Failure voids the sale.
- **Typical form:** Recorded Notice of Trustee's Sale (non-judicial); Notice of Sheriff's Sale (judicial).
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; trustee file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.02 Affidavit of Publication
- **What:** A sworn affidavit from the newspaper publisher confirming the Notice of Sale was published in a newspaper of general circulation for the required number of weeks and intervals.
- **Why it matters:** Statutory publication requirements are strictly construed. A defective affidavit creates a voidable sale.
- **Typical form:** Publisher's affidavit with attached tear-sheets or clippings.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file; publisher directly.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.03 Tear Sheets / Newspaper Clippings of Published Notice
- **What:** The actual published notices from the newspaper, confirming text and dates.
- **Why it matters:** Corroborates the affidavit of publication; confirms no material errors in the published notice text.
- **Typical form:** Photocopy or scan of newspaper pages.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file; publisher.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.04 Affidavit of Posting on Property and at Courthouse
- **What:** Sworn affidavit confirming that the Notice of Sale was physically posted on the subject property and at the courthouse or other statutorily required public location.
- **Why it matters:** Required in most non-judicial states (e.g., California, Utah). Failure to post invalidates the sale.
- **Typical form:** Affidavit of posting signed by process server or trustee.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 03.05 Photographs of Posted Notice (Property and Courthouse)
- **What:** Date-stamped photographs showing the posted notice on the property door/gate and at the courthouse.
- **Why it matters:** Photographic evidence corroborates the affidavit of posting and provides a defense exhibit if challenged.
- **Typical form:** Timestamped digital photographs.
- **Where to find it:** Process server; trustee file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.06 NOS Publication Compliance Memo
- **What:** Memo confirming that the publication schedule (number of insertions, intervals, final insertion date relative to sale date) meets the state statute.
- **Why it matters:** Timing errors (e.g., final insertion too close to sale date) are common challenge grounds.
- **Typical form:** Paralegal or counsel memo with statutory citation and date calculations.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.07 Notice of Sale — Text Review for Statutory Compliance
- **What:** A review confirming the NOS includes all required elements (property description, trustee name, lender name, sale location, time, contact for reinstatement, etc.) per the applicable statute.
- **Why it matters:** Missing required elements create voidability.
- **Typical form:** Counsel checklist or annotated NOS.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 03.08 Postponement / Continuance Records
- **What:** Complete records of every postponement of the sale, including: the date and method of each postponement announcement (oral at the sale site, recorded notice, or re-publication as required), the new sale date set at each postponement, and confirmation that no postponement exceeded the state's maximum continuance window without re-recording or re-publication.
- **Why it matters:** Each postponement must comply independently with the governing statute. A single defective postponement — wrong method, wrong location, or exceeding the maximum continuance period — can require the trustee to restart the entire notice process.
- **Typical form:** Trustee postponement log; affidavit of oral announcement; re-recorded NOS or re-publication affidavit if applicable.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 04 — Junior Lienholder & Required Party Notices

### 04.01 Junior Lienholder List with Confirmed Lien Positions
- **What:** A complete list of all junior lienholders (mechanics, judgment, subordinate deeds of trust, UCC filings, HOA liens) with confirmed recording dates and lien positions relative to OKOA's lien.
- **Why it matters:** Junior lienholders have statutory notice rights. Failure to notify a properly noticed junior lienholder can preserve their lien through the sale, impairing title.
- **Typical form:** Title search report; Schedule B of title commitment.
- **Where to find it:** Title company; county recorder search.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 04.02 Affidavit of Mailing to Borrower, Guarantor, Junior Lienholders
- **What:** Sworn affidavit from the trustee or its agent confirming that the NOD and NOS were mailed to the borrower, all guarantors, and all junior lienholders of record by certified mail to their last known addresses.
- **Why it matters:** Failure to mail to a junior lienholder of record can result in that lien surviving the sale. This is the single most litigated defect in non-judicial foreclosures.
- **Typical form:** Affidavit of mailing with attached certified mail tracking receipts.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 04.03 Certified Mail Receipts / Tracking Confirmations for Junior Notices
- **What:** USPS certified mail receipts or electronic tracking records confirming delivery (or attempted delivery) to each junior lienholder.
- **Why it matters:** Corroborates the affidavit of mailing. Courts have voided sales where mailing affidavits lacked supporting delivery evidence.
- **Typical form:** USPS certified mail receipts; tracking printouts.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file; USPS tracking records.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.04 HOA / Condominium Association Lien Search and Notice
- **What:** Search for any HOA or condominium association assessment liens and confirmation that the association was notified per statute.
- **Why it matters:** HOA super-priority lien statutes (e.g., Nevada NRS 116; limited super-priority in other states) can survive foreclosure if not addressed.
- **Typical form:** HOA lien search certificate; certified mail to HOA.
- **Where to find it:** HOA records; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.05 IRS / Federal Tax Lien Search and Notice
- **What:** Search for any federal tax liens against the borrower or property, and confirmation that the IRS was notified per 26 U.S.C. §7425 if a lien was found.
- **Why it matters:** Federal tax liens have a 120-day redemption right post-sale. If the IRS was not properly notified, the federal lien may survive the sale.
- **Typical form:** IRS lien search (UCC/lien search); Form 14135 or certified notice to IRS.
- **Where to find it:** Title company; IRS.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 04.06 Mechanics Lien Search and Notice
- **What:** Search for any recorded mechanics or materialmen's liens on the property, with notice to lienholders if required.
- **Why it matters:** Mechanics liens recorded before the trust deed can have priority; those recorded after are junior but still require notice.
- **Typical form:** Mechanics lien search; certified mail to lienholders.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder search; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 04.07 UCC Financing Statement Search (Fixture Filings)
- **What:** Search for any UCC fixture filings that may affect personal property fixtures on the real property.
- **Why it matters:** Equipment lenders with UCC fixture filings may have competing claims to collateral. Bidders need to know what they are acquiring.
- **Typical form:** UCC lien search certificate from Secretary of State.
- **Where to find it:** State Secretary of State; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 04.08 Judgment Lien Search and Notice
- **What:** Search for any judgment liens against the borrower recorded against the property, with notice to judgment creditors.
- **Why it matters:** Judgment liens are junior interests that require notice and are extinguished by sale only if properly noticed.
- **Typical form:** Judgment lien search; certified mail to judgment creditors.
- **Where to find it:** County clerk; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 05 — Borrower & Guarantor Notices

### 05.01 Affidavit of Mailing — Borrower Copy
- **What:** Specific confirmation that the borrower (and any co-borrowers) received the NOD and NOS by certified mail at their last known address.
- **Why it matters:** Borrower notice is a due process and statutory requirement; failure can void the sale or expose OKOA to damages.
- **Typical form:** Component of the master affidavit of mailing; separate certified mail receipt.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.02 Guarantor Notice — NOD and NOS
- **What:** Proof that all guarantors of the loan received notice of the NOD and NOS.
- **Why it matters:** Many states require notice to guarantors. Failure can bar a deficiency claim against the guarantor even if not fatal to the sale itself.
- **Typical form:** Certified mail to guarantor; component of affidavit of mailing.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file; lender file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 05.03 Bankruptcy Check on Borrower (PACER Search)
- **What:** A PACER search confirming that no borrower, co-borrower, or guarantor has filed for bankruptcy protection that would trigger an automatic stay.
- **Why it matters:** Proceeding with a foreclosure sale in violation of the automatic stay renders the sale void or voidable and exposes OKOA to sanctions under 11 U.S.C. §362.
- **Typical form:** PACER case search printout dated within 24–48 hours of the sale.
- **Where to find it:** PACER (pacer.gov).
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.04 Bankruptcy Stay Relief Order (If Prior Filing Exists)
- **What:** If the borrower has filed bankruptcy previously or currently, the court order granting OKOA relief from the automatic stay.
- **Why it matters:** Without a stay relief order, any sale is void as a matter of federal law.
- **Typical form:** Signed bankruptcy court order granting relief from stay.
- **Where to find it:** Bankruptcy case docket (PACER); foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.05 Borrower Contact Log / Workout Attempt Record
- **What:** A log of all borrower communications during the default period, including any workout discussions, forbearance negotiations, or written communications.
- **Why it matters:** Some states require good-faith workout efforts before foreclosure (e.g., California SB 818 / Homeowner Bill of Rights for residential). Also protects against claims of lender liability.
- **Typical form:** Call logs; email chains; workout proposal correspondence.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer file; lender file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 05.06 Loss Mitigation / Forbearance Offer Documentation
- **What:** Written evidence of any forbearance, loan modification, or loss mitigation offers made to the borrower and the borrower's response.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates lender compliance with any statutory or regulatory loss mitigation obligations, particularly for residential properties.
- **Typical form:** Forbearance agreement; modification proposal letter.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer file.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 05.07 SCRA — Servicemembers Civil Relief Act Search and Certification
- **What:** Defense Manpower Data Center search confirming whether the borrower, co-borrower, or guarantor is an active-duty servicemember, plus counsel certification of SCRA compliance status (court order obtained if required, or no military status found).
- **Why it matters:** Non-judicial foreclosure against an active-duty servicemember without a court order is a federal statutory violation that voids the sale. The SCRA search must be dated immediately before sale.
- **Typical form:** DMDC search printout; counsel certification memo.
- **Where to find it:** DMDC website; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 05.08 Death-of-Borrower Documentation (If Applicable)
- **What:** If the borrower entity or individual has died or been dissolved, documentation confirming the successor, personal representative, or estate trustee, along with notice to such parties and analysis of any acceleration-on-death clause or due-on-sale trigger.
- **Why it matters:** Foreclosing against a deceased individual or dissolved entity without proper notice to the estate or successor can create due process defects that impair the sale. Successor trustee changes also require analysis of the guaranty chain.
- **Typical form:** Death certificate or entity dissolution certificate; probate filing; notice to estate representative.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; Secretary of State; probate court; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 06 — Court Filings — Judicial Foreclosures

> Items 06.01–06.09 apply to judicial foreclosure states (Florida, Illinois, New York, New Jersey, and others). Mark `not_applicable` for non-judicial trustee sale states after tailoring.

### 06.01 Foreclosure Complaint — Filed and File-Stamped Copy
- **What:** The initial pleading filed with the court to commence the judicial foreclosure action.
- **Why it matters:** The complaint is the foundation of the judicial proceeding. A defective complaint may be dismissed or delay the sale.
- **Typical form:** Court-filed complaint for foreclosure of mortgage.
- **Where to find it:** Court docket (PACER or state court system); foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.02 Lis Pendens — Recorded Copy with Recording Stamp
- **What:** A recorded notice that a lawsuit affecting the title to the property is pending.
- **Why it matters:** The lis pendens provides constructive notice to all subsequent purchasers and lienholders. Without it, a bona fide purchaser could take free of the foreclosure.
- **Typical form:** Recorded lis pendens.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; court file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.03 Summons and Proof of Service on All Defendants
- **What:** The court-issued summons and proof of personal service (or service by publication if personal service failed) on the borrower and all named defendants.
- **Why it matters:** Due process requires service on all parties whose interests are being foreclosed. Failure to serve a junior lienholder leaves their lien intact after sale.
- **Typical form:** Executed summons with return of service or affidavit of service.
- **Where to find it:** Court docket; process server.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.04 Default Judgment or Final Judgment of Foreclosure
- **What:** The court's final judgment establishing the amount owed and ordering the foreclosure sale.
- **Why it matters:** The judgment is the legal authority for the sale. Without it, the sale has no judicial foundation.
- **Typical form:** Signed and filed Final Judgment of Foreclosure.
- **Where to find it:** Court docket; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.05 Order Setting Sale Date
- **What:** The court order specifying the date, time, and location of the judicial foreclosure sale (sheriff's sale or clerk's sale).
- **Why it matters:** The sale must be conducted on the court-ordered date and location. Deviations require further court order.
- **Typical form:** Court order scheduling foreclosure sale.
- **Where to find it:** Court docket.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.06 Publication of Sale Notice — Judicial Proceeding
- **What:** Affidavit of publication and tear sheets confirming publication of the judicial sale notice per court order and statute.
- **Why it matters:** Judicial sale notice publication requirements are separately mandated; failure can invalidate the sale even with a valid judgment.
- **Typical form:** Publisher's affidavit with tear sheets.
- **Where to find it:** Sheriff's office; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 06.07 Sheriff's / Clerk's Sale Certificate
- **What:** The official certificate issued by the sheriff or clerk documenting the sale event, sale price, and successful bidder.
- **Why it matters:** The certificate is the primary record of the judicial sale and the basis for issuance of the deed after any redemption period.
- **Typical form:** Certificate of Sale signed by the sheriff or clerk.
- **Where to find it:** Sheriff's office; court file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 06.08 Deficiency Judgment — If Sought (Judicial States)
- **What:** A separate judgment for the deficiency balance if the sale proceeds are less than the judgment amount.
- **Why it matters:** Deficiency rights and procedures vary widely by state. Some states prohibit deficiencies in certain circumstances. This affects the lender's loss position and the clean-up of the deal.
- **Typical form:** Application for deficiency judgment; court order.
- **Where to find it:** Court docket; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 06.09 Court-Confirmed Sale Order (States Requiring Confirmation)
- **What:** In states that require judicial confirmation of the sale (e.g., Illinois, some California commercial situations), the court order confirming the sale.
- **Why it matters:** Until confirmed, the sale is not final. The deed cannot be issued without confirmation.
- **Typical form:** Court order confirming sale.
- **Where to find it:** Court docket.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

## Category 07 — Trustee / Sheriff Sale Procedures

### 07.01 Trustee Appointment — Original Deed of Trust Naming Trustee
- **What:** The original trust deed showing the named trustee, or successor trustee appointment if the original trustee was replaced.
- **Why it matters:** Only the properly appointed trustee has authority to conduct the sale. An unauthorized trustee renders the sale void.
- **Typical form:** Recorded deed of trust; recorded Substitution of Trustee.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.02 Substitution of Trustee — Recorded Copy with Recording Stamp
- **What:** If the original trustee was replaced, the recorded instrument appointing a successor trustee, signed by the lender of record.
- **Why it matters:** The substitution must be properly executed and recorded before the NOD is filed (in some states) or before the sale. A defective substitution voids the trustee's authority.
- **Typical form:** Recorded Substitution of Trustee.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; trustee file.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.03 Trustee's Authorization to Proceed Letter
- **What:** OKOA's written instruction to the trustee authorizing commencement and continuation of foreclosure proceedings.
- **Why it matters:** Documents the lender's direction to the trustee; protects both parties.
- **Typical form:** Authorization letter or instruction letter on lender letterhead.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file; lender file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.04 Sale Procedures Handout for Bidders
- **What:** A written document distributed to prospective bidders at the sale describing the rules of the auction (opening bid, bidding increments, deposit requirements, payment terms, as-is condition, no representations).
- **Why it matters:** Clear sale procedures reduce disputes and protect the trustee and lender against post-sale claims of irregular conduct.
- **Typical form:** One-page or two-page sale rules handout.
- **Where to find it:** Prepared by trustee or lender's counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.05 Bidder Qualification Process Documentation
- **What:** Description of how OKOA or the trustee qualifies bidders prior to the sale (proof of funds, deposit requirements, registration).
- **Why it matters:** Unqualified winning bidders can disrupt the sale and cause delays. A documented qualification process protects the process integrity.
- **Typical form:** Bidder qualification form; proof of funds requirement notice.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; lender.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 07.06 Cashier's Check / Wire Instructions for Deposits
- **What:** Written instructions specifying the required deposit amount, acceptable form of payment (cashier's check, wire), and deadline for deposit by the winning bidder.
- **Why it matters:** Ambiguity about deposit requirements leads to failed sales and forfeited deposits. Clear written instructions protect the process.
- **Typical form:** Deposit requirement notice; wire instructions sheet.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; lender.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 07.07 Postponement Log (If Sale Was Postponed)
- **What:** A log of any postponements of the sale, including the statutory method of announcement (oral announcement at the sale location on the original sale date), and re-noticing if required.
- **Why it matters:** Improper postponements are a common basis for challenging the sale. Most non-judicial states permit only oral announcement postponements up to a maximum period.
- **Typical form:** Trustee postponement log; oral announcement affidavit.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee file.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.08 Sale Location Confirmation
- **What:** Written confirmation that the sale will be held at the statutory location (courthouse steps, designated county building, or other location specified in the NOS).
- **Why it matters:** Sale at an unauthorized location is void.
- **Typical form:** NOS (which specifies location); trustee confirmation letter.
- **Where to find it:** NOS; trustee.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 07.09 Trustee Errors-and-Omissions Insurance / Bond Verification
- **What:** Confirmation that the conducting trustee (or foreclosure trustee company) maintains errors-and-omissions insurance or a surety bond in the amount required by state law or by OKOA's vendor standards.
- **Why it matters:** Some states require licensed trustee companies to maintain a minimum bond. Beyond statutory requirements, trustee E&O coverage protects OKOA from losses caused by trustee procedural errors that expose the sale to challenge.

- **Where to find it:** Trustee; OKOA vendor management file.

### 07.10 Sale Conduct Script / Call Sheet

## Category 08 — Title at Sale

- **What:** A current title insurance commitment from a licensed title company reflecting all encumbrances, liens, and title exceptions as of a date within 30 days of the foreclosure sale.

- **Where to find it:** Title company.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 08.03 Lien Priority Analysis Memo
- **What:** A written analysis confirming the position of OKOA's lien relative to all other encumbrances and identifying which liens will be extinguished by the sale and which survive.

- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 08.04 Deed of Trust / Mortgage — Recorded Copy with Recording Stamp
- **What:** The recorded deed of trust or mortgage being foreclosed, confirming its recording date and book/page or document number.
- **Why it matters:** Priority is generally determined by recording date. The recorded instrument is the bidder's chain-of-title foundation.
- **Typical form:** Recorded deed of trust or mortgage.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 08.05 Chain of Title Report
- **What:** A full chain of title from at least the last 30 years showing all recorded conveyances, encumbrances, and releases.

- **Where to find it:** Title company; title plant.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.06 Wraparound or All-Inclusive Deed of Trust Analysis
- **What:** If the property has a wraparound or all-inclusive deed of trust (AITD), an analysis of how the foreclosing lien interacts with the AITD structure.

- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 08.07 Tax Lien and Assessment Lien Search
- **What:** Search confirming property tax payment status and any outstanding tax lien assessments.
- **Why it matters:** Property tax liens in most states have statutory super-priority and survive the foreclosure sale. Bidders must know the outstanding tax balance.
- **Typical form:** County assessor / treasurer search; tax certificate.

- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 08.08 Easement and CCR Review
- **What:** Review of all recorded easements, covenants, conditions, and restrictions (CCRs) affecting the property.
- **Why it matters:** Easements and CCRs survive the foreclosure sale and affect property use and value. Bidders and buyers must understand these encumbrances.

- **Where to find it:** County recorder; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 08.09 Bankruptcy Lift-of-Stay Order — Title Impact (If Borrower Filed BK)
- **What:** Where the borrower filed bankruptcy during the foreclosure, confirmation that the stay relief order is recorded or otherwise noted in the title chain and that the sale conducted post-stay-relief is insurable by the title company.
- **Why it matters:** A sale conducted after bankruptcy stay relief may still face title company underwriting requirements before they will insure. Having this analysis in the data room accelerates post-sale title insurance issuance for the buyer.
- **Typical form:** Recorded certified copy of stay relief order; title company underwriting approval memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel; title company.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 11 — Redemption Rights Analysis

### 11.01 Statutory Redemption Period Analysis (State-Specific)
- **What:** A written analysis of the state-specific statutory redemption period, if any, including the duration, who may redeem (borrower, junior lienholders), the amount required to redeem, and the deadline.
- **Why it matters:** A buyer at foreclosure may not receive clear title until the redemption period expires. This directly affects the buyer's ability to sell, refinance, or begin improvements.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo with statutory citation; state-specific redemption summary.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 11.02 Equitable Redemption Analysis
- **What:** Analysis of whether the borrower retains any equitable right to redeem the property even if no statutory redemption period applies (generally extinguished at the moment of the foreclosure sale in non-judicial states but relevant in judicial states before the sale is confirmed).
- **Why it matters:** Equitable redemption rights affect the timing of final title transfer.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 11.03 Junior Lienholder Redemption Rights Analysis
- **What:** Analysis of whether any junior lienholders have statutory redemption rights post-sale and the priority order for redemption.
- **Why it matters:** Junior lienholders in some states may redeem to protect their interests. Bidders need to understand this risk.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 11.04 IRS Redemption Right — 120-Day Window
- **What:** Specific analysis of whether the IRS holds a tax lien that was properly noticed (triggering a 120-day federal redemption right under 26 U.S.C. §7425(d)).
- **Why it matters:** If the IRS exercised its right, the bidder would be required to sell the property to the United States at the sale price. Buyers must understand this exposure.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo; IRS notification confirmation.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel; IRS.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 11.05 Redemption Period Calendar / Timeline
- **What:** A visual or written timeline mapping the statutory redemption period from the sale date to the date of clear title.
- **Why it matters:** Bidders need to understand the exact window before they can act with certainty on the acquired property.
- **Typical form:** Calendar or timeline prepared by counsel or trustee.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel; trustee.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 11.06 Per-State Redemption Period Summary Table
- **What:** A one-page table summarizing the redemption period rules for the relevant state(s) of operation — duration, who may redeem (borrower vs. junior lienholder vs. IRS), redemption amount formula, and whether possession transfers before or after the period expires.
- **Why it matters:** OKOA's portfolio spans multiple states. A standing summary table gives deal teams, investors, and bidders an immediate reference without requiring a fresh memo for each transaction. Errors in redemption period advice have caused buyers to make improvements on unredeemed properties and lose them to redeeming borrowers.
- **Typical form:** Counsel-prepared reference table; internal policy document.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel; OKOA legal department.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 11.07 Redemption Escrow Account Setup (If State Requires)
- **What:** Confirmation that any redemption escrow account required by state law has been established and funded, with the correct redemption amount held in trust and available to be disbursed to a redeeming party within the statutory deadline.
- **Why it matters:** Some states require the foreclosing lender or trustee to maintain a redemption fund or escrow. Failure to establish the account can extend the redemption period or create liability if a valid redemption is improperly rejected.
- **Typical form:** Trustee escrow account confirmation; counsel memo on state-specific escrow requirement.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 11.08 Redemption Right Notice to Borrower and Juniors
- **What:** Written notice to the borrower and all junior lienholders of their redemption rights, the redemption amount, and the applicable deadline, delivered in the manner required by state statute.
- **Why it matters:** Some states require affirmative notice of redemption rights post-sale. Failure to give required notice can toll or extend the redemption period, leaving the buyer's title in limbo longer than expected.
- **Typical form:** Trustee notice letter; state-specific statutory form.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 12 — Payoff & Bid Calculation

### 12.01 Credit Bid Calculation Worksheet
- **What:** A detailed worksheet showing how OKOA calculated its credit bid, itemized by: unpaid principal balance (UPB), accrued interest at the note rate, default interest from the date of default, late charges, origination fees (if addable under the note), attorney's fees, trustee's fees, publication costs, and all other costs of sale.
- **Why it matters:** The credit bid establishes the opening bid. An inflated or incorrectly calculated credit bid is a basis to void the sale. An understated credit bid results in unnecessary loss to OKOA.
- **Typical form:** Internal worksheet prepared by the servicer or foreclosure counsel.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 12.02 Unpaid Principal Balance Confirmation
- **What:** The exact UPB as of the sale date, reconciled from the loan history.
- **Why it matters:** The UPB is the foundation of the credit bid. Errors cascade through the entire calculation.
- **Typical form:** Loan accounting statement.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 12.03 Accrued Interest Calculation — Note Rate
- **What:** Day-count calculation of interest accrued at the contract note rate from the last paid interest date through the sale date.
- **Why it matters:** Interest is the largest variable component of the payoff. An error in the accrual rate or day count is a common challenge ground.
- **Typical form:** Interest accrual schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 12.04 Default Interest Calculation
- **What:** Day-count calculation of default interest (at the rate specified in the loan agreement) from the default date through the sale date.
- **Why it matters:** Default interest rates are frequently higher than the note rate and are often the subject of borrower challenges. Clear documentation is essential.
- **Typical form:** Default interest accrual schedule.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 12.05 Fees and Costs of Sale Itemization
- **What:** Itemized list of all fees and costs added to the credit bid: trustee's fees, publication costs, attorney's fees, title search fees, service of process fees, court filing fees (judicial), and any other recoverable costs.
- **Why it matters:** Only contractually and statutorily authorized fees may be included in the credit bid. Unauthorized fees expose OKOA to a reduced credit bid or challenge.
- **Typical form:** Trustee fee statement; counsel fee invoice.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 12.06 Anti-Deficiency Statute Analysis
- **What:** Analysis of whether the applicable state's anti-deficiency statutes (e.g., California Code of Civil Procedure §580d; Utah Code §57-1-32) limit or eliminate OKOA's ability to pursue a deficiency judgment.
- **Why it matters:** Anti-deficiency statutes affect OKOA's loss position and the loan's expected recovery. This analysis is relevant to credit bid strategy.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 12.07 Bid Strategy Memo — Credit Bid vs. Third-Party Sale
- **What:** Internal memo documenting OKOA's decision to credit bid (take title) or to encourage third-party bidding, and the rationale.
- **Why it matters:** Documents the business decision; relevant to fiduciary duty analysis and investor reporting.
- **Typical form:** Internal memo; credit committee decision.
- **Where to find it:** Lender file; credit committee minutes.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 12.08 Per-Diem Accrual Worksheet Through Anticipated Sale Date
- **What:** A daily accrual worksheet showing principal balance, note-rate per diem, default-rate per diem, and running totals projected through the expected sale date with postponement scenarios.
- **Why it matters:** Sale postponements extend the accrual window and can meaningfully change the credit bid amount. Having a pre-built per-diem table eliminates day-of-sale recalculation delays and gives both OKOA's team and auction bidders access to current payoff context.
- **Typical form:** Servicer or counsel spreadsheet with selectable sale date.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

## Category 14 — Post-Sale Documents

### 14.01 Trustee's Deed — Template (Pre-Sale) / Executed Copy (Post-Sale)
- **What:** The deed conveying title from the trustee to the successful bidder following the foreclosure sale. Pre-sale, a template should be in the data room. Post-sale, the executed and recorded copy replaces it.
- **Why it matters:** The trustee's deed is the instrument of title transfer. Defects in the deed — wrong legal description, unauthorized execution, failure to recite statutory compliance — create title defects.
- **Typical form:** Trustee's Deed Upon Sale (non-judicial); Sheriff's Deed (judicial).
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 14.02 Trustee's Deed — Recorded Copy with Recording Stamp (Post-Sale)
- **What:** The executed and recorded trustee's deed confirming transfer of title to the purchaser.
- **Why it matters:** Title does not pass until the deed is recorded in most states. Recording is the final step in non-judicial foreclosure.
- **Typical form:** Recorded Trustee's Deed Upon Sale.
- **Where to find it:** County recorder; trustee.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 14.03 Sheriff's Deed / Clerk's Deed (Judicial States)
- **What:** In judicial foreclosure states, the deed issued by the sheriff or clerk of court conveying title to the successful bidder after the redemption period.
- **Why it matters:** Same function as the trustee's deed in non-judicial states — the instrument of title transfer.
- **Typical form:** Sheriff's Deed or Clerk's Certificate of Title.
- **Where to find it:** Sheriff's office; court clerk.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 14.04 Deed of Reconveyance / Release of Deed of Trust (If Credit Bid — OKOA Takes Title)
- **What:** If OKOA credit bids and takes title, a deed of reconveyance releasing the deed of trust from the chain of title (since the lien merges with the fee interest).
- **Why it matters:** Without a release, the deed of trust remains in the chain of title as a cloud, complicating OKOA's future sale or refinancing.
- **Typical form:** Deed of Reconveyance or Release of Deed of Trust.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.05 Distribution of Sale Proceeds — Reconciliation
- **What:** Written reconciliation of how sale proceeds were distributed, including: costs of sale, satisfaction of OKOA's debt, payment to junior lienholders (if any proceeds remain), and excess proceeds held or distributed.
- **Why it matters:** Proper distribution of proceeds is required by statute. Improper distribution creates liability.
- **Typical form:** Trustee's distribution statement or closing statement.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.06 Excess Proceeds Protocol
- **What:** Written documentation of the amount of excess proceeds (if sale price exceeds the credit bid / all senior claims), the statutory procedure for claiming excess proceeds, and OKOA's process for notifying the borrower and junior lienholders.
- **Why it matters:** Failure to properly handle excess proceeds is a statutory violation in most states and creates liability to the borrower and juniors.
- **Typical form:** Trustee excess proceeds notice; interpleader deposit confirmation; state-specific surplus funds form.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.07 1099-A / 1099-C — Tax Reporting
- **What:** IRS Form 1099-A (Acquisition or Abandonment of Secured Property) and/or 1099-C (Cancellation of Debt) to be issued to the borrower after the foreclosure sale.
- **Why it matters:** OKOA has statutory tax reporting obligations. Failure to issue required 1099 forms creates IRS penalties.
- **Typical form:** IRS Form 1099-A; IRS Form 1099-C.
- **Where to find it:** Servicer; lender tax department.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

### 14.08 Post-Sale Title Insurance Binder (For Buyer, If OKOA Facilitates)
- **What:** A title insurance commitment issued to the successful bidder post-sale, insuring the title conveyed by the trustee's deed.
- **Why it matters:** Many institutional buyers require title insurance post-foreclosure. Having a binder available accelerates the buyer's due diligence and reduces barriers to competitive bidding.
- **Typical form:** ALTA Title Commitment.
- **Where to find it:** Title company.
- **Severity if absent:** low

### 14.09 Surplus Proceeds Notice to Borrower and Junior Lienholders
- **What:** Written notice to the borrower and any junior lienholders of record that excess proceeds are available, the amount, and the procedure and deadline for claiming them.
- **Why it matters:** Required by statute in most states. Failure to notify can result in the claimant having extended rights to claim and OKOA having liability for wrongful retention.
- **Typical form:** Trustee notice letter; state-specific surplus funds form.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.10 Trustee's Final Accounting / Sale Report
- **What:** A formal accounting prepared by the trustee after the sale summarizing all receipts (deposit, balance of purchase price), all disbursements (costs of sale, payoff of lender's debt, junior lien payments, surplus), and the closing balance confirming zero funds held.
- **Why it matters:** The final accounting is the trustee's discharge document. It demonstrates that all proceeds were applied correctly and provides the basis for closing the trustee's file. Some states require the trustee to file a formal accounting with a court or government body.
- **Typical form:** Trustee closing statement; trustee's final report.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 14.11 1099-S Reporting Requirement Check
- **What:** Analysis of whether the foreclosure sale triggers a 1099-S reporting obligation (proceeds from real estate transactions), and if so, confirmation that the trustee or closing agent has filed or will file IRS Form 1099-S with the required information on the seller (borrower) and the gross proceeds.
- **Why it matters:** The 1099-S requirement applies to foreclosure sales in most circumstances. The trustee or settlement agent who fails to file is subject to IRS penalties of up to $290 per return. Confirming compliance at the data room stage ensures the obligation is captured before the sale closes.
- **Typical form:** IRS Form 1099-S; trustee 1099-S compliance certification.
- **Where to find it:** Trustee; OKOA tax department.
- **Severity if absent:** medium

## Category 15 — State-Specific Compliance Memos

### 15.01 Jurisdiction Identification Memo
- **What:** A one-page memo identifying the state of foreclosure, confirming whether the foreclosure is proceeding as non-judicial (trustee sale) or judicial, and citing the primary governing statute.
- **Why it matters:** All procedural requirements flow from the correct identification of the governing statute. An error here cascades through the entire checklist.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.02 Utah-Specific Compliance Memo (If Utah)
- **What:** Confirmation of compliance with Utah Code Title 57, Chapter 1 (Trust Deed Act), including: NOD recording, 3-month cure period, NOS recording with minimum 20-day advance, publication for 3 consecutive weeks, and trustee qualification requirements.
- **Why it matters:** Utah has specific trustee qualification requirements and precise timing rules. Noncompliance voids the sale.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo with statutory citation checklist.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.03 California-Specific Compliance Memo (If California)
- **What:** Confirmation of compliance with California Civil Code §§2924–2924m, including: NOD with 90-day cure period (residential: additional HBOR requirements), NOS with minimum 20-day advance and 3-week publication, mailing requirements, and trustee qualification.
- **Why it matters:** California has the most extensive non-judicial foreclosure notice requirements in the country. Defects routinely result in sale postponement or invalidation.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo with statutory citation checklist.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.04 Nevada-Specific Compliance Memo (If Nevada)
- **What:** Confirmation of compliance with Nevada Revised Statutes Chapter 107, including: HOA super-priority lien analysis (NRS 116.3116), mediation requirements (residential), NOS publication, and trustee qualification.
- **Why it matters:** Nevada's HOA super-priority statute has generated significant litigation. OKOA must confirm HOA lien status before sale.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.05 Texas-Specific Compliance Memo (If Texas)
- **What:** Confirmation of compliance with Texas Property Code §§51.001–51.016, including: acceleration requirements, written notice to borrower at least 20 days before notice of sale, first Tuesday of month sale requirement, and posting/filing of notice.
- **Why it matters:** Texas has strict timing requirements (first Tuesday of the month) and specific mailing requirements. Noncompliance is a basis to void the sale.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.06 Florida-Specific Compliance Memo (If Florida)
- **What:** Confirmation of compliance with Florida Statute §702 (judicial foreclosure), including: complaint filing, lis pendens, service on all defendants, final judgment, publication, and 10-day post-sale confirmation period.
- **Why it matters:** Florida is strictly judicial. All sales require court supervision, and the confirmation process creates a post-sale window for challenges.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.07 New York-Specific Compliance Memo (If New York)
- **What:** Confirmation of compliance with New York Real Property Actions and Proceedings Law (RPAPL) §§1301–1391, including: complaint, lis pendens, referee appointment, referee's report, judgment of foreclosure and sale, and mandatory settlement conference (residential).
- **Why it matters:** New York judicial foreclosure is among the most procedurally complex in the nation, with mandatory settlement conferences for residential properties and strict defendant service requirements.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.08 Illinois-Specific Compliance Memo (If Illinois)
- **What:** Confirmation of compliance with the Illinois Mortgage Foreclosure Law (735 ILCS 5/Art. XV), including: complaint, lis pendens, service, judgment, sale, and confirmation hearing with 30-day post-sale redemption analysis.
- **Why it matters:** Illinois requires a judicial confirmation hearing post-sale, during which the court reviews sale conduct. Procedural defects discovered at confirmation can set the sale aside.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.09 Other State Compliance Memo — [State TBD]
- **What:** For any state not covered by 15.02–15.08, a bespoke compliance memo prepared by local foreclosure counsel confirming adherence to the governing state statute.
- **Why it matters:** Every state has unique requirements. A generic checklist without state-specific confirmation is insufficient for defensible sale compliance.
- **Typical form:** Counsel memo with statutory citation checklist.
- **Where to find it:** Local foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** critical

### 15.10 Residential vs. Commercial Property Classification Memo
- **What:** Written confirmation of whether the property is classified as residential or commercial for purposes of applicable foreclosure protections (California HBOR, New York settlement conference requirement, PTFA, anti-deficiency statutes).
- **Why it matters:** Residential properties are subject to additional procedural protections that do not apply to commercial properties. Misclassification in either direction creates procedural exposure.
- **Typical form:** Counsel or servicer memo.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel; servicer.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.11 State-Specific Anti-Deficiency Statute Analysis
- **What:** A state-specific memo analyzing whether and to what extent the applicable anti-deficiency statute limits OKOA's right to pursue a deficiency judgment after the foreclosure sale, including any purchase-money mortgage carve-outs, fair-value election requirements, and action-within-90-days limitations (e.g., California CCP §580d, Arizona ARS §33-814, Nevada NRS 40.455–40.459).
- **Why it matters:** Anti-deficiency rules directly affect OKOA's loss recovery strategy and credit bid level. In California, a non-judicial sale extinguishes the deficiency entirely. In other states, a fair-value hearing limits the deficiency to the difference between the judgment and fair market value. Misunderstanding the applicable rule at the time of bid authorization can result in OKOA leaving significant recovery on the table or pursuing an unenforceable deficiency claim.
- **Typical form:** Foreclosure counsel memo with statutory and case-law citations; deficiency exposure summary.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high

### 15.12 State-Specific Borrower Notification Requirements Timeline
- **What:** A state-specific timeline document mapping every mandatory borrower notification event from first default through post-sale surplus notice — including the triggering event, required method of delivery, statutory deadline, and consequences of a defective or missed notice.
- **Why it matters:** Many foreclosure sale invalidations trace to a single missed or defective notice. A consolidated timeline for the jurisdiction is the most reliable single-page compliance check. It also functions as an audit trail when the sale is challenged, because OKOA can demonstrate that every notice was tracked against a documented schedule.
- **Typical form:** Counsel-prepared compliance timeline; annotated statutory checklist.
- **Where to find it:** Foreclosure counsel.
- **Severity if absent:** high
