## Domain availability is not trademark clearance, and none of this is legal advice

This is a pre-screen. Availability was checked against domain registries only. A name can have a free domain and still infringe an existing trademark, and it can have a taken domain and still be legally available to you in your industry.

Before committing money to a name, search the official registers yourself, and consider a professional knockout search. Rough market costs: an attorney knockout search runs about **$150–$500**; a full US clearance search runs about **$1,000–$3,000**.

Search these by hand:
- United States trademarks — https://tmsearch.uspto.gov
- European Union and national registers — https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/
- Worldwide brand database — https://branddb.wipo.int *(open in a browser only — automated querying of this database is prohibited by its terms of use)*
- Your state or national business register, for company-name collisions

Also search the name plus your industry on the open web. In the United States, trademark rights can arise from **use** rather than registration, so an unregistered competitor already using the name may still have rights that a register search will not show.

## Method notes and evidence flags

- **Two rounds.** Round 1 (530 names, common Greek seeds) returned only **77** available — a **14.5%** hit rate. Common tech-adjacent seeds (cosmos, delta, sigma, syntax, system, metric, prism, planet, magnet, anchor) returned **0 available across every mutation**. Round 2 (438 names) switched to rarer seeds (gypsum, hymnal, tympan, gnosis, cystic, larynx) and endings in c/m/l/r/t, and returned **217** available — about **50%**. That difference is the reusable finding from this run.
- **Ending letters matter for availability**, not just sound: `-x` came back available 8% of the time, `-n` 4%, `-s` 5%, versus `-m` 36% and `-c` 26%.
- **The sound rules carry different evidence weight** (naming-craft Part 4): vowel congruence is well replicated; the hard/soft sound mapping holds at 72% across 25 languages and fails in a few; the phoneme-frequency table is one English-market correlational study whose word-initial pattern *reverses* for non-English-origin names, so it is a bonus signal and never a gate; the two-syllable rule is a soft default in general — it is a hard requirement here only because you asked for it.
- **Cross-language screening was not run by a native-speaker panel.** Any flag here is for human confirmation, not clearance.
- **RELEVANCE was scored against a developer / AI-terminal product**, since that is what Word-gen has been naming. Many names score 0 there — that is expected for a coinage batch and is not a defect; it means "needs a sentence of backstory", not "bad name".

**Re-verify the winner at your registrar's checkout before buying.** Availability is a snapshot; re-check with `bash ~/.claude/skills/acos-domain-finder/scripts/run check-domains --names <name> --tlds com`.
